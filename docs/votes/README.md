# Voting and agendas

October 23, 2026. Fifteen puzzles and a playground built from Week 87 of the Bellingham math circle (voting and agendas). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 87 worksheets are unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-87.md) proposed this port (fit B, size M) and shaped it: fixed cards on screen and a knockout the child builds by tapping letters, Can't certified by a letter's two pair wins or by the top cycle, blank cards filled to a goal, and reverse goals on the line (move the fewest favourites, or add a voter) instead of predict-the-winner or drag-until-green.

## The mathematics

Each voter has a card that ranks the letters, favourite at the top. In a **pair vote** each voter picks whichever of the two letters is higher on their card, and more votes wins; every puzzle has an odd number of voters, so a pair vote never ties. A **knockout** (a successive-elimination agenda) starts with a first pair; the winner stays in and meets the next letter, and the last winner wins.

- **Three letters: a top letter or a cycle.** With three letters the three pair results either have a letter that beats both others (a Condorcet winner) or go round: A beats B, B beats C and C beats A (Condorcet's paradox), though every card is a sensible ranking. A letter that beats both others wins every knockout: if it plays first it wins both its votes, and if it waits it beats whoever won the first pair. In a cycle the letter that waits always wins, because it beats the winner of the other two (puzzle 1, Week 87 shared Problems 1–2).
- **Which letters can win some knockout: the top cycle.** Call a group of letters *closed* when every letter in it beats every letter outside it. Once any letter of a closed group plays, every later winner is in the group: a member keeps beating outsiders, and a member that loses, loses to another member. So a closed group that leaves a letter out proves that letter can never win; this is the Can't certificate (puzzles 2, 5, 10 and 12). Closed groups are nested, and the smallest is the **top cycle**. Conversely every letter in the top cycle wins some knockout: inside the top cycle every letter reaches every other by a chain of pair wins (otherwise the letters a chain can't reach would form a smaller closed group), so by Camion's theorem the top cycle has a cycle through all its letters, and following it backwards to the target, with the outsiders played straight after the first letter, gives a knockout the target wins. The validator checks the equivalence for every letter of every puzzle by trying every order.
- **Writing cards to a goal.** A letter wins every knockout exactly when it beats each other letter. With three cards and three letters, the waiting letter wins every knockout exactly when the cards are A, B, C / B, C, A / C, A, B or the mirror A, C, B / C, B, A / B, A, C, in some order: each card agrees with at most two of a cycle's three wins, each win needs two cards, so every card agrees with exactly two (12 of the 216 ways to write three cards; shared Problem 3). If most cards already put some letter above the goal letter, that letter beats it whatever the blank cards say; those cards are the Can't certificate on the cards (puzzle 7).
- **On a line the middle favourite wins.** On a line from 0 to 10 each voter has a favourite spot and picks the nearer of two spots (an equal distance is no vote). A spot wins when it beats every other spot. With an odd number of voters the middle favourite (the median, counting repeats) wins: against a spot to its left, it and the voters to its right all pick it, and the same on the other side (Black's median voter theorem). It is the median, not the middle of the line or the average (puzzle 8). So the fewest voters to move so that a spot g wins is how many it lacks on its short side: max(0, 3 − #{at or right of g}, 3 − #{at or left of g}) for five voters, and g must end up as some voter's favourite. With four voters there is a winner only when the two middle favourites share a spot; at 1, 3, 7 and 9 every spot from 3 to 7 never loses but none wins, and a fifth voter at x makes the winner 3, x or 7, whichever is the middle (puzzles 9 and 15). Most voters strictly on one side of g all pick the spot next to g on their side over g, so they prove g can't win.

Experiments come first: a child taps letters into a knockout and watches the votes, or moves favourites and presses Vote. Conjectures follow ("the waiting letter wins", "work backwards from the crown", "the middle one wins"), and explanations are for older children with a grown-up. Every certificate is something a child taps and the board checks: a group of letters, some cards, some voters. The assumptions are explicit: strict rankings, an odd number of voters, sincere voting, a knockout with every letter, and whole-number spots on the line. Nothing here claims anything about strategic voting, ties, other agendas or two-dimensional preferences.

### Why its own family

Week 87 is ported as its own family. Its object (voters' rankings and pair votes) and its move (choosing the order of a knockout) belong to no family in the app. [Pairs that stay](../matching/README.md) also shows preference strips, but its move pairs people up and its question is stability; [Fair shares](../shares/README.md) divides goods by values. The voting cards borrow their look: a column per voter, favourite at the top, as in the matching strips.

## How a puzzle plays

- **Knockouts (1, 2, 5, 6, 10–12).** The voters' cards are on top, then the knockout mat, then a round letter button for each letter. Tap letters in the order they join: the first two vote, and each next letter meets the winner so far. Each vote is a row on the mat, survivor on the left: each side shows its letter, a dot per vote and the count, and the winner's side is pine. The cards light up the last vote: each card's picked letter in pine, the other letter dashed, the rest faded. The final slot on the right has a crown with the goal letter faint inside it, which is the only statement of the goal on the board; when the knockout ends the winner fills the slot, and a wrong winner leaves "C wins." Played letters turn to dashed outlines. Undo takes back a letter.
- **Can't on a knockout.** Can't is always there, also when the letter can win, so its presence tells nothing. Press it, tap letters to ring a group (the letter buttons become toggles), then press **Check**. A group with the goal letter is refused with "Leave D out."; a group with a letter outside it that beats one inside is refused with that pair, "E beats D."; either way the taps clear, and Check stays off for that group for good (through Undo too). An accepted group solves the puzzle and shows each of its pair wins with the votes, "F beats D, 2 to 1".
- **Cards (3, 7, 13).** One objective line names the goal, because the board can't show "every knockout". Printed cards are solid; blank cards are dashed with empty spaces. Tap a letter to write it in the next space of the active blank card (pine outline), top first; tap a card's next space to write on that card, and tap a written letter to take it off (the letters below move up). **Vote** is on when every card is full: it plays every knockout on the cards. If one fails, the line names it, "B wins when B waits.", and Vote stays off until a letter changes; this line is not a lock, since the child can check it. A pass shows the table of all three knockouts, each with its crowned winner. Puzzles 3 and 7 have **Can't**: tap cards and press Check. "A blank card can go either way.", "That isn't most of the cards." and "No letter is above C on all of them." refuse, as on the knockouts; an accepted set rings the letter above the goal on those cards and says "A beats B on most cards."
- **The line (4, 8, 14).** One objective line names the allowance ("Move one voter so that 7 wins."), which the dots alone didn't say. Spots 0 to 10 with the voters' tokens stacked over their favourites; the goal spot has a crown under its number. Tap a voter (pine ring), then anywhere in a spot's column (the columns tint while a voter is picked; its own column lets it go), or drag the voter there: the drop counts for the nearest column, also a little beside, above or below the line, and on a touch screen the voter rides 34 px above the finger while the column it will land in lights. Voters take taps a few pixels beyond their discs (44 px on a tablet). On a phone the line runs to the panel's edges, and its eleven columns, about 31 px wide, cap a voter's tap area at about 30 by 41 px: eleven 40 px columns would need a 470 px screen, and a line drawn down the screen would hide the left-to-right order the puzzles are about, so picking up stays a tap on the voter or a drag with the voter lifted above the finger, and putting down is a tap anywhere in the column. Arrow keys move a picked voter one spot. A moved voter turns green and leaves a dashed mark where it started; moving it back there un-moves it. The dots by Vote count the voters moved against the allowance, and a voter past it adds a red dot. **Vote** finds the spot that beats every other spot: the goal within the dots solves it; another winner rings that spot and says "4 wins."; the goal with too many moved says "Fewer moves would do." After a Vote, Vote stays off until a voter moves. Nothing shows the winner while the child moves voters.
- **Adding a voter (9, 15).** V5, in sun yellow, waits above the line; tap a spot's column to put it down (later, tap it and a spot to move it). The four voters stay put. Vote judges as above. **Can't** works as on the cards with voters: "That isn't most of the voters." or "They aren't all on one side of 8." refuse; V1, V2 and V3 at 1, 3 and 7 prove 8 can't win, "V1, V2 and V3 all pick 7 over 8."
- **Hints.** Before any move the first hint is the authored nudge. Then hints say the next step: "Start with B.", "Put C against B.", "Bring in A next.", "Write A at the top of V3.", "Move V3 to 7.", "Move V1 back to 0.", "Put V5 on 5.", "Press Vote.". On a Can't puzzle the hint gives the reason and the claim, "F beats D and E, so once F plays it keeps winning. Press Can't and ring F.". Level 2 marks the letter, card, voter, spot or button; level 3 offers Apply hint. When the goal can't be reached from the board (the wrong letter came in, or the cards written can't be finished), the dead end is told only behind Hint, with Undo back. Hints alone solve every puzzle from fresh and from random play.

## The playground

The playground has no goal, no Hint and never counts as solved. Choose 3 or 5 voters; every card starts blank. Write the cards with the letter buttons (tap a card's next space to choose the card). When every card is full the letter buttons run a knockout on the mat, with the cards lighting each vote; **Again** clears it, and changing a card clears it too. It holds shared Problem 3's design task: write cards with one winner, then cards where the waiting letter wins.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- Only legal moves are accepted: a letter plays once and only in a knockout; only blank cards are written, a letter once a card and at most three; Vote only on full cards (or with every voter down) and not again on a refused board; Can't only where the puzzle has it and only with a group, card set or voter set not refused before; nothing after a solve.
- Saves hold only what was done: the letters played, the letters written on each blank card, the voters' spots, the refused certificates, the refusal line and an accepted certificate. Every refused certificate in a save must be one the app would refuse, a refusal line needs its refused certificate and its true reason, an accepted certificate must be sound, and a solved flag needs the goal met.
- **Undo takes back moves, not claims.** Every refused certificate stays refused through the `carry` hook, and its line stays while the letters, cards or voters are where they were, so one Undo right after a refusal also takes back the move before it. A refused Vote is not a lock (it names a winner the child could find), so Undo takes it back. The picked voter, the ringed taps and the active card are view state: the pick belongs to the board it was made on, and a refusal clears the taps once, when it first shows.

The top-cycle characterisation of knockout winners and its proof by Camion's theorem are standard results stated here with their proofs; the app checks them by exhaustive search on every puzzle rather than relying on a source.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Things to watch:
  1. whether children read the lit cards (each voter's pick in pine) as the reason a vote went one way, or only watch the counts on the mat; and whether the faint crowned letter in the final slot reads as the goal without any words;
  2. whether Can't with a ringed group is understood on the knockouts (it asks for a group of letters that each beat every letter outside it), or whether children ring letters at random until Check accepts one; each refusal names a true pair, so watch whether children use those lines or ignore them;
  3. on the line, whether children move voters on purpose toward the crowned spot and count the dots, or press Vote after every move to see who wins (after a Vote, Vote is off until a voter moves, but nothing stops a move-and-vote loop); and whether picking up and putting down voters works on a phone, where each column is about 31 px wide, by tapping a column or by dragging with the voter lifted above the finger.
- **Not built from the card.** Upper Problem 2 (the cyclic cards can't come from distances on one line: the middle letter is never a voter's strict last choice) is a claim about every arrangement and stays on paper; the card's live "drag three letters and favourites and the cards update" impossibility screen was not built. Line Problem 3's explanation (why the middle favourite always wins) is in the grown-up notes, not a puzzle.
- **More letters.** Puzzles 6, 11 and 12 use four and five letters; the worksheets stop at three. They are new, unpiloted, and may be too long for some children; the hints work backwards from the crown.
- **Story and grade trails.** Voting and agendas is off the Lantern Road and has no keeper lines; the family is grade-free and in no K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
