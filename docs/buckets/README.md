# Pebble Duel: Buckets

October 17, 2026. Eight puzzles built from Week 81 of the Bellingham math circle (Buckets of Fish), added to Pebble Duel as one group, **Buckets**, as the Week 81 review card's Decision asks (`plans/review/week-81.md` in the worksheets repository, October 17, 2026): the capped game of Problems 1–3 and the guide's take-1-to-3 encore. Problems 4–5 (the longest game, and whether a game can go on forever) stay on paper. None of this has been played by children in the app; the Week 81 packet is unpiloted too.

## The mathematics

Buckets stand in a row, each holding some fish. A move takes one fish from a bucket and may add one fish to each bucket to its left: to all of them, some or none. Whoever takes the last fish wins.

- **Every play ends,** although the number of fish can grow: the rightmost bucket never gains a fish, so it empties for good after finitely many moves, and then the bucket to its left can only lose fish, and so on (Hamkins). With adds of at most K, the longest game from a_1, …, a_n (bucket 1 on the left) has a_1 + a_2 (K + 1) + … + a_n (K + 1)^(n−1) moves: take one fish and add the most to every bucket on its left, every time. From 1, 1 and 1 with adds of 1 that is 7.
- **The second player wins exactly when every bucket is even.** A move from an all-even position changes the bucket it takes from by one, so it leaves an odd bucket. From any other position, take a fish from the rightmost odd bucket and add one to each odd bucket on its left (none to the even ones): every bucket is even again. That repair is the only winning move, since every bucket left of it must change by exactly its parity and every bucket right of it cannot change. So a player who hands over all-even positions takes the last fish. The rule does not depend on the cap: it holds with adds of up to 3, or with no limit, though then the winning move is no longer unique.
- **The encore: multiples of 4.** With takes of 1, 2 or 3 and adds of up to 3, the second player wins exactly when every bucket is a multiple of 4, by the same argument with 4 in place of 2: a move changes the bucket it takes from by 1 to 3, and from any other position the unique repair takes what is over the last multiple of 4 from the rightmost bucket that is not one, and tops up each bucket on its left to the next multiple. The adds of 3 matter: with adds of at most 1 or 2 the rule fails at once, since 1 and 1 is then a second-player start.

What the app checks: in a duel, that the child wins against a perfect opponent; in every reply, that the child, going second, wins against each of my first moves; in a who-starts round, that the child chooses who starts and then wins, three games in a row from new starts; in Every start, that the marked cards are exactly the second-player starts. Why the rule holds stays with grown-ups, in the notes.

What a child is meant to discover: that adding fish is a choice, and sometimes the winning one is none (0, 0 and 1) or two buckets (3, 3 and 1); which two-bucket starts to hand over (2 and 0, 0 and 2, 2 and 2); that every bucket even is the thing to leave me, and how to restore it after any move; and in the encore, that the target changes to fours.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Buckets drawn as open paper jars with an ink rim, numbered underneath, fish inside in rows of two (four in the encore). The board is at most 380 px wide. On the child's turn a bucket holds at most 12 fish on three buckets and 16 in the encore, whatever the child does (the validator searches every game; 16 comes from refilling fully from 5 and 5 twice). With 16, End turn ends about 616 px down on a 375 × 667 phone, and the smoke checks it is in view.
- **A move.** On the child's turn every bucket with fish is a button. A tap takes a fish from it: the fish lifts (sun) and a dashed place for one fish appears in each bucket to its left. A tap on a bucket on the left adds a fish there (pine); a second tap takes it out again. In the encore a second and third tap on the taken bucket take more, up to 3, and taps on a bucket on the left add up to 3, one more tap putting them back. A tap on the bucket taken from, once it can take no more, puts the fish back and drops the move. **End turn** makes the move; **Cancel** drops it. A tap on a bucket with fish right of the one taken from starts a new take there instead, dropping the move begun, with no message (before October 23 it said "That move is not allowed. Check How to play.", for what was almost always a change of mind). An empty bucket right of the take, like any empty bucket before a move is begun, is not a button, and a tap on it says the move is not allowed. So the controls only ever offer moves the rules allow: refills only on the left, at most one each (or three). The move being built is view-only, outside the save, until End turn.
- **My reply** comes at once, with one line saying what I did ("I took 1 from bucket 3 and added 1 to buckets 1 and 2. Your turn"); the fish I took shows dashed and the fish I added carry an ochre ring, until the child starts a move.
- **Duels** (1, 2, 5, 7). You go first. **Undo** takes back your move and my reply together, as one move. After a losing move Hint says, only when pressed, that I can now always win, and offers the rescue Undo. When the game ends my way, the line above the buckets says "I took the last fish."; Hint, only when pressed, says "Undo or restart to try again."
- **Every reply** (3). You go second from 2 and 2; I make my first moves in turn. Pips show the first moves answered, and the save keeps each won game. After a win, **Next first move**; after a loss, **Try again** replays the same first move. No Undo.
- **Who-starts rounds** (6, 8). Before each game, **Go first** or **Go second**, styled alike so neither looks like the default; the choice is final. (Until October 23 they were Me first and You first, the child's voice beside lines where the app speaks as "I"; Stalks in Pebble Duel says Go second too.) Three pips fill with wins in a row; a loss empties them. After a loss, **Same start again** replays that start as practice that does not count ("Wins count from a new start.", shown before the choice and again after a win); **New start** draws a new one, half the time from the second-player starts and never the start just played. No start one move from the end is drawn: with adds of 1, those are the three single-fish starts, each a free win for Go first. No Undo.
- **Every start** (4). Fifteen cards, each two small buckets of fish in rows of two. The fish grow with the cards' grid (a container query): 15 px wide on a 390 px phone and 14 px on a 375 px one, where the grid reaches 8 px into the panel's padding, and 26 px on a tablet or desktop, where the grid may be 540 px wide (until October 23 they were 11 px on a phone and 13 px elsewhere). The worksheet's four-by-four layout is kept, so 16 px on a phone is out of reach. Tap a card to mark it (pine) and again to clear it. **Play**, then a card, then Go first or Go second, plays a practice game from it against me; Back returns to the marks, untouched. **That's all** judges every mark at once. While a second-player start is missing, it says "There is another." and stays off until the marks change, whatever else is marked. Once none is missing, it is final: if a marked card is one where whoever starts can win, I play it at once. The line above the board begins "Whoever starts from 1 and 1 can win. I go first.", and I go first and win against any reply (no Back until the game ends). Then the marks are cleared, and the line keeps why ("Whoever starts from 1 and 1 can win. I took the last fish. The marks are cleared."), which matters when the wrong card is one move from the end (1 and 0, or 0 and 1) and the play-out is over at once. Back returns to an empty set of cards. No count is ever shown.
- **Hints.** In a duel or every reply, Hint before any move shows the puzzle's first authored hint; after a move, "Some move wins from here. Which buckets would you like to leave me?". From the second press the bucket a winning move takes from is ringed in ochre, and Apply hint makes the move on the third. Its second and third authored hints are for grown-ups: the notes list them. In a who-starts round Hint shows the three authored nudges by level and never says who should start (after a win, "Try a new start."), as in Star race. In Every start Hint names the next step: take off a wrong mark, then add a missing card, then That's all; during a play-out, any move.

## Certificates and claims

- **Duels and every reply.** A win against a perfect opponent from the given start. The opponent never misses a winning move, so from a first-player start the child must find the one winning move whenever it is the child's turn, and from 2 and 2 must answer each first move.
- **The opponent when it is losing** leaves the fewest winning replies, then the longest game, as the card asks, rather than moving at random. Here every move from a losing position leaves exactly one winning reply (the repair is unique), so the first rule always ties and the longest game decides: take one fish and add the most to every bucket on its left. Every such move shortens the longest game by exactly one, whichever bucket it takes from, so I take from a bucket chosen at random. Children therefore meet refills: I never make the game shorter than I must. With perfect play the longest game takes the child 11 moves (from 3, 3 and 3; 5 moves from 5 and 5 in the encore).
- **Who-starts rounds.** Three wins in a row against perfect play from new starts, as in Star race. Starts are drawn half from the second-player starts, so always choosing Go first passes about 1 time in 8 (the validator measures 13.5% on puzzle 6 and 12.5% on puzzle 8). The save keeps each won game of the streak (start, who went first, every turn), and validation replays each against the perfect opponent, so a full streak cannot be saved without three real wins. Like Star race, hints alone do not finish a round: the decision is who starts, and a hint that said so would make the wins certify nothing. The validator plays the rounds with a solver.
- **Every start.** A find-every claim judged as a whole, as port-common asks, in Star race's order: a missing start first ("There is another.", locked until a change), then a wrong mark, played out with me first and the marks cleared, then solved. With every answer marked and nothing wrong it is solved. The validator plays the guessing strategies against the mechanic and counts taps (a mark, That's all, Back and End turn are one each; a move is one tap a fish taken or added, plus End turn):

  | Knowing the answer | Playing every card second, perfectly | Mark everything, strip the wrong ones | Leave one out at a time | Test groups by halves |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 4 | 110 | 174 (12 games lost) | 157 (11 lost) | 78 (4 lost) |

  "There is another." is free, so it answers a yes-or-no question about a group: leave a group unmarked and mark every other card not known to be wrong, and "There is another." says the group holds an answer, while a play-out says it holds none (and costs a lost game and the marks). Halving groups this way finds the three answers in 78 taps and 4 lost games, less than playing every card (110) though about 20 times knowing the answer, and it takes planning a child is unlikely to bring. Star race's Every start allows the same. Locking That's all after "There is another." until a practice game has been played would close it; that is not done.

  Random marks almost never pass: marking each card with chance ½ never passed in 20,000 tries, and with chance 3/15 passed 12 times.
- **Nothing gives the answer away.** Titles name the start or the cards, How to play states the move and the taps, and first hints point at play. No title, How to play line, rule, objective or first hint says even, odd, pair, multiple or four (the validator checks each). The grown-ups' notes, which hold the answers, are left out of How to play (`"proof": true`, as in Star race); the Grown-ups catalog still lists them. Later hints may name more: a round's third hint asks whether the fish I left can be paired up.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- Only moves the rules allow are accepted: a take of 1 (1 to 3 in the encore) from a bucket that has fish, adds of 0 or 1 (0 to 3) only to buckets on its left, on the child's turn; a choice of who starts only before a game, and only once; Same start again only after a loss; Next first move only after a win and Try again only after a loss; nothing after a solve. In Every start, cards 1 to 15; That's all only with something marked and no standing "There is another."; Back from a play-out only after it ends; in a practice game, a card before the choice.
- Saves are replayed from their start: turns alternate, every move is legal, every one of my moves from a winning position wins, and a game lasts no longer than the longest game from its start. Saves land before a choice, on the child's turn, or after a game. A duel starts from its start with you first; every reply's game begins with the first move of its round, and the save holds a real win against each earlier first move. A round's streak equals its list of won games, each a real win from a start in the pool and new after the one before; a loss empties it; practice games never add to it; a full streak ends on a fresh win. An Every start save keeps the marks sorted and distinct; "There is another." must be what That's all says about them; a play-out must start from the first-player card That's all named about the marks it holds, and once it ends the marks must be cleared; done only when they are right.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Things to watch first:
  1. Whether children find the move gesture (tap to take, tap on the left to add, End turn), and whether they add fish by default because the rule allows it, as 0, 0 and 1 tests.
  2. Whether the fish laid out in rows of two (four in the encore) make the rule visible before it is found. The worksheet's guide calls pairing the counters what makes the target visible; in the app it is there from the first game. A plain heap would hide it.
  3. Whether children choose who starts by looking at the buckets, or fall back on Go first, and whether a lost Every start play-out teaches or only discourages.
- **Problems 4 and 5** (the longest game from a refill of 10, 30 or 100, and whether a game can go on forever) stay on paper, as the Decision says: the first comes down to one number, the second is a claim about every play that no finite board checks.
- **The uncapped game** (Hamkins's original, any number of fish added) is not built: its positions are unbounded, so it would need fish shown as numbers.
- **A streak's starts are not proved random.** A hand-made save could hold three real wins from easy starts; only the wins themselves are checked.
- **K–1.** The card notes that the worksheet has no K–1 route. The group is grade-free; its prerequisites say a grown-up can read how a move works.
- **Story.** The group is not on the Lantern Road.
- **Below the board on a phone** (shared layout): on 375 × 667 the Undo, Restart and Hint row sits just below the fold in Every start and in the encore rounds, and "That move is not allowed" (a tap on an empty bucket) appears under that row. The move controls are always in view.
