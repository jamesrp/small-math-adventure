# Strands

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 75 of the Bellingham math circle (twists on a cylinder). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 75 worksheet is unpiloted.

The family runs on the shared [portal board](../portal-board.md): its cylinder is a portal-board surface glued left to right only, with real rims at the top and bottom, and both views (the room and the unrolled view) are the board's. Portal rooms ([its notes](../portals/README.md)) is the model family. Puzzles 1–5 and 7–9 are the worksheet's Problem 6 and 7 pairs, puzzles 6, 11 and 12 come from the review card's App fit, and puzzle 10 is new; each puzzle's `provenance` says where it comes from.

## The mathematics

**The object.** A strip whose left and right edges are glued is a cylinder, an annulus (ℝ/ℤ) × [0, 1] with two rims. A **strand** is a simple arc from a fixed point A on the bottom rim to a fixed point B on the top rim, inside the cylinder between them. Cut the cylinder along the seam and lay copies side by side: this **unrolled view** is the universal cover, a strip ℝ × [0, 1]. A strand lifts to a path from the A of the first copy to the B of some copy w; w is its **winding**, the signed number of times it goes round.

**Facts the puzzles rest on.** Each holds for strands with both ends fixed, moved by sliding on the cylinder (isotopy fixing the ends), never lifted off it.

1. **Winding classifies.** Sliding a strand with its ends fixed never changes its winding, and two strands with the same winding slide into each other. The full twist of the cylinder (turn the top rim once round, dragging everything with it) fixes both rims and adds 1 to every winding.
2. **The fewest crossings.** Two strands with windings a and b share A and B. Away from those ends they cross at least max(|a − b| − 1, 0) times, and two straight strands cross exactly that often. *Lower bound:* in the unrolled view, a copy of the first strand that starts k copies right of A ends at the B of copy a + k. For 0 < k < b − a (or b − a < k < 0) the second strand starts on one side of that copy and ends on the other, so it crosses it; crossings with different copies are different points of the cylinder. *Upper bound:* straight lifts meet a copy only where their horizontal gap is a whole number of turns, |a − b| − 1 times strictly inside. A common twist changes neither strand's crossings, so only a − b matters.
3. **Three strands.** Pull three strands tight: each pair crosses max(gap − 1, 0) times, where the gap is the distance between their Bs in copies. On a line the outer gap of three ends is the sum of the two inner gaps. Pairwise targets 1, 1, 3 need gaps 2, 2, 4: windings 0, 2, 4 (the card's answer) or any shift. Targets 1, 1, 1 need gaps 2, 2, 2, and 2 + 2 ≠ 2: impossible, and the strand whose end is in the middle is the certificate. (The brief's "0, 2, 4 possible" reads the card's windings as targets; targets 0, 2, 4 are impossible, since the gaps would be at most 1, 3 and 5, and 5 is not 3 plus at most 1.)

**The model in the app.** The cylinder is four lattice columns round and eight rows up; A and B sit half way round from the seam. A strand is a **bead** on each of the seven rows between the rims, joined by straight pieces, so every strand goes upward. An upward strand never crosses itself: at each height it has one point. This loses nothing, since every winding has an upward strand and the straight strands that attain each minimum are upward. Beads of two strands never share a spot on a row, so two strands meet only between rows, where they cross; A and B are not counted. Crossings are then the whole turns strictly between consecutive gaps (blue's column minus red's, row by row). Beads stay inside the copies the unrolled view shows: the first copy, both Bs, every copy blue must cross from end to end, and a copy more on each side, at most six across. **The lattice is fine enough:** for every puzzle, the validator finds the exact minimum over every placement of both strands' beads in the view and it equals max(|a − b| − 1, 0). (The review card warns that a coarse grid raises minima: a 4 × 3 grid gives 3 for windings 0 and 3, where the answer is 2.)

**Experiments, conjectures, explanations.** The experiments are untangling (drag beads until no crossing will go) and choosing ends. The conjectures come from them: only the difference of windings matters; the fewest is one less than the difference; once, once, once can't be done. The explanations are acts the app checks, each as one claim checked only when it is complete: Done, then a tap on each copy of red that blue must cross (the lower bound pointed at), then That's all; or, with two pairs right and the three ends at different Bs, Can't and a tap on the strand whose end is strictly in the middle (its two gaps add up to the outer gap). The proofs in words stay in the grown-up notes.

**Grade bands.** The worksheet is grades 4–5 only. The app is grade-free: puzzles 1–3 need only dragging and watching rings disappear (a strong 2–3 player could untangle them); puzzles 4–5 and 7–10 add the copies of red; puzzles 6, 11 and 12 need counting crossings for three pairs at once.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** On a wide screen the cylinder and the unrolled view sit side by side, the cylinder in its own column 190–230 px wide; on a phone the unrolled view comes first, with the cylinder beneath it. The cylinder is 230 × 397 px on a phone too, so its beads take taps 40 px across. Red is rose, blue sky and green leaf. The seam is a dashed edge in both views, the first copy is shaded sun, and every crossing is an ochre ring. In the unrolled view of an untangle puzzle, red's copies are drawn faintly beside red, and blue's crossings sit on them.
- **Untangling** (puzzles 1–5, 7–10). Slide a bead sideways along its row, in either view. In the cylinder a slide goes the short way round in the direction the finger moves, across the seam if need be; a spot held by the other strand on that row is skipped. Tab to a bead in the cylinder and use the left and right arrow keys (up and down go to the next bead). Undo takes back a move.
- **The claim: Done, the copies, That's all.** Done checks nothing: it starts the claim, "Tap each red copy that blue must cross, then press That’s all.", and the beads rest. A tap on a copy of red (or near one: a tap goes to the nearest copy) marks it and a second tap unmarks it; Tab with Enter or Space does the same. Back leaves the claim and takes Done's place in the bar, so a second tap on Done never sends a claim. That's all sends the claim, checked whole. With crossings to spare it says "There is a way with fewer." (whatever copies were tapped); at the fewest with other copies it says "Those aren’t the copies blue must cross.", or "Blue must cross some copy of red." when none were tapped and some must be. Exactly the forced copies solve the puzzle; for puzzles 1–3 that is no copies. After a refusal, Done waits for a bead to move. So Done is never a yes-or-no test for the fewest: the claim is judged only with its certificate in.
- **Design** (puzzles 6, 11, 12). Three strands from A pull themselves tight; each pair crosses the fewest times its ends allow. Each strand's end is a disc above its B in the unrolled view, side by side where ends share a B. Tap a disc (a tap goes to the nearest disc), then tap a B; or drag a disc along the rim. The three end buttons in the bar do the same with the left and right arrow keys. Chips above show each pair's number, and a chip turns pine when its pair crosses that often.
- **Can't.** Can't waits for the first moved end. Like Done it checks nothing: it starts the claim (a press of Done, Can't or the Back that takes their place within 350 ms of the last is dropped, so a quick double tap opens a claim once), "Tap the strand whose end is in the middle.", the end buttons become the three strands and Back takes Can't's place. A tap on a strand (a button, or the nearest disc) sends the claim, checked whole and refused in this order: without an attempt (two pairs on their numbers and the three ends at different Bs) "First get two pairs right, with every end at a different B."; where there is a way, "There is a way."; and a strand that isn't strictly between the other two, "Its end isn’t between the other two.". The strand strictly in the middle solves the puzzle, and the board draws its two gaps and the outer gap they add up to (in Puzzle 12 always 2 + 2 = 4). After a refusal, Can't waits for an end to move. Ends moved and back to 0, 0, 0, or two ends at one B, never certify anything.
- **Undo** never escapes a refusal: undoing to the same strands or ends keeps "There is a way with fewer.", "Those aren’t the copies blue must cross.", "Blue must cross some copy of red.", "There is a way.", "First get two pairs right, with every end at a different B." or "Its end isn’t between the other two.", and the claim still waits for a move. A kept refusal leaves the claim press nothing of its own to show, so one Undo right after a refusal also takes back the move before it, and the refusal stays with the strands or ends it was on. Any move ends a claim being made, so a board met again later never brings back a claim from before.
- **The feedback line** sits between the bar and the boards, so it is in sight on a phone (its bottom is about 400 px down on a 375 × 667 screen).
- **Copy.** One sentence: "Untangle them, then press Done." or "Each pair crosses as often as its number.". No count, budget or answer slot is on screen; the fewest is never printed. The rules and controls are in How to play, each said once: the objective there ends at "press Done" (the claim's taps are in the controls), and on design puzzles the controls say only "press Can’t and tap a strand"; the claim's own line names the middle strand when Can't is pressed.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, never a claim. After that, hints name one move in words ("Drag the fourth blue bead from the bottom two places left.", "Move the green end four copies right."). At the fewest they give the whole claim with its reason: "Every crossing left is with a copy of red that blue has to cross: the copies that start one and two copies right of A. Press Done, tap them, then That’s all." (or "No crossings are left. Press Done, then That’s all."). After a refused claim at the fewest, the hint first moves a bead one place without adding a crossing, since the claim waits for a move. In puzzle 12 the chain first builds an attempt (two pairs right, three different ends), then gives the reason and the claim: "Blue’s end is between the others: two copies from red’s and two copies from green’s. So red and green ends are four copies apart: they cross three times, never once. Press Can’t and tap blue." The second level marks the bead and its spot, the disc and its B, or Done or Can't with the copies or strand; the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle: from fresh, after a wrong move, after every kind of refused claim, and from random tangles. Hints are remembered, and each takes a few milliseconds of CPU.
- **What the app avoids.** No twist-word quiz, no predict-the-fewest question and no shown target count. Every solve is something done with the strands, and every claim is checked with its certificate supplied by the child.

## The playground

Two strands with beads, windings 0 and 1 to start. Slide beads, move either end (tap a disc and a B, drag it, or use the end buttons; the moved strand is re-laid by a twist of that strand alone), and twist the whole top rim once round either way with the twist buttons, which adds a turn to both windings and changes no crossing. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. This is the worksheet's Problems 1 and 4 without the twist-word quiz.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the strands' beads (untangle, playground), the three windings (design), plus the refusal told, whether an end has moved, the middle strand after a right Can't, whether Done solved it, and the windings (playground). Crossings, copies, chips and gaps are derived from it. A claim being made (Done or Can't pressed, copies tapped so far) belongs to the interface, not the save, and any applied move ends it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a bead to a whole column on its own row inside the view, never onto the other strand's spot on that row; an end to a B inside the view; a claim only where the puzzle has one, never while a refusal waits, with distinct whole copies (untangle) or one of the three strands (design), and Can't only after an end has moved; twists only in the playground and only inside the view; nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: the right keys and nothing else; seven beads per strand on whole columns inside the view, apart on every row; "There is a way with fewer." only above the fewest; the other untangle refusals and a solve only at the fewest ("Blue must cross some copy of red." only where some must be crossed); "There is a way." only on an attempt where there is a way; "First get two pairs right…" only off an attempt; "Its end isn’t between the other two." and a middle strand only on an attempt where there is no way, the middle strand strictly between the others; untouched ends only at the start.
- `scripts/build-strands.mjs` computes start crossings, the fewest, the fewest moves to it, the forced copies and tight witnesses with the mechanic's functions, and checks them against the table. `scripts/validate-strands.mjs` checks everything again by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things for testers to watch first:
  - whether children on a computer drag beads in the cylinder or in the unrolled view (on a touch screen only the cylinder's beads slide), and whether the unrolled view's copies of red mean anything to them before Done asks them to tap one;
  - whether Done is pressed when no crossing will go, or as a guess, and whether tapping the forced copies before That's all reads as the reason or as a chore (and whether a refusal that waits for a bead to move feels fair);
  - whether the end discs are found and hit on a phone (they are small there; the end buttons in the bar are the large targets), and whether children see that only the gaps between ends matter in puzzles 6, 11 and 12; and whether "First get two pairs right, with every end at a different B." leads children in Puzzle 12 to the attempt that shows why, rather than reading as a rule to obey.
- **Phone size.** On a 375 px phone the unrolled view is about 13 px a column, too narrow for 40 px bead targets, so on a touch screen its beads are pictures: beads slide in the cylinder, and a finger on the unrolled view of an untangle puzzle scrolls the page (a mouse still slides beads in either view; the design puzzles' end strip keeps its drag). Crossing rings and the A there are drawn larger on a phone. The playground's end strip is 33 px tall on a phone, between the bead rows; the end buttons in the bar are the large targets. Not tried on a real phone or tablet; checked by tests, the validator and browser suites at phone and desktop widths only.
- **Guessing.** A claim is judged only whole, and each refusal waits for a move and stays with its board through Undo, but a determined child can still move a bead out and back and try other copies; nothing counts tries.
- **Many beads.** A long hint move can drag a bead several turns round ("14 places") after a random tangle; the authored starts need two or three places at most.
- **The twist as a move.** The twist is only in the playground. A puzzle that asks for a twist of one strand to reach a target (the worksheet's P7 last question) is not built.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
- **Paper only.** The worksheet's yarn cylinder, seam-crossing records, twist words and written rule stay on paper.
