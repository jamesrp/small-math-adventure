# Corners

October 17, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 56 of the Bellingham math circle (corners of a solid, Euler's formula and the angular gap), in two flat groups: **Rings**, a corner builder, and **Trees**, erasing an opened drawing down to a tree on the shared graph board. They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-56.md) and its second run decide what is built; its Decision (October 10, 2026) reads: a corner builder where the child places triangle, square and pentagon corners around a point and the app folds a pointed corner, lies flat or refuses an overlap, with every closing ring found, a checked "That's all" and the angle total as the "can't" certificate; and erasing edges down to a tree (Problem 7) on Week 53's graph engine. Unfolding a turning solid stays with the real paper solids, and there is no typed V − E + F and no gap prediction. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet source. The packet has not changed since the card: its last source commit (October 6) predates the card (October 10).

## The mathematics

**Corners.** Regular polygons with the same side fit round a point side to side. A corner of a regular n-gon is 180(n − 2)/n degrees: a triangle's is 60°, a square's 90° and a pentagon's 108°. A **ring** is three or more corners in order round the point; read from another corner, or the other way round, it is the same ring. **Fold** joins the first corner's free side to the last one's.

- **When a ring folds.** Three or more corners fold into a pointed corner with no dent exactly when their angles add to less than 360°. (For a convex corner the face angles must total less than a whole turn, and each must be less than the others together; every angle here is less than two triangles, so the second condition always holds.) At exactly 360° the corners lie flat, and past it they overlap. The missing part of the turn is the corner's **gap** (its angular defect). The app's folded corner is the one whose edges lie on a round cone; the validator checks every face angle and that every face has the rest of the corner on one side.
- **How many rings.** Up to turning and flipping, 11 rings of triangles and squares fold to a point: three, four or five triangles; three squares; and seven mixes (SST, STT, SSST, SSTT, STST, STTT, STTTT). They come from 10 sets of corners, since two squares and two triangles go round in two ways, side by side or opposite. Four rings lie flat: SSSS, TTTTTT and the two orders of two squares with three triangles (SSTTT and STSTT). Pentagons add 14 pointed rings (25 in all) and no flat one. With squares and pentagons only, the pointed rings are the four rings of three.
- **Some corners fixed.** With some corners fixed and the other places free, some filling folds to a point exactly when the filling with the smallest corner on offer in every place folds: a bigger corner only adds to the total, and the order round the point never changes it. So that smallest ring, folded flat or overlapping, is the proof that no filling folds. This is the "can't" certificate: the child builds it and points at it with Can't. A ring that lies flat needs the total exactly, so the smallest filling decides nothing there: with two triangles fixed and three places, only two squares and a triangle make the 240° left (Rings 5).

**Trees.** An **opened drawing** is a convex solid seen through one face that has been taken out: a connected drawing in the plane with no crossings, whose dots and lines are the solid's corners and edges and whose closed regions are its other faces (a Schlegel view; the worksheet's tetrahedron and cube, and the card's octahedron).

- **Erasing a line.** A line with two different spaces on its sides lies on a loop: erasing it keeps every dot joined and merges the two spaces, so exactly one closed region goes (whether the other space is the outside or another region). A line with the same space on both sides is the only way between its ends: erasing it cuts the drawing in two and leaves the regions alone.
- **Down to a tree.** A tree, every dot joined and no loop, has no closed region and one line fewer than dots: a tree of two dots or more has an end dot (walk along lines without going back; with no loop the walk must stop, at an end dot), and taking an end dot off with its one line leaves a tree, until one dot is left (guide p. 7's leaf argument, which Trees 3 makes a move). Erasing loop lines until a tree is left therefore takes one erasure per region, and dots − lines + regions stays at its tree value, V − (V − 1) + 0 = 1, all the way; taking end dots off keeps it at 1 too, down to 1 − 0 + 0. With the missing face back, V − E + F = 2. The erased lines join the faces into a second tree (each face a dot, each erased line a link), F − 1 lines beside the tree's V − 1: Euler's formula again.
- **Redrawing.** Problem 6 puts a new dot along an edge of the cube: one more corner and one more edge, the same faces, so V − E + F is still 2. Opened, that dot has only two lines, on one loop; once one is erased, the other is the only way to it (Trees 2).
- **The counts.** Tetrahedron 4 dots, 6 lines, 3 regions; cube with a dot on an edge 9, 13, 5; octahedron 6, 12, 7. Any order of erasures works as long as no erasure cuts; peeling from the outside never gets stuck.

Experiments come first: build rings, fold them and watch the gap shrink as corners are added; erase lines and see which ones cut dots off. The conjectures (three to five triangles fold and six lie flat, four squares lie flat, two squares with three more corners never fold, every erasure takes one region, every end dot takes one line) come from the shelf and the board. The explanations (the angle total, the smallest filling, the case check the solved shelf draws, one region per erasure, the tree's count) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes; the worksheet's written proofs (Problems 7 and 8), the total gap of 720° (Problems 4, 5 and 8) and the reverse inventories (Problem 9) stay on paper.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The ring board.** A row of shape buttons above the board adds a corner of that shape (each button is the shape's picture, named "Add a triangle corner" and so on). The board is a square card with the point in the middle and a whole turn dashed round it. Corners go round counterclockwise from the right in the order they are added; the gap left over is shaded, and corners past a whole turn are drawn faded with a red dashed edge, with a red arc over the overlap from the ray to the right (drawn in pieces of at most 180°; past a second whole turn, as with seven or eight pentagons, it goes on round a circle just outside the first). Tapping a corner takes it out (Enter or Space on a focused corner does the same); the last one drawn is on top, so a tap where two overlap takes out the one that shows. **Fold** folds a ring of three or more (in Make, a full ring): a pointed corner is drawn in shaded faces seen from outside, from the direction that shows every face most (the centre of the smallest cap holding the faces' outward directions; the cone's axis would hide a face whenever the cone's centre falls outside the corner, as for a pentagon and two triangles), tipped up to 18° while every face still shows at least 22% of itself, and read from its first turning in the alphabet, so a ring turned round is drawn alike; a ring that makes a whole turn is drawn lying flat with "It lies flat."; an overlap stays on the board with "They overlap.". Tapping the folded corner lays it flat again. Any change to the ring clears the fold. The words under the board are only about the ring on the board, and only while the puzzle is open.
- **Find every** (Rings 1, 3, 4, 7 and 9). **Keep** puts the ring on the shelf below as a small picture, with no verdict, a copy of a kept ring (turned, flipped or not) included; it is off only while the ring on the board is, corner for corner, the one just kept, so a second tap doesn't shelve it twice. Tapping a ring on the shelf takes it off. **That's all** judges the whole shelf: if every ring on it has the goal's fold, none is there twice and none is missing, the puzzle is solved; otherwise one refusal, in the shelf's box: the first kept ring that doesn't belong, marked ("This one lies flat.", "This one overlaps." or "This one folds to a point."), else the first ring kept twice, both copies marked ("These two are the same ring."), else "There is another.". Only the refusal's subject is marked, and any change to the shelf (a Keep or a ring taken off) clears the refusal and its marks, so no verdict stands over a ring it doesn't describe; a change to the ring on the board leaves it, since it is about the shelf. There are no slots and no count. Rings 7 asks for rings of five corners, and five dots by the shape buttons show the places. **The solved shelf** is redrawn as the case check: a row for each number of corners, the child's rings folded and in order of their total, each row ending with the smallest ring of its size too big for the goal, drawn flat in a dashed box: for a pointed corner the first that reaches a whole turn (SSSS, SSTTT and TTTTTT lie flat in Rings 9), for a flat ring the first past one (SSSTT and STTTTT overlap in Rings 4, and a row of four has none), so every bigger ring of that size misses too; the last row is the first size whose smallest ring is already too big.
- **Make** (Rings 2, 5, 6 and 8). The ring starts with its fixed corners, each with a pin, and the dots by the shape buttons show how many places it has. The fixed corners can't be taken out. A full ring with the goal's fold (a pointed corner, or in Rings 5 a flat ring) solves the puzzle. **Can't** is on only for a full ring that has been folded and didn't meet the goal. It is accepted when every free place holds the smallest corner on offer and the ring doesn't fold to a point; the board keeps the ring folded flat or overlapping, with "Even the smallest corners make a whole turn." (or "go past a whole turn"). Every other Can't gets "That ring doesn't show it.", the same words on every ring of every Make puzzle, possible or not, with nothing marked; it stays, locked, until the ring changes.
- **The tree board.** The opened drawing on the shared graph board: dark dots, ink lines and each closed region in its own tint (the tint of the first face of the whole drawing it holds), so when two regions become one, one tint takes over both, and a region opened to the outside loses its tint. Tapping a line erases it (drawn faint and dashed); tapping an erased line draws it back; Enter or Space on a focused line does the same. A tap anywhere in the lines' wide tap areas goes to the nearest line, so near a dot where several meet, the line under the finger wins; a dot itself is not a control. Dots cut off from the biggest piece turn red. When a tree is left, it is drawn in pine. In Trees 3 the tree is not the end: the lines stay as they are (Undo goes back), the dots become the controls, and a tap anywhere near a dot left (within about a fingertip) goes to the nearest one; an end dot, one with one line, comes off with its line and stays faint, and a dot with more lines doesn't move. One dot left solves it. The screen-reader status names the lines erased and the dots taken off and says whether every dot is joined and whether any region is shaded, with no counts.
- **Copy.** One short objective per puzzle, because the board alone can't say which claim is asked: "Find every ring that folds to a point." (or "that lies flat", or "of 5 corners"), "Fold a pointed corner.", "Make a ring that lies flat.", "Erase lines until a tree is left." and "Erase lines down to a tree, then take off end dots until one dot is left.". How to play opens with what the corners and Fold are, or what an opened drawing and a tree are, then the gestures. The playground shows no objective.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then, in find-every puzzles: a kept ring that doesn't belong to take off, with why ("One ring on the shelf lies flat. Take it off."), a ring kept twice ("Two rings on the shelf are the same ring. Take one off."), a ring on the board to Keep ("This ring folds to a point and isn't on the shelf. Press Keep."), the next corner to add or take out toward the nearest missing ring ("Add a triangle.", "Take out a square.", or "Tap the folded corner to lay it flat."), and "Every ring is on the shelf. Press That's all.". In possible Make puzzles, the steps to the filling closest to the child's, then "Press Fold.". In impossible ones, swap each bigger free corner for the smallest, fold with the reason ("Fold it. Triangles are the smallest corners here, and even with them the corners make a whole turn."), then "Any other corner in a free place is bigger, so no ring like this folds to a point. Press Can't.". In trees, a line to draw back while dots are cut off ("Some dots are cut off. Draw a line back to join them."), then a line with the outside on one side and a shaded region on the other, and in Trees 3 "Take off an end dot with its line.". The words name a shape or a kind of line, never "this", so the first level makes sense before anything is marked; the second level marks the corner, button, shelf ring, line or dot, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after wrong moves and refused claims, and from random states.

## Certificates and claims

- **Can't: the child builds the certificate and points at it.** The argument "even the smallest corners fill the turn" is a ring the child can build and fold, so a bare Can't is never a solve: Can't is off until a full ring has been folded and failed, and it is judged on that ring. Only the ring with the smallest corner in every free place is accepted. In the two impossible puzzles 1 of 27 fillings (Rings 6) and 1 of 8 (Rings 8) passes, counted by the validator. Every other ring, on those and on the possible puzzles (Rings 2 and 5), gets the one refusal "That ring doesn't show it." with nothing marked and no remedy named, so the refusal says neither which corner to change nor whether a filling exists. The validator checks the words are identical on all four puzzles and that a follower who changes whatever corner a refusal marks solves nothing beyond the fillings that needed no refusal (2,000 random fillings each). The hint chain builds the smallest ring and gives the reason before it reaches Can't; "Press Can't." is never a first hint. A flat goal has no Can't certificate here (that no ring with a pentagon lies flat is a divisibility argument), and the one flat Make puzzle can be done, so Can't there is always refused, as on any possible puzzle.
- **That's all: judged as a whole, drawn by the app.** Keep answers nothing: it shelves any ring of the right size, a copy included, and judges none (its only rule is the double-tap guard above, which looks at the ring corner for corner, not turned or flipped). That's all is off until a ring is kept, and judges the whole shelf once with one short refusal and no count; whether two kept rings are the same after turning or flipping is part of that judgement ("These two are the same ring."). The argument that a shelf is complete is a case check over the rings of each size in order of their total, which can't reasonably be tapped; after a right claim the app draws it, the solved shelf in rows each ending with its smallest ring too big for the goal, and the explanation is in the grown-up notes. Random shelves of the right size pass rarely (the validator counts them, with and without copies; the easiest, Rings 1, passes about 1 in 20 from the six rings a child can keep there).
- **Refusals are kept.** A refused That's all stays locked while the shelf is the same, and a refused Can't while the ring is the same. Undo takes back moves, never the lock: it removes the refusal's words (so the board still changes) and keeps That's all or Can't off until the shelf or ring changes. Refusals print no numbers.
- **Trees need no claim.** A tree is checked on the board (every dot joined, one line fewer than dots); a cut is shown in red at once, which is the drawing itself, not a verdict.
- **Titles, objectives and first hints don't give the answer away.** No title or objective names a ring, a count or the possibility of Can't (every Make puzzle has a Can't button and the same objective for its goal); first hints suggest an action.

## The playground

Triangle, square and pentagon corners, up to eight, with Fold and the folded corner tapped flat again. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. The grown-up notes suggest building the corners of the paper solids on the table: the cube's, the tetrahedron's, the square pyramid's top and base.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the ring, the fold, the shelf, That's all's answer and whether its words show, and done (find every); the ring with its fixed corners first, the fold, Can't's answer and whether its words show, and the claim (make); the ring and fold (playground); the erased lines in order, and in Trees 3 the end dots taken off in order, each with its line (trees). Everything drawn is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: corners from the menu up to the places (or eight), take-outs of free corners only, Fold of a ring of three or more (a full ring in Make) once per ring, laying flat only a pointed or flat fold, Keep of a ring of three or more (of the right size) unless it is corner for corner the ring just kept, taking off only a shelf ring, That's all only on a non-empty shelf not already refused, Can't only on a full folded ring not already refused, lines that exist (in Trees 3 only until a tree is left), end dots only once a tree is left and never the last dot, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: corners from the menu with the fixed ones first; a fold only on a foldable ring and only as Fold gives it; a shelf of keepable rings (copies allowed); a refusal only as the claim would give it on that shelf or ring (Can't's only on a full ring that isn't the proof and hasn't met the goal); done only with exactly the answers, each once; a claim only on a folded smallest ring; erased lines as an increasing list of real lines; end dots only off a tree, each an end dot with its own line when it came off, with one dot left at least.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-corners.mjs` with the mechanic's own functions and checked against the table above, and again by `scripts/validate-corners.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, including this revision. Things to watch: whether children read the folded corner, seen from outside, as the paper fan folded up, and connect it with a corner of a paper solid (if not, the paper solids should come first); whether find-every turns into keeping rings one at a time and pressing That's all after each, using "There is another." as a counter, and whether "the same ring turned or flipped" is understood or produces many "These two are the same ring." refusals (Rings 4 and 9 depend on it); whether the solved shelf's rows are read as the reason the shelf is complete; whether "That ring doesn't show it." leaves children stuck on the impossible puzzles (if so, the hint chain is the help, not the refusal); whether, in the tree drawings, children erase freely and draw lines back, or look for a line with two tints beside it, and whether the red cut-off dots and the dot on the cube's edge are noticed; and whether, in Trees 3, the lines locking once a tree is left surprises children who wanted another tree (Undo goes back).

- **More drawings.** Card fix 3 also names the prism's (6 dots, 9 lines, 4 regions) and the square pyramid's through its base (5, 8, 4); only the octahedron's is built.
