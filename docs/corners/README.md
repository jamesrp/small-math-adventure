# Corners

October 17, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 56 of the Bellingham math circle (corners of a solid, Euler's formula and the angular gap), in two flat groups: **Rings**, a corner builder, and **Trees**, erasing an opened drawing down to a tree on the shared graph board. They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-56.md) and its second run decide what is built; its Decision (October 10, 2026) reads: a corner builder where the child places triangle, square and pentagon corners around a point and the app folds a pointed corner, lies flat or refuses an overlap, with every closing ring found, a checked "That's all" and the angle total as the "can't" certificate; and erasing edges down to a tree (Problem 7) on Week 53's graph engine. Unfolding a turning solid stays with the real paper solids, and there is no typed V − E + F and no gap prediction. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet source. The packet has not changed since the card: its last source commit (October 6) predates the card (October 10).

## The mathematics

**Corners.** Regular polygons with the same side fit round a point side to side. A corner of a regular n-gon is 180(n − 2)/n degrees: a triangle's is 60°, a square's 90° and a pentagon's 108°. A **ring** is three or more corners in order round the point; read from another corner, or the other way round, it is the same ring. **Fold** joins the first corner's free side to the last one's.

- **When a ring folds.** Three or more corners fold into a pointed corner with no dent exactly when their angles add to less than 360°. (For a convex corner the face angles must total less than a whole turn, and each must be less than the others together; every angle here is less than two triangles, so the second condition always holds.) At exactly 360° the corners lie flat, and past it they overlap. The missing part of the turn is the corner's **gap** (its angular defect). The app's folded corner is the one whose edges lie on a round cone; the validator checks every face angle and that every face has the rest of the corner on one side.
- **How many rings.** Up to turning and flipping, 11 rings of triangles and squares fold to a point: three, four or five triangles; three squares; and seven mixes (SST, STT, SSST, SSTT, STST, STTT, STTTT). They come from 10 sets of corners, since two squares and two triangles go round in two ways, side by side or opposite. Four rings lie flat: SSSS, TTTTTT and the two orders of two squares with three triangles (SSTTT and STSTT). Pentagons add 14 pointed rings (25 in all) and no flat one. With squares and pentagons only, the pointed rings are the four rings of three.
- **Some corners fixed.** With some corners fixed and the other places free, some filling folds exactly when the filling with the smallest corner on offer in every place folds: a bigger corner only adds to the total, and the order round the point never changes it. So that smallest ring, folded flat or overlapping, is the proof that no filling folds. This is the "can't" certificate: the child builds it and points at it with Can't.

**Trees.** An **opened drawing** is a convex solid seen through one face that has been taken out: a connected drawing in the plane with no crossings, whose dots and lines are the solid's corners and edges and whose closed regions are its other faces (a Schlegel view; the worksheet's tetrahedron and cube, and the card's octahedron).

- **Erasing a line.** A line with two different spaces on its sides lies on a loop: erasing it keeps every dot joined and merges the two spaces, so exactly one closed region goes (whether the other space is the outside or another region). A line with the same space on both sides is the only way between its ends: erasing it cuts the drawing in two and leaves the regions alone.
- **Down to a tree.** A tree, every dot joined and no loop, has no closed region and one line fewer than dots (take off a dot at the end of a branch, with its line, until one dot is left). Erasing loop lines until a tree is left therefore takes one erasure per region, and dots − lines + regions stays at its tree value, V − (V − 1) + 0 = 1, all the way. With the missing face back, V − E + F = 2. The erased lines join the faces into a second tree (each face a dot, each erased line a link), F − 1 lines beside the tree's V − 1: Euler's formula again.
- **The counts.** Tetrahedron 4 dots, 6 lines, 3 regions; cube 8, 12, 5; octahedron 6, 12, 7. Any order of erasures works as long as no erasure cuts; peeling from the outside never gets stuck.

Experiments come first: build rings, fold them and watch the gap shrink as corners are added; erase lines and see which ones cut dots off. The conjectures (three to five triangles fold and six lie flat, four squares lie flat, two squares with three more corners never fold, every erasure takes one region) come from the shelf and the board. The explanations (the angle total, the smallest filling, one region per erasure, the tree's count) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes; the worksheet's written proofs (Problems 7 and 8), the total gap of 720° (Problems 4, 5 and 8) and the reverse inventories (Problem 9) stay on paper.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The ring board.** A row of shape buttons above the board adds a corner of that shape (each button is the shape's picture, named "Add a triangle corner" and so on). The board is a square card with the point in the middle and a whole turn dashed round it. Corners go round counterclockwise from the right in the order they are added; the gap left over is shaded, and corners past a whole turn are drawn faded with a red dashed edge, with a red arc over the overlap. Tapping a corner takes it out (Enter or Space on a focused corner does the same); the last one drawn is on top, so a tap where two overlap takes out the one that shows. **Fold** folds a ring of three or more (in Make, a full ring): a pointed corner is drawn in shaded faces seen from above; a ring that makes a whole turn is drawn lying flat with "It lies flat."; an overlap stays on the board with "They overlap.". Tapping the folded corner lays it flat again. Any change to the ring clears the fold.
- **Find every** (Rings 1, 3, 4, 7 and 9). **Keep** puts the ring on the shelf below as a small picture, with no verdict; a ring already there, turned or flipped, gives "Already on the shelf." and rings its copy. Tapping a ring on the shelf takes it off. **That's all** judges the whole shelf: if every ring on it has the goal's fold and none is missing, the puzzle is solved and the shelf is drawn folded; otherwise one refusal, either the first kept ring that doesn't belong, ringed on the shelf ("This one lies flat.", "This one overlaps." or "This one folds to a point."), or "There is another.". There are no slots and no count. Rings 7 asks for rings of five corners, and five dots by the shape buttons show the places.
- **Make** (Rings 2, 5, 6 and 8). The ring starts with its fixed corners, each with a pin, and the dots by the shape buttons show how many places it has. The fixed corners can't be taken out. A full ring that folds to a point solves the puzzle. **Can't** is on only for a full ring that has been folded and didn't fold to a point. It is accepted when every free place holds the smallest corner on offer; the board keeps the ring folded flat or overlapping, with "Even the smallest corners make a whole turn." (or "go past a whole turn"). Otherwise "This corner could be smaller." rings the first free corner that isn't the smallest, on possible and impossible puzzles alike.
- **The tree board.** The opened drawing on the shared graph board: dark dots, ink lines and the closed regions shaded. Tapping a line erases it (drawn faint and dashed); tapping an erased line draws it back; Enter or Space on a focused line does the same. A tap anywhere in the lines' wide tap areas goes to the nearest line, so near a dot where several meet, the line under the finger wins; a dot itself is not a control. Dots cut off from the biggest piece turn red. When a tree is left, it is drawn in pine.
- **Copy.** One short objective per puzzle, because the board alone can't say which claim is asked: "Find every ring that folds to a point." (or "that lies flat", or "of 5 corners"), "Fold a pointed corner." and "Erase lines until a tree is left.". How to play opens with what the corners and Fold are, or what an opened drawing and a tree are, then the gestures. The playground shows no objective.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then, in find-every puzzles: a kept ring that doesn't belong to take off, with why ("Take this ring off the shelf: it lies flat."), a ring on the board to Keep ("This ring folds to a point and isn't on the shelf. Press Keep."), the next corner to add or take out toward the nearest missing ring ("Add a triangle.", "Take out this square.", or "Tap the folded corner to lay it flat."), and "Every ring is on the shelf. Press That's all.". In possible Make puzzles, the steps to the closing filling closest to the child's, then "Press Fold.". In impossible ones, swap each bigger free corner for the smallest, fold with the reason ("Fold it. Triangles are the smallest corners here, and even with them the corners make a whole turn."), then "Any other corner in a free place is bigger, so no ring like this folds to a point. Press Can't.". In trees, a line to draw back while dots are cut off, then a line with the outside on one side and a shaded region on the other. The second level marks the corner, button, shelf ring or line, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after wrong moves and refused claims, and from random states.

## Certificates and claims

- **Can't: the child builds the certificate and points at it.** The argument "even the smallest corners fill the turn" is a ring the child can build and fold, so a bare Can't is never a solve: Can't is off until a full ring has been folded and failed, and it is judged on that ring. Only the ring with the smallest corner in every free place is accepted. In the two impossible puzzles 1 of 27 fillings (Rings 6) and 1 of 8 (Rings 8) passes, counted by the validator; every other filling is refused with "This corner could be smaller." and the corner ringed. On a puzzle that can fold, every Can't gets the same refusal, so Can't is no test of whether a filling exists. The hint chain builds the smallest ring and gives the reason before it reaches Can't; "Press Can't." is never a first hint.
- **That's all: judged as a whole, drawn by the app.** Keep answers nothing. That's all is off until a ring is kept, and judges the whole shelf once with one short refusal and no count. The argument that a shelf is complete is a case check over the sets of corners up to a whole turn and their orders, which can't reasonably be tapped; after a right claim the app draws the shelf folded, and the explanation is in the grown-up notes. Random shelves of the right size pass rarely (the validator counts them; the easiest, Rings 1, passes about 1 in 20 from the six rings a child can keep there).
- **Refusals are kept.** A refused That's all stays locked while the shelf is the same, and a refused Can't while the ring is the same. Undo takes back moves, never the lock: it removes the refusal's words (so the board still changes) and keeps That's all or Can't off until the shelf or ring changes. Refusals print no numbers.
- **Trees need no claim.** A tree is checked on the board (every dot joined, one line fewer than dots); a cut is shown in red at once, which is the drawing itself, not a verdict.
- **Titles, objectives and first hints don't give the answer away.** No title or objective names a ring, a count or the possibility of Can't (every Make puzzle has the same objective and a Can't button); first hints suggest an action.

## The playground

Triangle, square and pentagon corners, up to eight, with Fold and the folded corner tapped flat again. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. The grown-up notes suggest building the corners of the paper solids on the table: the cube's, the tetrahedron's, the square pyramid's top and base.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the ring, the fold, the shelf, "Already on the shelf.", That's all's answer and whether its words show, and done (find every); the ring with its fixed corners first, the fold, Can't's answer and whether its words show, and the claim (make); the ring and fold (playground); the erased lines in order (trees). Everything drawn is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: corners from the menu up to the places (or eight), take-outs of free corners only, Fold of a ring of three or more (a full ring in Make) once per ring, laying flat only a pointed or flat fold, Keep of a ring of three or more (of the right size) not twice in a row, taking off only a shelf ring, That's all only on a non-empty shelf not already refused, Can't only on a full folded ring not already refused, lines that exist, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: corners from the menu with the fixed ones first; a fold only on a foldable ring and only as Fold gives it; a shelf of keepable rings, each once up to turning and flipping; a copy pointer only at the board's own ring; a refusal only as the claim would give it on that shelf or ring, Can't's only as "could be smaller"; done only with exactly the answers; a claim only on a folded smallest ring; erased lines as an increasing list of real lines.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-corners.mjs` with the mechanic's own functions and checked against the table above, and again by `scripts/validate-corners.mjs` by other methods.
