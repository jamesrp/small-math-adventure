# Nesting cards

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 19 of the Bellingham math circle (no card inside another: subset antichains). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 19 worksheet is unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-19.md) says keep, and its App fit and Decision shaped this family: choose cards from a scrambled deck with nesting pairs linked on screen, reach a target size or extend a fixed start, and certify "best" by dragging every card into nested rows.

## The mathematics

Each card carries some of a few pictures (circle, triangle, square, star and, on one deck, a diamond), each always in its own corner. Write the pictures as letters A to E. A card is a subset of the pictures, and card S **fits inside** card T when S ⊆ T. Two different cards **nest** when one fits inside the other. A **collection** is a set of cards with no card inside another: an antichain of the subsets ordered by inclusion. A **row** is a list of cards in which each fits inside the next: a chain.

- **A row gives a collection at most one card.** Any two cards of a row nest. So if r rows hold every card of a deck, every collection has at most r cards. A collection with one card in each of r rows therefore proves two things at once: no collection is bigger, and no fewer rows hold the deck. This is the easy half of **Dilworth's theorem**; the hard half says that for any finite set of cards the largest collection and the fewest rows are always equal, so this two-sided certificate always exists: on the full decks, after cards are removed, and with cards kept (below).
- **Cards with the same number of pictures never nest.** S ⊆ T with |S| = |T| forces S = T. So each size is a collection.
- **The full decks.** On n pictures the largest collections have C(n, ⌊n/2⌋) cards: 2, 3, 6 and 10 for n = 2, 3, 4, 5 (**Sperner's theorem**). Rows meet the bound: on the sixteen-card deck, six rows each holding one two-picture card. The largest collections are only the middle sizes: on four pictures the six two-picture cards are the only collection of six, on three pictures the one-picture and two-picture cards are the only collections of three, and on five pictures the ten two-picture and ten three-picture cards are the only collections of ten. The number of all collections, the empty one included, is 6, 20, 168 and 7,581 (Dedekind's numbers); no puzzle asks for these counts, and the validator checks them.
- **Full is not largest.** A collection to which no card can be added need not be as large as possible: on the eight-card deck, the circle-and-triangle card with the square is full (every other card nests with one of them) at two cards, while three is possible.
- **Kept cards.** A collection that must keep a card S can take no card that nests with S. With the circle kept on four pictures, the cards left are the non-empty cards without a circle (the eight-card deck on triangle, square and star, less its empty card), whose largest collection is three, so the most is four. The certificate adapts: a row whose cards all nest with a kept card gives the collection nothing and needs no chosen card.
- **Removing cards.** The adult guide's example (p. 11) removes the circle card and the triangle-and-square card from the eight-card deck. That breaks both collections of three, and two rows then hold the six cards left, so two is the most.

What children are meant to find: that the app's refusals always point at a card that fits inside or holds a chosen one; that each size is safe; that a deck can be full of faded cards and still not largest; and that when every card sits in a row with one chosen card per row, nothing more can be done. Experiments come first (tap, fade, try again); the conjectures (same size never nests, the middle size is best) come from the reach and find-every puzzles; the explanation (one card per row) is the rows puzzles' certificate, and the words for it are grown-up notes.

**The app's representation.** The worksheets have children circle cards and draw rows on paper. In the app a tap chooses a card, but a card that nests with a chosen card is not chosen: it fades, and an ochre dashed line joins it to every chosen card it nests with, which get an ochre ring. So the chosen cards are always a collection, and every refusal shows its reason. (The review card words this as "the app links any two chosen cards that nest"; refusing the choice keeps the screen's rule and the line in one place.) Rows are made by dragging cards into strips below the deck.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Cards are paper squares with ink outlines; each picture has its own corner and colour (circle sun, triangle sky, square rose, star leaf, diamond lilac), and a faint dot marks the corner of a missing picture, so equal pictures line up from card to card. Puzzles with several decks frame each deck.
- **Taps.** Tap a card to choose it (a pine ring) and again to put it back. A card that nests with a chosen card fades instead, with a dashed ochre line to each chosen card it nests with, which get an ochre ring; a tap on a faded card shows its line again. Putting back a chosen card un-fades the cards that nested only with it. Each card answers taps up to the middle of the gap around it; lines never take taps.
- **Reach** (1, 2). Circles above each deck fill as cards are chosen; the deck is done when they are full. No objective is shown: the circles are the goal.
- **Extend** (4, 9). Pinned cards carry a pine pin and stay chosen. "Add as many cards as you can." A deck is done when every other card is chosen or faded and no collection keeping the pins is bigger; its frame turns pine and a grey line joins each faded card to a chosen card it nests with, the whole argument. If every card has been tried and a bigger collection exists, "Another way fits more cards." appears under that deck. That answer is kept in the save, and through Undo, until the deck's chosen cards change, so taking back the last tap does not turn it into a free yes-or-no question.
- **Best** (3, 6, 8, 10, 11). "Choose as many cards as you can, and put every card in a row with one chosen card." (11: "Add as many cards as you can, and put every card in a row.", since a row that nests with the pinned circle needs no chosen card). Drag a card into a row, onto the dashed strip to start a new row, onto a card in a row, or back to the cards; a row keeps its cards smallest first, and an ochre bar sits between two neighbours that don't nest. There is always one more dashed strip than the rows in use, so the screen never shows how many rows are needed. A circle at the start of each row fills pine when the row holds exactly one chosen card, turns ochre when it holds more, and stays dashed when it holds none; in puzzle 11 a row whose cards all nest with the pinned circle gets a slashed circle and needs no chosen card. The puzzle is solved when every card is in a row, every row nests, and each row has one chosen card (or the slashed circle): the certificate above.
- **Every** (5, 7, 12). "Find every collection of this size." When the chosen cards fill the circles the collection is kept below as small cards and the cards go back; making it again gives "Found already." and a flash on the one kept. **That's all** says "There is another." and then waits (disabled) until a new collection is found; when none is left it solves the puzzle. Puzzle 7 has only one collection to find, so its That's all is the claim that no other six exists (4–5 P8). There are no empty slots and no count.
- **"Can't add" and "can't be bigger".** There is no Can't button. "Can't add" is the child's own taps: each faded card is joined to the chosen card it nests with (the line the review card asks for), and an extend deck is judged only when its last card has been tried: done, or "Another way fits more cards.". "Can't be bigger" is the child's rows, judged as they stand: nothing is tinted or ringed in advance, a wrong row is not refused or ignored but shows its bar or its mark, and a set of rows that is not a certificate simply does not solve. Tapping every card in a plain order is not, in general, a way round the choice. In every reach, extend and find-every deck where some full collection is smaller than the most, apart from puzzle 1's first group (below), tapping in reading order (along the rows, from the top left) chooses a card that stops the collection short. Puzzle 2 stops short in all six plain orders (along the rows either way, from the top or the bottom, and down or up the columns); the sixteen-card decks of puzzles 7 and 9 stop short tapped backwards, down the columns (four or five wide) and up the columns as shown. Some plain orders still finish: puzzle 1's second and fourth groups tapped right to left; puzzle 4's third deck in every plain order except reading order and down the columns (with the circle-and-triangle card pinned, any order that reaches a two-picture card before the square finishes, so no layout stops both reading order and its reverse); and puzzle 9's second deck tapped along the rows from the bottom left. Decks with nothing to choose (puzzle 1's third group, puzzle 4's first two, puzzle 9's first) finish in any order, where trying every card is the whole argument, and so does puzzle 1's first group, kept gentle as the very first deck. In find-every puzzles a plain order makes at most one collection, and That's all still has to be claimed. In rows puzzles a plain order can choose a largest collection (puzzle 8's does, in reading order), but the certificate is the rows, which taps don't make. That's all is the one claim; its answer is kept through Undo (Undo takes back moves, not "There is another."). That's all leaves the cards as they were, so one Undo right after it also takes back the tap before it.
- **Keyboard.** Tab reaches every card; Enter or Space taps. In rows puzzles a number key puts the focused card in that row (one past the last row starts a new one), and 0, Delete or Backspace takes it out. After a move focus returns to the card moved, never to That's all.
- **Hints.** Before any move the first hint is the authored nudge (it names no answer). Then hints aim at the largest collection (or, in reach and every puzzles, a collection of the size not yet found) that shares the most with the chosen cards: put back a chosen card not in it, choose one that is ("Choose the circle and star card."), then in extend decks try each card left, and in rows puzzles take a card out of a row that breaks or mixes two rows, and put cards in rows ("Put the star card in the row with the empty card."). The rows follow a fewest-row cover chosen to keep the child's rows where it can (a largest-weight matching of "fits inside"), as long as following those hints reaches a solve (checked by playing them ahead); otherwise they follow a cover fixed by the deck, whose every hint takes out a card in the wrong place for it or puts one in the right place, so a run of hints always ends in a solve. In find-every puzzles, once every collection is found the hint gives the reason ("Every collection of three here has cards of only one size, and you have found both."; in puzzle 7, "… and you have found it.") rather than "Press That's all."; the second level marks the button. The second level marks the card or row; the third offers Apply hint. Hints are memoised per board and take a few milliseconds (tens under heavy load when a rows hint is first played ahead).

## The playground

The four-, eight- and sixteen-card decks, chosen with three deck buttons (the eight-card deck first). Taps and rows work as in the puzzles. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. A grown-up or a partner can set a challenge ("choose two cards: how many more can join?").

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is each deck's chosen and faded cards, plus the rows (best and playground), or the decks told "Another way fits more cards." (extend), or the collections found, the answer to That's all and the done flag (every), or the deck picked (playground). Lines, rings, marks, bars and messages are derived from it; the card whose line is showing is view state.
- Only legal moves are accepted: taps on a deck's cards (a tap on a pinned or faded card changes nothing), drops into an existing row or one new row, That's all only in find-every puzzles and not again until a new collection, deck buttons only in the playground, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: the chosen cards are a collection holding every pinned card; every faded card nests with a chosen one; a deck told "Another way fits more cards." holds a full collection smaller than the most; rows hold each card at most once, smallest first, with no empty row; every collection found has the size and is found once; none rests chosen at full size (it would have been kept); "There is another." only while one is missing; "Found already." only for one found, with no cards chosen; done only with every one found.
- The answers and witnesses are computed by `scripts/build-nests.mjs` with the mechanic's search and hints, and checked again by `scripts/validate-nests.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch first:
  1. Whether a fading card and its line read as "this one fits inside that one" rather than as a tap that failed, and whether children in the extend puzzles keep trying every card until the deck is done, or stop at a full collection and wait.
  2. Whether dragging every card into rows is manageable, especially sixteen cards on a phone (cards about 47 px, the rows below the deck), and whether the filled circle for "one chosen card in this row" is understood without reading How to play.
  3. Whether the find-every puzzles (5, 7 and 12) feel like a discovery, or That's all is pressed as a guess to hear "There is another."
- **Refuse, not link.** The review card's App fit says the app links two chosen cards that nest; here the second card is refused and linked instead, so a collection is never illegal on screen. If children want to choose both and see the clash, a version that allows it would need a different solve check.
- **Reshuffled decks.** Puzzles 2, 4, 7, 9, 10 and 11 no longer show the worksheets' printed order, so that tapping in order gets stuck; a child with the paper packet beside the screen will see the same cards in other places. The rows hints sometimes fall back to the cover fixed by the deck (when following the child's own rows would go round in circles), and then they may take apart a row the child could have kept.
- **Lines.** A line between neighbouring cards is only a gap long, and the ochre ring on the chosen card carries the link there; a line between far cards crosses the cards between (over a white halo, never taking their taps), and a finished sixteen-card deck in puzzle 9 shows fourteen lines at once.
- **Rows on five pictures.** Puzzle 12 has no rows: ten rows of up to six cards is a great deal of dragging. A rows version, or rows for a part of the deck, is open.
- **Paper only.** K–1 P2 and P3, the written explanations, counting all collections, and the bonus packet stay on paper.
- **Grade levels and story.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail. Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Devices.** Drags are tested in Chromium with a mouse and with simulated touch; not yet on a real tablet or phone.
