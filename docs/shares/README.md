# Fair shares

October 10, 2026. Ten puzzles built from Week 58 of the Bellingham math circle (fair division with different values). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 58 packet is unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-58.md) says keep, and its App fit and Decision shaped this family: no live value table to click through (the app never adds up a tray while the child works), cards dragged into trays beside each person's taste card, find-every puzzles ended by a checked That's all with no count, the identical-tastes odd case certified by the child's pairing and then a halving tool, favourites with four or five people whose Can't is certified by k people with fewer than k favourites, and a cut-and-choose duel. Ties count as no envy, one person's number is never set beside another's, and there are no "will B envy?" screens. Problems 4 and 7 (the general proofs) stay on paper.

## The mathematics

People share things they value differently, and each person adds up their own values (values are additive). A division gives every item to somebody.

- **Envy-free and proportional.** A division is envy-free when each person counts their own share worth at least as much as each other share, by their own values; a tie is fine. It is proportional when each person counts their own share worth at least 1/n of everything. Adding up one person's comparisons (own ≥ each other share) gives n × own ≥ total, so envy-free implies proportional. For two people the two coincide. The comparisons are always within one person: A's 9 is never compared with B's 3.
- **Different tastes help.** A (red 3, blue 1) and B (red 1, blue 3) sharing R1, R2, R3, B1 have 16 labelled divisions, of which exactly 4 are envy-free: all reds to A (B ties 3 against 3) or any two reds to A, with the blue always to B (puzzles 1 and 4). With R1, R2, B1, B2 there are 5: both reds to A, or one red and one blue each, four ways that are all ties (puzzle 5). Whether a division works depends only on how many reds and blues A gets, so the catalog of those counts, with the binomial number of labelled ways in each fair cell, certifies that a list is complete.
- **Whole items can block it.** U and V both count each of three red cards as 1. With identical tastes each person counts the two trays the same way, so nobody envies only when the trays are worth the same: with equal cards, the same number each. Pairing the cards one for U, one for V leaves one over, so no whole division works; cutting that card in half gives each 1½ (puzzle 2). Pairing proves impossibility here only because both tastes are the same and every card is worth the same; it is not a general certificate.
- **Favourites: Hall's theorem.** With n people, n items and one item each, a person envies exactly when someone else holds an item they value more than their own, so envy-free means everyone holds a top-valued item (a favourite). That is a perfect matching in the bipartite graph of people and favourites. By Hall's marriage theorem it exists exactly when every group of k people likes at least k items between them, so a short group (k people, fewer than k favourites) certifies Can't (puzzles 3, 6, 8, 9). When each person has exactly two favourites, the people are edges of a multigraph on the items, and a matching orients each edge to one end: a component with as many people as items has exactly two orientations, which is why puzzle 8 has two answers and such a puzzle can never have exactly one.
- **Cut and choose.** On a strip that can be cut anywhere, if the cutter cuts into two pieces equal by their own values, the cutter gets exactly half whichever piece the chooser takes, and the chooser, taking the piece better for them, gets at least half. Both are envy-free. It needs an exact cut by value, not by length or colour: a cut at the colour join can lose half when the chooser's taste is like the cutter's (puzzles 7 and 10). The app's strips are 12 squares so an exact cut lies between squares.

Experiments come first (give out cards, try shapes, cut). The conjectures (two reds for A, the blue for B; ties are fine; three people who like two shapes can't all be content; cut where it is equal for you) come from refusals and stars. The explanations (the catalog, the pairing, Hall's condition, the cut-and-choose guarantee) are in each puzzle's grown-up notes; Week 58's written arguments (Problems 4 and 7) stay on paper.

## How a puzzle plays

- **Trays (puzzles 1, 2, 4, 5).** Each person has a taste card: their token, then a red chip and a blue chip, each with dots and a numeral (only the colours on the board). Below it is their tray. Cards start on the table. Drag a card into a tray or back to the table, or tap a card to lift it (pine ring; the other cards rest) and tap a tray or the table; arrow keys send a focused card to the left tray, the right tray or (down) the table. Nothing is added up while the child works.
- **Fair** is accepted only with every card given out. If somebody would swap, "A would swap." appears and, under each tray, that one person's count of it (A's token and a number); nobody else's numbers appear. Fair then waits for a change. In puzzle 1 a fair division solves it.
- **Find every (4, 5).** A fair division is kept as a small thumbnail of the two trays, and the cards stay put so the next one is a small change. Fair on one already kept says "Found already." **That's all** says "There is another." until every one is kept, and then stays off until a new one is found. There are no slots and no count. After a right That's all, the catalog appears: a grid by how many reds (rows) and blues (columns) A gets, each cell showing A's tray as coloured pips, fair cells in pine with "×3"-style counts of the labelled ways. It is drawn by the app rather than tapped, because the certificate is the whole table of cases, not a few things to point at.
- **Can't and the cut (2).** Can't is off until all three cards are given out, so it is a claim about the child's own attempt. After Can't the child pairs the cards, one from each tray: tap a card, then a card in the other tray. Nothing marks which taps are right. A second card from the same tray is refused ("One from each tray.") and the taps clear; tapping a paired card unpairs it. The tap that leaves no pair to make is the claim, judged at once: accepted when exactly one card is left over (ochre ring, "One left over."), refused otherwise with the count ("3 left over." when all three cards share a tray, where Can't itself is the claim because nothing pairs across; "They pair up." in the validator's four-card variant). A refusal clears the taps, turns Can't off and is kept in the save, through Undo, until a card moves. The certificate is the child's own pairing: a lopsided attempt can't certify anything, so the child has to bring the trays within one card first. Then **Cut** is the halving tool: press it and tap a card, which becomes two half cards (½ R3) in the same tray, each worth half. Fair with a whole card and a half each solves it.
- **Favourites (3, 6, 8, 9).** Shapes not given sit at the top; each person is a row with their token, their favourite shapes and a slot. Drag a shape onto a person (or the top, to take it back), or tap a shape and then a person; a person holds one shape, and giving them another swaps the two. The puzzle is solved when everyone holds a favourite. **Can't** is off until a shape has been given. After Can't, tap people (the ones tapped get an ochre ring; nothing marks the others) and press **Check**, which needs a group size to judge: a short group is accepted and drawn, the people ringed with their favourite shapes, "3 people, 2 shapes."; a group with enough shapes is refused with its own counts, "2 people, 4 shapes.", the taps clear, and that refusal is kept in the save, through Undo, until a shape moves, so the same group can't be checked twice on the same shapes.
- **Cut and choose (7, 10).** Your taste card and B's, face down until you cut. "Cut so you get half, whichever piece B takes." Tap or slide along the strip to place the knife on a join between squares (Tab and Enter work on each join), then press **Cut**. B takes the piece worth more to B (on a tie, the one worth less to you). The pieces separate with B's token over B's piece and your own count of each piece under it. An equal cut gets a star (★) and round two starts; otherwise "Not equal for you." and the knife can be placed again. In round two B cuts the strip equal by B's values and the board says "Take a piece."; tap one. The bigger one for you gets a star and solves it; the other gets "The other piece is more for you."
- **Copy.** The board shows one objective at most: "Share the cards so nobody would swap." (1, 2), "Find every way nobody would swap." (4, 5), "Give everyone a favourite." (3, 6, 8, 9), and in the duel the round's own line. How to play has the full rules, including that a tie is fine.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge; it is never a claim. Then hints move toward the nearest fair division (fewest cards to move) one card at a time ("Put R2 in B's tray."), then "Press Fair."; in find-every puzzles toward the nearest one not yet found, then "Press That's all." In puzzle 2 they first give the cards out, then give the reason with the claim ("U and V count every card the same, so nobody would swap only when each has as many cards. Pair the cards one from each tray: one is left over."), with the second level marking Can't and the cards to pair, then the cut. No hint says "Press Can't". In favourites they give shapes toward the matching that keeps most of the child's ("Give the circle to C."); in a can't they first give a shape, then name the short group with its counts ("A, B and C only like the circle and the triangle: 3 people, 2 shapes."), and the second level marks Can't and the group. In the duel they give the total and the half, then the cut, then the piece to take with both of your counts. The second level marks the card, tray, shape, person, join or piece on the board, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start and after wrong moves and refused claims.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is: where each card is (and which card is cut, a shown Can't with its pairs, or a refused Can't with the count left over) with the ways found and That's all's refusal; or who holds each shape, whether a shape has been given, a shown short group or the last refused group; or the child's cut and the piece taken. Everything shown, including who would swap, the counts and the catalog, is derived from it.
- **Undo takes back moves, not claims.** Ways found stay found, a refused That's all stays refused until a new way is found, a shown Can't (with its pairs) stays shown, and a refused Can't or a refused group stays refused while the cards or shapes are where they were, so a claim followed by Undo answers nothing new. A refused Fair is not a lock: it names who would swap in one arrangement, which the child could check themselves.
- Only legal moves are accepted: cards and shapes that exist, to trays and people that exist; Fair only with everything given out and once per arrangement; That's all only in find-every puzzles and not twice without a new way; Can't only after every card is given out, with disjoint pairs each taking one card from each tray and nothing left to pair across, once per arrangement; one cut, only after Can't; groups of two or more people in order; cuts between squares, not the same cut twice running, and a piece only after a starred cut.
- Saved boards are checked against all of this: found ways are fair, in order; refusals match the board; done only when it is true; the duel's stars match the cuts.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-shares.mjs` with the mechanic's own functions and checked again by `scripts/validate-shares.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch: whether children count both trays with one person's card, or compare A's own tray with B's own tray (the card's main pitfall), and whether "A would swap." with A's two counts helps; whether the find-every puzzles become pressing Fair on every arrangement in turn (16 of them) rather than organizing by how many reds A gets; and whether children compute the equal cut or slide the knife and press Cut until a star appears.
- **Pairing as a gesture.** In puzzle 2 the pairing is taps after Can't, one card from each tray. Whether children read it as "one for you, one for me" is untested; a drawn line between the paired cards may help. With three cards the pairing has one pair, so it can fail only by a same-tray tap or a lopsided attempt; the four-card variant that can be refused with "They pair up." is in the validator only.
- **Three people.** Problem 6's three-person table (proportional, with an envy cycle) and Problem 7's question are not here; the favourites puzzles take its idea to four and five people with one item each.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail. The card's K–1 route (an oral Problem 1 and both trials of Problem 5) maps to puzzles 1 and 2.
