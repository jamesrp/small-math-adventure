# The Lantern Road (version 4)

The road is a race to the Lantern Fair. Six travelers haul their lantern tree along seven stops. A keeper sets the puzzles at each stop. Plume, a show-off peacock, leads a rival caravan one stop ahead and leaves a score to beat. There is no plot or villain. The road has stars, a rival, two earned tools, two side puzzles to come back for, and a finale.

Design decisions, October 2026 (James and Claude):

- Characters live inside the puzzles. Keepers and Plume speak one short line when a puzzle opens and react to what the player does: a mistake, a hint, a solve (and, from October 24, a stuck cover or balanced colors in a proof garden). They never restate rules. Those stay on the board and in How to play.
- The six travelers are a fixed party. They are the pieces in the ferry's seating puzzle and otherwise appear in the scenes, busy at each stop. They do not speak.
- There is no losing. The tension comes from Plume's scores and from stars. Keepers never join the caravan.
- The travelers' wagon goes the whole way. It rides across on Snooze's shell, at the stern behind the deck, and goes up Windy Ridge as the cable car's cargo, which is what Billie's stones balance (decided October 10).
- Art is bright and saturated, with dark stops mixed in (night marsh, Halloween hollow, stormy lighthouse, night fair) the way Bloons TD varies its maps. Art is made separately; see [art/ROADMAP.md](art/ROADMAP.md).

Design decisions, October 24, 2026 (Claude as product owner, before James's playtest), from two critics' play-throughs of the whole road on a phone and a tablet:

- **The balance (Cable-car pebbles, Prize booth) speaks plainly and takes back whole weighings.** One Undo takes back one weighing: the pebbles placed for it and the Weigh, back to the screen after the weighing before. Candidates and answer picks are not Undo steps. Candidates says "maybe" and "normal". A wrong answer is told only from the child's own weighings and names only the pebble they chose ("F balanced, so F isn't the heavy one."); an answer the scale hasn't singled out gets "The scale hasn't shown which one yet. Weigh again." The library's Odd-pebble Balance is the same board.
- **A painted proof ends with the child's answer.** At the Hollow and in the other garden, a finished painting asks "Which color has more squares?" (Gold, Green or Same), and the counts show only after a right answer; the chalk, or the side puzzle's star, comes with that answer. With the Checker the whole proof is that one question. A painting whose colors balance says "Same counts: paint can't prove it here." instead of marking a fault. The proof cards appear only once a star or paint is on the board, so Prove no longer opens on the argument as a checklist.
- **Keepers react to a stuck cover and to balanced colors.** In a garden nobody can cover, a cover with squares left that no domino fits gets a tease (Rattle: "Stuck? Mwahaha! Maybe nobody can…"; Mr. Hops: "Stuck? So was everyone else."). In the other garden, saying the colors are the same gets "Hmm. I'd try stars, myself." This is the 4–5 route to its star proof, which the road has not shown before; the first Hint there also says "Paint can't prove this garden. Try stars." Neither says which squares.
- **The race is visible.** Plume's card shows the child's count beside Plume's number while the puzzle is played ("You 3 · Plume 4 swaps"), and the solved strip repeats both. On the first scored board of each trail (Seats on the shell) Plume says the number once, in Plume's own voice ("I did it in 4 swaps. Bet you can't!"; 4, 6 and 7 swaps by trail, checked against Plume's score by the tests); replays don't repeat it. The travelers' wagon waits at the ferry on a fresh map, before the first puzzle.
- **The first Hint is a free nudge.** It shows the board's first authored hint (the idea, never a place) and never costs the No-hints star. The second and third presses show a move, offer Apply, or (on note-only boards such as the rematch) give the strategy away, so they cost it. Snooze's "Psst. Try here." answers only a hint that shows a move; the nudge gets "Mm… a thought for you." When a hint cost the star, the solved strip says "Replay to win it". A side puzzle still waiting for its tool has no Hint: "Needs the pump" or "Needs the chalk" stands in its place, and hints pressed on it in an older save are cleared when the tool arrives. In Plume's rematch the No-hints star is judged on the winning run only: every game's end closes the hint panel, a costly hint is remembered on the board until the run of wins is broken, and Restart clears it.
- **Plume shows off at Windy Ridge.** Pebbles with Plume is where a child first meets Nim, so Plume is careless while winning: while some pile still has 3 or more pebbles, Plume throws away its first two chances to win in a game, each time with the smallest take from the biggest pile (leftmost on a tie) that leaves the child a winning position and more than one pile. Plume never passes up the last pile. Otherwise it plays a winning move when it has one and, with none, takes 1 from the biggest pile. Every reply follows from the board, so Undo and replays meet the same Plume. Exact chances of winning a game (`tests/road.test.mjs`): tapping at random 14% (K–1, piles 2 and 5), 11% (2–3, 1, 2, 4) and 14% (4–5, 3, 4, 5), against 5%, 1% and 0% under perfect play; taking a whole last pile when there is one and never leaving a single pile, 57%, 28% and 39%. The rematch at the fair stays perfect play. Both pebble games name Plume in their lines, hints and How to play, and the rematch's Go first / Go second screen says how many wins in a row are still needed ("Win three in a row.", "Two more in a row.") or that a game from the same start is practice.
- **Scores mean what they say.** Coloring counts paints (each time a lantern is colored), not colors. Plume's number stays below a press budget; where the fewest presses already fill the budget, any solve would tie Plume, so there is no card and no third star: Landing lamps on 2–3 (toggle-03, 2 of 2) and 4–5 (toggle-05, 4 of 4), Pumpkin patch on 2–3 (toggle-08, 6 of 6), and Harbor lamps on 2–3 (toggle-09, 4 of 4) and 4–5 (toggle-12, 6 of 6). No faster solve exists on any of them. Stars already earned stay, and totals count them.
- **A real ending.** The finale: each keeper says goodbye in one line and Plume concedes, the trail's medal is awarded, the stars show as "55 of 62" with the puzzles that still have stars to win (each opens a fresh replay), and the next steps are this trail's Puzzles shelf and then the next trail up, never a lower one. The finished fair on the map opens its stop sheet, which has a Finale button.
- **Smaller fixes.** Replay from a stop sheet, the Journal or the finale starts a fresh attempt. Lines that restated rules or announced up front that a board is impossible were rewritten (Wick's, Plume's rematch, Rattle's and Mr. Hops's gardens), Billie's kites and the coloring board both say "color", and Wick's mirror room says "the ringed corner". Map stops show a focus ring, and once a stop has a medal the map explains the medal colors beside the star total.
- **Staged boards speak in their picture's words.** How to play, read-aloud, the goal line, hints and feedback name travelers and seats, the bell wheel, ropes and lamps, and boardwalks and platforms (`stageHelp`, `dress` in `dist/road-stage.js`), never the library's cups and letters, clocks, wires and lanterns, or roads and dots. The page is named for the encounter. The grown-up notes keep the library's wording and open with one line on what the stage dresses (“On the road the wires are ropes …”).
- **A lamp tap shows its ropes.** A tap on a ferry lamp makes the ropes at that lamp glow for a moment. It is never a move: only a press on a rope flips lamps.
- **Phones get their own crop of each stage.** Each shows its own part of the picture, larger than the middle crop: the seats with anyone standing on the bow, the bow from the bell to the bottom of its wheels, the lamps and their ropes, and the boardwalks down to Hops's bank. Each crop keeps the pieces clear of the keeper's portrait. On a short phone the bell's stage is drawn narrower, so the goal and the whole chart fit under it.
- **The bell wheel shows the count alone.** The word “bells” beside it could not be drawn large enough to read, so it is gone.
- **The bell refuses in words.** A missing or too-large count gets a line in the feedback (“Tap a number first.”, “Choose 1 to 30 bells.”), not the browser's tooltip.
- **Wide, short screens put the scene beside the board.** On a tablet or laptop in landscape the scene and the keeper sit beside the board, so the board starts at the top; Plume's card sits under the keeper there. Stages keep their own layout.
- **One count of the child's moves.** Plume's card counts them, so the boardwalk stage no longer shows its own steps (it says how many lamps are lit), and Every stall hides its board's Distance line while the card shows. The lamps' "presses left" stays on the ferry and in the lantern boards: it is the board's limit, not the race.
- **In the Old Workshop, Plume's card moves left.** It sits in the scene's top-left corner, because the workshop art puts Sprocket in the top-right corner reserved for it. The art brief now asks for that corner to be kept clear; once it is, the card can return.

Design decisions, October 24 evening (Claude as product owner, before James's playtest), from a fresh replay of the fixed road:

- **The crypt door says "light".** Its positions are lights on every trail, as the board draws them (a lit lantern is 1): the goal "Each old try shows how many lights were right. Find the code.", the tap targets and their spoken names ("Light 2"), the hints and the feedback all say light. The table is headed "Old try" and "Right". After Check, K–1 sees only ✓ or ✗ beside each old try and one line under the table, "Change one light and try again."; 2–3 and 4–5 also see how many lights each old try gets right with their code. On a phone that line is scrolled into view. The library's code boards keep their own words (tests, exact matches, positions).
- **One noun per board.** Where a keeper's line and the board disagreed, the line changed, and two titles with it: Billie's balance is **Cable-car pebbles** ("One pebble's the wrong weight!"), the fair's booth says "One pebble's a fake!", Rattle lights "pumpkin lanterns", Sprocket's board is **Turntable cups**, Wick's "beacon bell" is stuck, and Mr. Hops asks for "every road".
- **Every lantern has a letter.** The 4–5 Kites (and the library's ten-lantern board, which is the same drawing) show A–J, in the board, its clash line and spoken names. Saved boards store colors in order, so old saves load unchanged.
- **A road's length sits beside it, and only when lengths differ.** On the stall boards (and every route board in the library) the number is placed off the road, away from the middle of the drawing; where every road has the same length no number is shown.
- **A tap that can't go anywhere says why.** A stop further along the map answers "That's further on!", and a junction no road reaches from where the walker stands answers in the board's words ("No boardwalk goes from A to C.", "You've walked the road from A to B."), in a short note by the finger. Neither is a move.
- **Smaller fixes.** The floor patch's Checker colors is a 44 px switch. A stop sheet shows a side puzzle still waiting for its tool as "Needs the pump" (or the chalk) with the tool's picture, not Play. The star proof's "More stars than partners" stays a plain "not yet" while too few stars are placed and turns red only when a placed star breaks the rule. A keeper's portrait shows the keeper's colour until the picture loads. Spring-water Jugs has the glyph ⊔. On the finished tall map the fair's stars sit above its landmark, clear of the medal key and the party wagon.

### Side puzzles and tools

Two optional side puzzles appear on the map as soon as their stop is reached, before the tool that solves them exists:

- **Snooze’s bath** (Turtle Ferry) is a pair of jugs that can only pour into each other, so the target is unreachable: pouring alone only trades between two states. After the **pump** from the Old Workshop, Fill and Empty appear and it can be solved. These are new instances: K–1 4/3 → 2, 2–3 5/3 → 4, 4–5 9/4 → 6. Each was checked to be impossible by pouring and solvable with the pump (5, 5 and 7 moves).
- **The other garden** (Glowworm Marsh) is an uncoverable garden. Before the **chalk** from Spooky Hollow, Prove it can’t is locked. After it, the garden can be proved. In 2–3 it is the 6×6 board, and the Checker shortcut is earned by the painted proof at the Hollow; one tap paints it, and the child still answers which color has more. In 4–5 it is the “shared partner” garden, where the colors balance, so paint cannot work and a star proof is needed after painting at the Hollow; saying the colors are the same brings Mr. Hops’s nudge toward stars.

Side puzzles never advance the road.

### Plume and stars

Each road puzzle gives up to three stars: **solved**, **no hints** (no Hint beyond the first, free nudge in this play; the rematch judges its winning run) and **beat or tied Plume**. The third star exists only where the family has a natural count:

| Family | Plume’s number counts | Best possible |
|---|---|---|
| Cup swaps | swaps | the minimum number of swaps |
| Lantern wires | presses | the fewest presses (search) |
| Jugs | fills, empties and pours | the fewest steps (search) |
| Tile gardens | placements and lifts | number of tiles |
| Routes | distance walked | the shortest walk over every road (search) |
| Latin squares | marks and erasures | number of blanks |
| Coloring | paints (each lantern colored or recolored) | number of lanterns |
| Clock, billiards, signal code | rings, launches, checks | 1 (predict, then act) |

Undo takes a move off the count. Road routes may walk a road more than once, and any walk that covers every road lights the lamps (up to three times the shortest), so the shortest walk is the count to beat: an Euler trail where one exists, otherwise the shortest walk that repeats as little as possible. Balances already fix their budget, and games and proofs have no count, so they top out at two stars. Plume’s lead shrinks along the road: optimum + 2 at the first two stops, + 1 in the middle and exactly optimal from the lighthouse on, where only a perfect solve ties. Prediction puzzles shrink the same way (3, 2, 1 tries). Plume’s number stays below a press budget, and where the fewest presses fill the budget there is no card (above). The best stars per encounter are kept, and replays can only raise them.

Plume waits at Windy Ridge for the pebble game (regular Nim, with Plume showing off while winning, as above; the library's copies answer with a winning move when there is one and a random move otherwise) and at the fair for the rematch against perfect play.

### Grade trails

Each grade trail (K–1, 2–3, 4–5) is its own run of the road with its own progress, stars and tools. Switching the puzzle level switches trails. A stop shows a pip for every trail that has lit it (bronze K–1, silver 2–3, gold 4–5, explained beside the star total), and the finale offers the next trail up.

### Around the road: Puzzles, Grown-ups and reading aloud

Design decisions, October 24, 2026 (Claude as product owner, before James's playtest), from the two play-through reviews:

- **Puzzles opens closed, with the road first.** Every family starts closed. A **From the road** shelf at the top holds the families of the boards this explorer has met on the road (any trail), in road order. Under **More puzzles** come the other base families in pack order, then the newer families, newest first. No family shows a solved/total count: AGENTS.md's rule against total/explored counters in puzzle selection is the organizer's standing decision, so it holds here too. Instead each puzzle this explorer has solved carries a ✓ on its button, the mark the road's solved strip gives "Solved" (stars stay the road's own, for its hints and Plume's scores). A family with grade groups (Tile gardens, Cup swaps) lists the explorer's grade first. No family carries a grade, and every family's Easy puzzles claim a K–1 start, so the band decides the order inside a family rather than hiding families. A child reaches a board in two taps: a family, then a puzzle. From October 24 evening, the Puzzles tab pressed from a library puzzle reopens that puzzle's family and scrolls to it: Back already did, and a home-screen app has no Back button, so the tab is a child's only way back to the shelf they were on. From anywhere else Puzzles still opens closed.
- **K–1 hear each puzzle.** The first time a puzzle screen opens in a visit, a K–1 explorer hears the keeper's line, then Plume's where Plume speaks, then the board's instruction: the goal above the board, or How to play's goal where the board shows its own. A solved board's line is read alone. Nothing is read on the map, when the page loads or reloads (browsers allow sound only after a tap), or on a screen already read in this visit. While a voice is heard, Listen and Read aloud show a stop square, are named "Stop reading", and stop it. Each reading first stops the last, so the app never speaks over itself. Grown-ups has the setting for each explorer: on by default for K–1, off for 2–3 and 4–5, and on wherever a grown-up turned on the old read-aloud setting. Listen is 44 px. With reduced motion, the keeper's talk pose stays a still. Checked in Chromium with a stand-in voice only: on an iPad, Safari may hold the first reading until the child taps Listen once.
- **Grown-ups starts with the explorers.** Each explorer's row shows each started trail (the stop reached and its number, or finished; stars; tools), when they last played, and the Puzzles library's counts, labelled as the library's. A copy imported beside an explorer with the same name is marked "(imported Oct 24)", with the time when two copies arrived the same day. The order is explorers and backups, offline status, puzzle notes, paper activities, then the Mathematics notes grouped by area, every area closed. No top-bar tab is current there. An address typed straight to `#parents`, or a reload there, shows the same gate as the Grown-ups button; Back to play or Escape goes to the road.
- **New explorer** opens with the cursor in the name field.
- **An update says so once, and waits.** A downloaded new version still waits, but a small banner on the map now says "A new version is ready." with Reload, and Grown-ups shows the same with its offline status. Reload asks the waiting copy to take over and reloads that tab. Nothing is shown on a puzzle, and another open tab keeps its page and board until it reloads. The first deploy that carries this still shows the old message, because the page that notices it is the old page.

## Screens

- **Header**: the explorer's name with the grade trail beside it (switching it switches trails), and three tabs: Road, Puzzles and Journal (none is current on Grown-ups). On a phone (700 px wide or less) the header is one row, so a puzzle starts near the top: the lantern, the three tabs and a round button with the explorer's initial. That button opens the explorer's row (name, trail and Grown-ups) under the bar until the page changes; Escape closes it too.
- **Map**: one picture of the road in two layouts, wide (1000:420) and tall for phones (600:900). It shows no place names. Each stop is a tappable region over its landmark (`MAP_REGIONS`), and the next stop's region glows, more strongly on hover. A reached region shows its stars and trail pips. The map also shows the travelers’ wagon at the current stop (at the ferry before the first puzzle), Plume’s wagon one stop ahead, side-puzzle badges with a lock until their tool, and the tools earned, star total and medal key in the top-right corner. A stop further along answers a tap with "That’s further on!". A finished stop opens a sheet listing its puzzles with stars and Replay, which starts a fresh attempt (a side puzzle still waiting for its tool says what it needs instead); once the road is finished the fair's sheet has a Finale button. Stops show a focus ring from the keyboard.
- **Road puzzle**: a scene shows the stop at its current stage (0–3 puzzles solved). The Turtle Ferry's three puzzles and the marsh boardwalks are instead played inside a **stage**, the stop seen from above and in front, with the pieces in the picture (`dist/road-stage.js`): the travelers sit in their seats on Snooze's deck and are tapped to swap (pair buttons below); the bell hangs at her bow over one or two big bell wheels, the bell count is tapped on a number chart below and the bell itself rings; the lamps stand on the deck's rim or a lamp tree and their ropes are tapped; Hops walks the boardwalks from junction to junction, lighting each walk's lamp. Travelers glide between seats. A tap on a lamp lights the ropes at it; each rope takes taps 44 px wide and lights up while pressed. Each stage fits the screen without scrolling; on a phone it shows its own crop of the picture (`phoneView`), and the seat tags, lamp letters, wheel numbers and platform letters read at 12 px or more. The keeper’s portrait overlaps its bottom edge, with the speech bubble beside it under the picture, so the art stays clear. Listen reads the line aloud; K–1 hear it, then the instruction, by themselves the first time (see above). Plume’s card shows where there is a score (in the stage's top-left corner, or under the keeper on a phone): the child's count beside Plume's, and Plume's boast on the first one. The board is below; on a wide, short screen the scene and keeper stand beside it instead. A side puzzle waiting for its tool shows what it needs where Hint would be. On a solve, a strip shows this play’s stars and what each was for (with both counts, and "Replay to win it" when a hint cost a star), any tool earned, Next and Replay. Dark stops darken the page around a light board.
- **Finale**: the fair picture, a goodbye from each keeper and Plume's concession (each with Listen), the trail's medal and any others won, the stars as earned of the most, the puzzles with stars still to win, then Puzzles, the next trail up and See the road.
- **Puzzles** and **Grown-ups**: see "Around the road" above. Grown-ups shows each explorer's road progress beside the library's counts.

Every picture is an art slot with a drawn placeholder (`dist/road-placeholders.js`) until finished art is marked ready in `dist/art/manifest.json` (`dist/art.js`, `dist/art-slots.js`).

## Saves

`profile.journey` is `{version: 4, trails: {k1|23|45: {started, completed: [main encounter IDs in order], side: [side IDs], stars: {encounterId: 1–3}}}}`. Road boards are campaign copies with IDs `road4-<encounter>-<band>`. They never share a save with the library. Tools are derived from `completed`, so they cannot be forged separately. Storage validates order, side puzzles (stop reached and tool earned), star ranges, a completed board for every completed encounter, and that every saved road board belongs to an encounter its trail has reached.

Earlier stories stay readable: a version-3 journey moves to `profile.roadJourney` (validated by the frozen `dist/caravan-road3.js`), alongside the version-1 `caravanJourney` and version-2 `rescueJourney`. Their boards stay in the catalog for validation and appear in the Journal under “Earlier …”.

## Verification

`npm test` plays all three trails to the fair and checks stars, Plume’s scores against each instance’s optimum (and the budget-capped boards), the race card and Plume's boast, the free first hint, side-puzzle locks, the exact chances against the showing-off Plume, the rematch's winning-run hints, the finale's totals and next trail, trail independence, forged saves and the archives. `npm run build` validates every non-proof road board by its hints. Proof boards are validated with their sources. `node scripts/road-browser-smoke.mjs` plays the road through the interface (K–1 on a phone, 2–3 on a tablet, 4–5 on a desktop), including the race count, the free first hint, the locked bath, the finale and the fair's sheet, then the Puzzles shelf on each trail and K–1 reading aloud, `node scripts/caravan-browser-smoke.mjs` checks its saves there, and `node scripts/road-stages-browser-smoke.mjs` (`TEST_PHONE=1` for the phones alone) checks the staged boards and scenes at 390×844, 375×667, 820×1180 and 1180×820. `TEST_FAMILY=road node scripts/layout-audit.mjs` measures the road's encounters (see [layout-audit.md](layout-audit.md)).

Not yet checked on an iPad, and no child has played it. Family playtesting should look at pacing, whether Plume’s scores feel fair, whether children notice the race count and the showing-off Plume at the Ridge, and whether the side puzzles are found.
