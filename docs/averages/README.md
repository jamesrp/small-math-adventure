# Sharing circles

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 20 of the Bellingham math circle (averaging and the maximum principle). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 20 worksheet is unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-20.md) says keep, and its **Decision** is what this family builds: inverse and design goals only (choose squares so a circle hits a target, change one square to move a circle, pick ends so every circle is whole, decide whether a circle can tie the biggest square), with the chase from a highest circle as the "can't" certificate, and no animated relaxation as the solve. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet problem or says it is new.

## The mathematics

A board is a small graph whose places are **squares**, which keep fixed numbers, and **circles**. A circle **works** when its number is the average of the numbers on every place joined to it. A **filling** gives every circle a number so that all circles work at once. This is the discrete Dirichlet problem, and a filling is a discrete harmonic function with the squares as its boundary.

- **One filling.** If every circle can reach a square along lines, there is exactly one filling (in real numbers). Every board in the puzzles has this property, and the validator checks it. The difference of two fillings is zero on the squares and also averages on the circles, so by the next fact its biggest and smallest values are on squares, where it is zero; so it is zero everywhere.
- **The maximum principle.** In a filling, every circle lies between the smallest and the biggest square its circle group touches (a circle group is the circles joined to each other through circles). Reason: take the biggest number in the group; on a circle it is the average of numbers no bigger than itself, so all of them equal it, and the equality spreads through the group until it reaches a square.
- **The equality case, and the chase.** Let M be the biggest square. A circle can equal M only if its whole circle group, and every square that group touches, equals M. So a circle given M in a board whose circle group touches a smaller square can never work with the rest: from that circle, each place joined to a circle at M must be M (none can be bigger, and the average must come out M), and the chain of forced places runs through circles until it reaches a square. A square below M breaks the chain. This chase is the certificate the child taps after **Can't** (puzzles 5, 8 and 11). When every square the group touches equals M, the filling does put the circle at M (puzzles 4 and 12).
- **Whole numbers can fail.** Whole-number squares can force fractions. On a path of L steps between whole ends A and B, the circles go up in equal steps of (B − A) ÷ L, so they are whole exactly when L divides B − A (puzzle 7: three steps from 0, so the right square is 0, 3, 6 or 9). Off a path the denominators come from the board: on puzzle 10's tree the circles are (2S + 8) ÷ 5 and (S + 24) ÷ 5, so the bottom square S must leave 1 on division by 5. The 5 is the determinant of the circles' equations.
- **Near and far squares.** Each circle is a weighted average of the squares, with weights that add to 1. On the path of puzzle 9 the circles are (2A + B) ÷ 3 and (A + 2B) ÷ 3, so the near square counts twice as much as the far one. The weights are the chances that a random walk from the circle first reaches each square (harmonic measure).
- **No squares.** A board of circles with no square has only constant fillings, by the same spreading argument (the playground's triangle).

What children are meant to discover by doing: a circle with one neighbour copies it; with two neighbours it is halfway, so a path goes in equal steps; you can't make a circle bigger than every square; a tie with the biggest square spreads and is stopped only by squares; and some boards have no whole-number filling. Experiments come first (set numbers, watch circles light), then conjectures (equal steps, multiples of 3, "the 0 is in the way"), then explanations, which here are the chase the child taps and the grown-up notes in each puzzle. The worksheet's written proofs (grades 2–3 Problems 3 and 5, grades 4–5 Problems 3 and 5) stay on paper.

Grade bands: puzzles 1 to 3 need sums to about 12 shared into two or three equal parts (the worksheet's K–1 Problems 3 and 4 and grades 2–3 Problem 1); puzzles 4 to 8 add two circles at once and one-step chases (grades 2–3 Problems 2 and 4); 9 to 12 are the grades 4–5 ideas (weights, divisibility off a path, a chase through a triangle of circles, separate circle groups). Sources: Doyle and Snell, *Random Walks and Electric Networks* (arXiv math/0001057), sections 1.1.5 and 1.2.2, as the worksheet guide cites.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Squares and circles on the graph board's lines. Given places are pine with light numerals; the child's places are paper, dashed while empty. A circle that works has a sun halo. The chosen place has a pine ring. The numbers sit in the shapes; there are no cubes.
- **Setting numbers.** Tap a free place to choose it, then tap a number in the row below the board. With a keyboard, Tab to a place and type a digit, or use the arrow keys to step its number up or down; focus stays on the board or the number row. Every circle lights the moment it works.
- **Find every** (puzzles 1, 2, 6, 7 and 10). A way is kept the moment the board makes it, as a small picture in a row under the board. Making a kept way again shows "Found already." and rings its picture. **That's all** is accepted only when every way is kept; otherwise it says "There is another." and stays off until a new way is made. Undo keeps the ways found and the refusal, so That's all can't be used to ask. There are no empty slots and no count. In puzzle 2 the number row is a set of cards: a card on one place leaves any other place.
- **Change one square** (puzzles 3 and 9). Both squares start with their numbers and look given until changed; the changed one turns sun. Changing the other square puts the first back. A circle's number is shown, so "grows by 2" is on the board as the new number.
- **Tie or Can't** (puzzles 4, 5, 8, 11 and 12). One circle is given the biggest square's number, in pine. Make every circle work, or press **Can't**. Can't is off until the child has put a number on the board. After Can't, the chase starts at the given circle: the places the child may tap next (joined to a circle already in the chase) are tinted, a tapped circle shows the top number in ochre italic ("this would have to be 8"), and a square stops that branch, with a pine ring if it agrees. Tapping a smaller square solves the puzzle, and the app then draws the whole argument: every circle of the group at the top number, every square it touches ringed (pine if it agrees, brick with a cross if smaller) and the path from the given circle to the square the child reached. Undo takes back chase taps. Pressing Can't again ends the chase and lets numbers change. On a board where the tie is possible the chase never meets a smaller square, and when it has reached every place it can, "Every square it reaches is 8." says so; that is the one refusal.
- **Copy.** Each puzzle shows one short objective, because the goal (all circles at once, every way, one change, the tie) is not on the board: "Find every way to make the circle work.", "Find every way to make every circle work.", "Find every way to make the circle work by changing one square." or "Make every circle work." How to play holds the full rule and the chase.
- **Hints.** Before any move the first hint is the authored question. Then in find-every and change puzzles the hints aim at the way not yet found that needs the fewest changes from the board, squares first ("Make the left square 3."), and say "Press That's all." when every way is kept. In a tie puzzle with a filling they fill it, and end a chase first ("Press Can't again."). In a tie puzzle without one they first make an attempt (a circle on the chase set to the top number), then give the reason with the claim ("Every place joined to an 8 circle must be 8 too, and the 0 square can't be. Press Can't."), then name each chase tap ("Tap the middle circle."). "Press Can't." is never the first hint. The second level marks the place on the board (and its number, once that place is chosen) or the button; the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after a wrong number, after a wrong That's all, after a wrong Can't and after random play with Undo.

### Why the child taps the chase

The certificate for Can't is a walk from the given circle through circles to a smaller square: a short list of places, each joined to the one before. The child can point at it, so a bare Can't is not a solve; the child taps the walk, and each tap is a step of the argument (this circle would have to be 8 too). The full argument, the whole circle group and every square it touches, is drawn by the app only after the child has reached a smaller square. A Can't press is never refused on its own, so it gives nothing away: on a possible board the chase simply runs out of places.

## The playground

Four boards, chosen with picture buttons: a path (0 and 12 squares, three circles), the grades 2–3 tree (0, 6 and 8), a diamond (2 and 12 squares, two circles joined to both and to each other) and a triangle of three circles with no square. Every place is free, squares too, and circles light as they work. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. On the triangle only equal numbers work, the K–1 Problem 6 and 7 idea.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the free places' numbers, plus the ways kept, the refusal and the done flag (find every and change), or the chase (tie), or the board chosen (playground). Which circles work, "Found already." and the drawn argument are derived from it. The chosen place is view state outside the save.
- Only legal moves are accepted: whole numbers in the puzzle's range on free places, a card on one place at a time, one changed square at a time, That's all only in find-every and change puzzles and not again before a new way, Can't only after an attempt and not during a chase, chase taps only on places joined to a circle in the chase, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: every number whole and in range on a free place; cards different; at most one square changed and none empty; every kept way a real way, kept once, and the board's way kept if it makes one; "Found already." only for the way on the board; "There is another." only while a way is missing; done only with every way kept; a chase only after an attempt, from the given circle, each place joined to a circle before it, and ending at the first smaller square.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-averages.mjs` with the mechanic's exact solve and checked again by `scripts/validate-averages.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch: whether children find the tap-a-place, tap-a-number pattern and read the sun halo as "this circle works", or fix one circle at a time and break the other on the two-circle boards (puzzles 6, 7, 9 and 10); whether the chase reads as an argument (each ochre number "would have to be 8") or as tapping until something turns red, and whether children press Can't on puzzles 4 and 12, where it is wrong, and make sense of "Every square it reaches is 8."; and whether find-every puzzles get organized (sorting by the smallest square in puzzle 1) or That's all is pressed as a guess, and whether That's all staying off after "There is another." is clear.
- **Cubes.** The card's App fit has a child tap a circle to raise or lower its stack; the app shows numerals and a number row instead. K–1 children who share cubes on the worksheet's mats may need a stack view.
- **Fractions.** Whole numbers only: a filling that needs fractions is simply not a way. The card asks to keep fractions to a clearly marked mode; there is none yet, so the grades 4–5 Problem 7 fraction boards and cards stay on paper.
- **No relaxation.** By the Decision there is no animation of values settling and no run button, in the puzzles or the playground.
- **Not ported.** K–1 Problems 1, 2 and 5 (building circle stacks, linked circles, the 5-cube impossibility), grades 2–3 Problems 3, 5 and 6 and grades 4–5 Problems 1, 3, 5 and 6 (no-peak and uniqueness questions and their proofs, counting constant fillings), and the bonus packet. Puzzle 11's Can't is the 4–5 Problem 2 question in tie form.

- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
