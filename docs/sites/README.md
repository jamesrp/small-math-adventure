# Nearest dots

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 15 of the Bellingham math circle (nearest-site regions). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 15 worksheet's printed packets are unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-15.md) says keep, and its decision of October 10 shaped this family: a new family of nearest-site regions with dots on a half-square grid so that every edge is exact; make a target region within a dot budget, touch some regions and avoid another, and take a place while keeping others; the child's segment through R as the certificate for "can't", and a checked "can't with fewer" beside each fewest-dots witness; regions hidden until Check, and no question asking which dot owns a place.

## The mathematics

A **place** belongs to every dot nearest to it, measured by straight-line distance; a place just as near to two or more dots belongs to all of them. A dot's **region** is the closed set of its places (its Voronoi cell). Everything below is for dots at distinct points of the plane.

- **Two dots: the halfway line.** The places just as near to s as to k form the perpendicular bisector of s and k, and the places at least as near to s form the closed half-plane 2(k − s)·z ≤ |k|² − |s|². So a region is the intersection of one half-plane for each other dot: it contains its dot, has **no dents** (it is convex), has straight sides, and may run on forever. Each side lies on the halfway line between the region's dot and one other dot. Two regions **touch** when they share a place, even a single point; a corner where three or more regions meet is a place three or more dots share, the centre of a circle through those dots with no dot inside. Adding a dot adds one more half-plane to every old region, so it can only take places away.
- **Making a region (the mirror construction).** Let T be a region with straight sides and no dents, holding the dot A inside. Put a new dot at A's mirror image across each side of T. Each side is then the halfway line between A and its new dot, so A's region is exactly T. Conversely, each side of A's region lies on the halfway line between A and some other dot, and a line is the halfway line between A and only one point, A's mirror image across it. So when T has k sides and no other dot is present, the k mirror images are needed, and with a budget of k they are the only answer (puzzles 1–4 and 9). On the half-square grid a mirror image is not always a grid point: the worksheet's triangle in grades 4–5 Problem 7 needs dots at (±24/13, 16/13), so puzzle 9 uses a triangle whose mirror images land on the grid.
- **Taking a place (no dents).** If two places belong to A, so does every place on the segment between them, because each half-plane holds the whole segment. So when R lies on the segment between two places A must keep (A itself counts), no dots can take R from A: that segment is the certificate (puzzle 10). More generally A's region holds the whole shape spanned by A and the places it keeps (a segment or a triangle here). A place outside that shape can be cut off from it by a line, and a dot at A's mirror image across the line takes the place and leaves the shape to A (puzzles 7 and 11); in the app that dot must also land on the grid inside the frame, which the answers below check. The puzzles use only segments as certificates; a place inside the triangle A, P, Q but off its sides would need the triangle, and no puzzle asks for it.
- **Closing a region (the fewest dots).** A region is closed in (bounded) only if it has at least three sides: one half-plane runs off, two give a strip, a wedge or a smaller half-plane, all running off. Each side needs its own other dot, so A alone needs at least three new dots, and three around A close it (puzzle 8). B between A and C on a line already has the two parallel sides of a strip; one more half-plane cuts the strip off at one end only, so B needs two new dots (puzzle 12, the worksheet's grades 4–5 Problem 6). The app also checks every placement of fewer dots on its grid.
- **Four at once.** Four dots on one circle with no dot inside share its centre. With A, B and C each 2 squares from the middle of the board (grades 2–3 Problem 2), the only free grid point 2 squares from the middle is opposite C (puzzle 5). The same place counts as touching: in puzzle 6, D there touches C at that single point.

**What children are meant to discover.** Experiments come first: put dots down, press Check, and see the regions. The conjectures are that the edge is halfway between two dots, that a new dot goes as far past an edge as the region's dot is before it (a mirror image), that a place between two kept places can't be taken, and that a closed region needs three dots around it (or two more for a dot between two others). The explanations (equal distances on the halfway line, a region has no dents, a closed shape needs three sides) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes. The worksheet's written explanations stay on paper.

**Grade bands.** The puzzles are grade-free. As a rough map: puzzles 1–3 match the K–1 packet's strip (Problem 8) and need no reading with a grown-up reading the goal; 4–7 match grades 2–3 (a slanted edge, four at once, touch and avoid); 8–12 match grades 4–5 (fewest dots, the triangle, taking a place). The playground fits every band.

These are **Voronoi diagrams** (David M. Mount, *CMSC 754 Computational Geometry*, Lecture 10, pp. 1–3: the definition, cells as intersections of half-planes, convexity, and vertices as centres of empty circles).

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Graph paper with the frame drawn in ink and half-square lines faint; the board shows half a square of shaded margin past the frame, and regions are drawn out to the board's edge, so a region that runs on past the frame is seen running off (the map is cropped, not panned). Fixed dots are discs in sun, sky and rose with their letter; new dots are small ink discs (D in puzzles 5 and 6 is labelled D). Marked places in the take puzzles are crosses with an italic letter. In the make puzzles the target is shaded with a dashed edge.
- **Moves.** Tap where grid lines cross inside the frame to put a dot there; tap a dot to take it away; drag a dot to move it (one move, one Undo). Slots under the board show how many new dots are left, and a tap when they are full shakes them instead of adding a dot. A mouse shows a ring where a dot would go. With a keyboard, the arrow keys move a ring, Enter or Space puts down, picks up or drops a dot (the first press only shows the ring), Delete or Backspace takes one away, and Escape drops a dot held.
- **Check.** Nothing about the regions is shown until **Check**: then every region is tinted (A, B, C in their colours, D in leaf green, other new dots grey), every edge is drawn in ink, a small ring marks each place three dots share and a larger pine ring each place four share. The region the puzzle is about is outlined in pine when it is right and in ink when not; in puzzle 6 a contact with C's region is drawn in ochre; in the take puzzles each marked place gets a pine ring when it is right and a dashed ochre ring when not. Any change (a dot put down, moved or taken away) hides the regions again, and they are hidden while a dot is dragged, so a dot can't be wiggled until an edge lines up. The make, touch, four and take goals are solved at the Check that shows them right.
- **Can't** (take puzzles). Can't is greyed until there are dots on the board and Check has shown them: a claim is drawn round the child's own try, and a fresh board's Can't does nothing. Press Can't (it stays pressed) and tap two of A and the places A keeps; rings show where to tap (with a keyboard, Tab reaches them and Enter or Space taps). If the place to take is on the segment between them, the puzzle is solved: the segment is drawn in pine over the try's map, with R ringed. Otherwise the segment is drawn dashed in ochre with "Misses R.", and the same pair waits until something changes. A refusal says only whether R is on that segment, which the board already shows, so it is not a yes/no oracle about the puzzle and needs no lock; a right claim always needs the certificate.
- **Fewest** (fit puzzles). Fewest is greyed until Check shows the region inside the frame. With the fewest dots it solves the puzzle and draws the argument: each side's whole halfway line, dashed in pine, and each side's dot joined to the region's dot, so the sides can be counted. With more, "Fewer dots can." appears and Fewest stays greyed for that many dots or more. Undo takes back moves but keeps that answer (the `carry` hook), so pressing Fewest and undoing can't be used to ask whether a count is the fewest.
- **Why the app draws the Fewest argument.** "No fewer dots can do it" is a statement about every placement of fewer dots; nothing on the board can be tapped to certify it. The child supplies the tappable half, a witness with that many dots; the argument (a closed region needs three sides, each with its own dot, and parallel sides need two more) is drawn by the app after a right claim and explained in the grown-up notes.
- **Copy.** Each puzzle shows one short objective above the board: "Make A's region exactly the shaded part." (1–4, 9), "Make one place belong to four dots." (5), "Make D's region touch A's and B's, but not C's." (6), "Take S from A, but keep P and Q with A." (7; R in 10, P alone in 11) and "Fit A's whole region in the frame, with the fewest dots." (8; B in 12). How to play gives the rules: every place belongs to the nearest dot and ties belong to all; a region is all its places and the map goes on past the frame; new dots go where grid lines cross inside the frame and regions stay hidden until Check; plus one line for each kind (exactly the shaded part everywhere; touching includes a single point; rings mark shared places; Can't after a Check with two places that show why; Fewest).
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, which never names the answer. Then hints say what to do in words measured from a named dot: "Put a dot 2 squares right of A.", "Put D 4 squares below C.", "Move the dot to the spot 2 squares right of A.", "Take away the dot 2 squares below A.", "Press Check." and "Press Fewest.". In puzzle 10 they lead to a try first ("Put a dot on R.", "Press Check.") and then give the reason with Can't: "R is on the straight line from P to Q. Press Can't and tap P and Q."; "Press Can't." is never the first hint. In the fit puzzles they keep as many of the child's dots as they can. The second level rings the spots or the button on the board, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after a wrong dot, after random play, after a refused Can't and after a refused Fewest. Each hint is remembered per board, and the slowest (the first in puzzle 5, cold) takes about 15–40 ms on a laptop.
- **Focus.** After a move, focus lands on the board (or, after a solve, on the completion heading); Check, Fewest and the Can't picks go grey or away after use, so focus does not stay on them. It never lands on Can't.

## The playground

Two dots to start, at (−1.5, −1) and (1.5, 1) as in K–1 Problem 2, and up to ten, each with a letter and a colour. Regions are always shown and are redrawn while a dot is dragged. There is no goal, no Check and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. Its notes suggest a third dot between two, four at the corners of a square, and the question whether adding a dot can ever give an old dot more places (grades 4–5 Problem 5: no).

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the new dots, sorted, and whether Check has been pressed since the last change; in the take puzzles also the right claim or the refused pair; in the fit puzzles also the done flag and the smallest count at which Fewest was refused; in the playground, the dots in the order they were put down. Everything shown is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a dot on a free grid point inside the frame within the budget (not on a fixed dot or another new dot), a move to a free grid point, taking away a dot that is there, Check once per change, Can't only in the take puzzles, after a checked try, with two different places and not the pair just refused, Fewest only in the fit puzzles after a Check that shows the region in the frame and below a refused count, and nothing after a solve. In the playground, at most ten dots and no Check.
- Saved boards are checked: whole-number points in half-squares inside the frame, no doubles, none on a fixed dot, sorted, within the budget; a claim only of the certifying pair and only with a checked try, a refusal only of a pair that does not certify and only with a checked try, never both; done only with the fewest dots checked and fitting; a refused count above the fewest and within the budget; no unknown fields.
- **Exact arithmetic.** Points are stored in half-squares, so every dot and place has whole-number coordinates. A half-plane is a·u + b·v ≤ c with whole numbers, and a corner is a triple of whole numbers (X, Y, D) meaning (X/D, Y/D); with dots within 6 half-squares of the middle, every number stays far below 2⁵³, so every edge, tie, touch and fit is exact (`dist/families/sites/plane.js`).
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-sites.mjs` with the mechanic's geometry (corners among every pair of lines) and checked again by `scripts/validate-sites.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Three things to watch: whether hiding the regions until Check leads children to predict an edge before pressing it, or to press Check after every dot (if Check becomes a reflex, a short "Where will the edge be?" moment, or limiting free Checks, may help); whether children see a mirror image (as far past the edge as A is before it) or keep nudging a dot toward the shaded edge itself, as puzzle 1's notes expect; and whether Can't in puzzle 10 is found by reasoning about the segment, or by trying pairs until one is accepted (there are only three pairs, so the try and the drawn segment carry the argument; watch whether the segment is understood).
- **Tap size on a phone.** At 375 px wide, grid points are about 20 px apart (half a square); taps land on the nearest crossing, and a drag can fine-tune, but small fingers may need a zoomed board or a larger half-square grid.
- **Fewest without a certificate to tap.** The "can't with fewer" side is drawn by the app after a right Fewest, not supplied by the child (see How a puzzle plays). A tappable version (tap the sides of the region and count them) is possible but not built.
- **Triangles as certificates.** Can't takes a pair of places; a place inside the triangle A, P, Q (off its sides) would need all three, and no puzzle asks for it.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines or art.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail. The worksheet's drawing tasks (draw the regions of given dots, explain why a boundary works for every point) and its "which dot is nearest to each cross" warm-ups stay on paper, as the review card decided.
