# Stretch bands

October 10, 2026. Ten puzzles and a playground built from Week 22 of the Bellingham math circle (meeting regions: four-point convex partitions). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-22.md) says keep, and its Decision is what this family builds: a short set on a lattice where lines and coincidences are exact; find every split that meets and declare "That's all"; place D so that a named split is the only one, or exactly two or four meet; tap every place C can go; build two boards with no common split. It is not a colour-the-dots tap-through: in the puzzles the app draws a split's bands only after the child claims it meets (or as a certificate), never live while colouring. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet source.

## The mathematics

Labelled dots A, B, C (and D) sit on points of a square lattice; two letters may share a point. A **split** puts the letters in two non-empty groups, yellow and blue; swapping the colours gives the same split, so four letters have 7 splits and three have 3. A group's **band** is the convex hull of its dots: a dot, a segment or a filled triangle. A split **meets** when the two bands share a point; touching counts.

- **Radon's theorem in the plane.** Every four dots have a split that meets, coincident and collinear dots included. Three dots have one only when they are on one line, so four is the sharp number (puzzle 6, whose crosses each draw three dots' splits apart; the guide's proof is on its p. 18).
- **How many meet.** With four different points and no three on a line, exactly one split meets: one dot inside the triangle of the other three (that dot alone), or a convex quadrilateral whose diagonals cross (the diagonal pairs; puzzle 3). With exactly one triple on a line, two meet: the middle dot of the triple alone, and the two ends against the middle dot with the fourth. Four on a line give four: each middle dot alone, and the interleaved and nested pairings. Two letters on one point give at least four: every split that puts them apart. Over all placements of four labels the count is 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 or 7, never 0 or 3.
- **A fixed triangle.** With A, B and C fixed and not on a line, D makes 1, 2 or 4 splits meet: 1 in general position, 2 on a line through two of them, 4 only on top of one of them, where the four are the splits that put D and that letter apart (puzzles 2, 5, 7 and 9; the review card's `card_app_check.py` finds the same).
- **Three dots and a line.** With A and B fixed, C has a split that meets exactly when C is on the line through A and B, between them or past either one (puzzle 6). On the lattice the line through (2, 1) and (6, 3) climbs 1 for every 2 across, so it meets the lattice only every 2 across.
- **Two boards.** Every board has a split that meets, but two boards need not share one (2–3 Problem 4). Four dots on a line take four splits; after a single move at least three are still on the line and the moved board still shares one of the four (the validator tries every single move), so two boards that start on the same line need two moves (puzzle 10).

Experiments come first: colour a split, picture its bands, shelve it with Meet, and see the bands when That's all judges the shelf. The conjectures (four always work, three on a triangle never, one in general position, two or four in special positions) come from finding every split on several boards and from placing D. The explanations (inside or crossing, the middle dot of a line, letters on one place, the case proof) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes; the worksheet's written proofs stay on paper.

**Why a lattice.** Every coordinate is a whole number, so whether a dot is on a line, a segment or an edge is decided by integer cross products with no rounding. The fixed triangle (1, 1), (5, 1), (1, 5) has every side a multiple of 4 across and up, so wherever D goes on its board, two bands that miss are at least 0.6 apart, and a band never seems to touch a disc it misses (the review card's pitfall "thick strokes faking contact"). On the every-split boards bands that miss are at least 0.8 apart; the validator checks both.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Each letter is a lettered disc on a lattice point, cream until it has a colour. Two letters on one point sit side by side (three in a triangle, four in a square), and each one's tap circle stops halfway to its neighbour, so a tap goes to the disc it lands on. A band is a filled shape, a thick segment or a ring round a lone dot, in its group's colour; where the two bands of a split meet, a pine ring marks the shared point (a pine line marks a shared stretch). Two bands on one line are drawn a little apart so both show.
- **Find every** (puzzles 1, 3, 4 and 8). Tap a letter to switch it between yellow and blue (Enter or Space on a focused disc does the same). No band is drawn on the board. **Meet** puts the current split on the shelf below as a chip of its two colour groups, with no verdict; a split already there gives "Already on the shelf.". Tapping a chip takes it off the shelf. **That's all** judges the whole shelf: if every split on it meets and none is missing, the puzzle is solved and the shelf is drawn with its bands; otherwise one refusal, either the first shelved split that misses, ringed on the shelf and drawn apart ("This one doesn't meet."), or, if all meet, "There is another.". The refusal is saved and That's all stays disabled until the shelf changes. There are no empty slots and no count.
- **Place D** (puzzles 2, 5, 7 and 9). The goal is one or more splits shown as chips, yellow group then blue. Tap any place on the board to move D there (D may sit on another letter's place: tap that letter), or use the arrow keys. **Check** is accepted when the goal's splits meet and no other split does. Otherwise the board draws one split that breaks the rule: a goal split that misses ("This one doesn't meet.", its chip ringed) or an extra split that meets ("This one meets too."). Check then waits for a move.
- **Every place for C** (puzzle 6). Tap a free place to put C there. If some split of A, B and C meets, the place keeps a pine ring and the split is drawn; if none does, it gets a cross, "No split meets." appears and the three splits are drawn apart in small pictures. **That's all** comes after the first find, says "There is another." while a place is missing, and then waits for a new find.
- **Two boards** (puzzle 10). Each board has its own A, B, C and D, one to a place. Tap a letter to choose it on its board (it gets a pine ring), then a free place on the same board to move it; arrow keys move the chosen letter. **Check** is accepted when no split meets on both boards; otherwise "This split meets on both." appears and that split is drawn on both boards. After the solve each board lists its own splits.
- **Copy.** Every puzzle shows one short objective, because the board alone can't say which claim is asked: "Find every split whose bands meet.", "Move D so only this split meets." (or "these splits"), "Find every place for C where a split meets." and "Move dots so no split meets on both boards.". How to play adds the rules and three small pictures of the kinds of band.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then: in find-every puzzles, a shelved split that misses to take off ("Take ACD and B off the shelf."), the letter to switch toward the nearest split still to shelve ("Make B blue."), "Press Meet." and "Press That's all.". In place puzzles, where D goes in words ("Put D inside the triangle of A, B and C.") and "Press Check."; for every place for C, the next place on the line ("Put C on the line through A and B, past B."); for two boards, the letter and board to move, from a search for the shortest fix ("Move A on the second board."). The second level marks the disc, chip, place or button, and the third offers Apply hint (which moves a letter without changing the one the child has chosen). Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after wrong moves and claims, and from random states.

## Certificates and claims

Every claim is checked, and Wave 5's rules apply as follows.

- **A wrong claim gets one short refusal, and the certificate is drawn round the child's own attempt.** A refused That's all draws the child's own shelved split that misses apart, at least 200px wide with thin bands so the narrowest gap is at least 4px (the validator measures every one); a refused Check draws one split of the child's own placement that breaks the goal; a place for C that fails draws its three splits apart at the same size; a refused Check on two boards draws the shared split on both.
- **Why the app draws these rather than the child tapping them.** There is no Can't in this family: every goal can be reached. The negative facts are about all splits at once (That's all is a case check over seven splits; "no split meets" for three dots is three facts at once), or about the gap between two bands, which is not a thing that can be pointed at. The child's part is the attempt: a colouring, a placement, a place for C. That's all, with no count and no slot per answer, is the claim the child makes; the app answers it with "There is another." and nothing more, so it cannot be used to locate the missing split.
- **That's all comes after an attempt.** It is disabled while the shelf is empty (or before the first find of a place for C), so a fresh board solves nothing. Meet answers nothing, so colouring and pressing Meet in turn cannot settle a board: a shelf holding all seven splits is refused, naming the first that misses.
- **Every claim can fail.** Each claim is one button (That's all on the shelf, Check) judged at once against the board, and nothing tints, rings or focuses the taps that would make it right: places and letters look alike until a hint is asked for at the second level, and rings and crosses only record places already tried. A wrong claim gets one short refusal, kept in the save.
- **Undo never escapes a claim.** Undo takes back colours, moves and shelving, but keeps a refused That's all while the shelf is the same, a refused Check while the board is the same, and the places for C found and crossed with "There is another." (That's all stays disabled until a new find). A kept refusal leaves the claim press nothing of its own to show, so one Undo right after a refusal also takes back the move before it.
- **Goals don't give the answer away.** No title, objective or first hint names a place; the goal chips name the splits, which is the task, not its answer.

## The playground

The 6 × 6 board with four letters, two yellow and two blue. Tap a letter to switch its colour; the bands are drawn live, with the pine mark when they meet. **Move** switches to moving: tap a letter, then a free place (or use the arrow keys); press Move again to colour. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. It is the worksheet's game: one player places the dots, the other looks for a split that meets, and the placer cannot win.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the colouring, the shelf, "Already on the shelf.", the refusal (the split shown apart, or "There is another.") and done (find every); D's place, a refusal and done (place); the places found and crossed, C's place, "There is another." and done (every place for C); both boards' letters, a refusal and done (two boards); the letters and colours (playground). Everything drawn is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a known letter, a lattice point on the board, D not onto its own place, C not onto A, B or its own place, letters one to a place on the two boards and in the playground, Meet only on a two-colour split and not twice in a row, a chip taken off only when it is on the shelf, That's all only on a shelf and not twice on the same shelf, Check not twice without a move, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: colourings of 0s and 1s for every letter; a shelf of real splits, each once; "Already on the shelf." only for the current colouring when it is on the shelf; a refusal only as That's all would give it on that shelf (the first split that misses, else "There is another." while one is missing); done only with exactly the splits that meet; a refusal only in a wrong place and done only in a right one; places found that work and crosses that don't, each once, none on A or B, with C on one of them; two boards of four letters on four different places, a refusal only when they share a split and done only when they don't.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-radon.mjs` with the mechanic's integer hull tests and checked against the table above, and again by `scripts/validate-radon.mjs` by linear programming.
