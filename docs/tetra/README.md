# Mirror twins

October 17, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 37 of the Bellingham math circle (tetrahedron chirality). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. No children have played them yet, and the Week 37 worksheet is an unpiloted prototype. The [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-37.md) shaped this family through its App fit and its Decision of October 10, 2026. The model is a flat view from corner A, and a tap on a corner turns the model a third of the way round it. The solves are turning to match, merging letters, placing letters so a pair matches or can't, and sorting into turn-to-match groups. A sort ends with a That's all that the app checks, with no class count shown, and then a Can't certificate for the two stacks that have the same letters. "Can't match" is a claim the app checks against the two views from A. The worksheet packet has not changed since the card. The printed models are used in puzzles 1–5, 7 and 10. The other puzzles are new or come from the worksheet's questions; each puzzle's `provenance` says which.

## The mathematics

A **model** is a rigid regular tetrahedron with a letter on each of its four corners. Two models **match** when a rotation carries one onto the other letter for letter. Rotations count; reflections do not. Which way a letter faces does not count either.

- **The turns.** A tap on a corner turns the model 120° about the axis through that corner and the centre of the opposite face. The tapped corner stays where it is, and the other three move one place round it, clockwise as seen from outside looking at that corner. The four turns generate the rotation group of the tetrahedron. That group has 12 elements, and they are exactly the even permutations of the four corners (A₄). So taps reach all 12 poses, each within three taps, and they never reach a reflection: a reflection swaps two corners and fixes two, which is an odd permutation. The validator checks this by building the solid from alternate corners of a cube and turning it by Rodrigues' formula. It finds twelve matrices, each of determinant +1, inducing exactly the 12 even permutations.
- **Four different letters: two classes of twelve.** There are 24 ways to put A, B, C and D on the corners. Each model looks 12 different ways, so the 24 labellings fall into exactly two classes of 12, and each class is the mirror image of the other. Two models with the same four letters match exactly when the permutation taking one to the other is even. A **mirror twin** is a model's reflection, for example a copy with two letters swapped. It never matches the model.
- **The certificate: the view from one corner.** Put the same letter, say A, in the middle of both models. That letter is then nearest the eye, and the other three letters make the triangle behind it. On one class B, C and D run clockwise round A, and on the other class they run anticlockwise. A turn that matches the models must put A on A. That leaves only the three turns about the middle (none, one tap or two), and each of them keeps the way round. So if the three letters run opposite ways on the two models, no turn can match them. This is the 4–5 Problem 3 argument, and it is complete: A has to go on A, and all three turns about A fail. It also works the other way. With four different letters and the same letter in the middle of both models, the arrows run opposite ways exactly when the models can't match. The validator checks this on all 864 traced pairs.
- **Two equal letters: always a match.** Make any two letters equal and the model becomes its own mirror image. The mirror through the other two corners and the midpoint of the equal pair's edge swaps the equal corners and changes nothing. Two reflections make a rotation, so the twin, which is the model reflected, is also the model turned. So every one of the six merges lets the twins match (puzzles 4, 6 and 8). All 12 arrangements of A, A, B and C match each other (puzzles 5, 7 and 12), and so do the arrangements of any other set of letters with a repeat. The validator checks every pair of the 256 labellings with letters A to D.
- **Restoring a fourth letter can land in either class.** Change one A on each of two matching A, A, B, C models to D. If the two changed As meet when the models match, the new pair still matches. Otherwise the result is a pair of mirror twins (puzzles 7 and 10).
- **What is not claimed.** Nothing here is about chirality beyond rotation classes of labellings. The organic-chemistry connection in the grown-up notes is an analogy: a carbon atom with four different groups is chiral in the same way.

Children should experiment first. They turn the models to match, try a Can't, merge two letters, place letters and sort. Their conjectures come from that work. One is that the twins never match whatever you do. Others are that making two letters the same always fixes it, that two As always match, and that there are only two kinds of A, B, C, D model. The explanations are the view from A, which is the Can't certificate, and the mirror through two equal corners. Grown-ups can take these up in each puzzle's notes. The worksheet's counting question (4–5 Problem 5, the 12 rotations of a bare frame) and its written explanations stay on paper.

Rough bands: puzzles 1–4 suit K–1 with a grown-up reading How to play. Puzzles 5–8 suit grades 2–3. Puzzles 9–12 suit grades 4–5. The puzzles are grade-free, and these bands are entry points, not ceilings.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Each model is a triangle with a corner in the middle, three paper faces and ink edges, and a lettered disc on each corner. The other model sits on a sun card. Yours is plain paper, and its four discs are buttons. A tap on a disc, or Enter or Space on a focused one, turns your model a third of the way round that corner. The letters then glide round the axis while a wire frame of the solid turns with them (460 ms; prefers-reduced-motion turns this off). Discs never overlap, and a tap goes to the disc it lands on. The faces between the discs take no taps. Can't's arrows are drawn on a layer above the discs, in blue on a pale halo, so neither the discs nor the ink edges hide them.
- **Turn to match** (puzzles 1–5). The puzzle is solved the moment every corner shows the same letter as the other model. The turning puzzles show no objective: the two models and the tappable corners say it. Puzzles 2–5 also have **Can't**.
- **Can't** (puzzles 2–5, 9 and 10, and the end of 11 and 12). The button is off until the child has turned the model at least once. Pressing it starts tracing. On each model the child taps two of the three outer letters. The first tap marks where an arrow starts, and the second draws a blue arrow round the middle, from the first letter through the second. A tap on the start takes the arrow back, and a tap on a model that already has an arrow starts that arrow again. While tracing, the last message is hidden. The claim is judged when both arrows are in:
  - *Different middle letters* gives "Not the same letter in the middle." This refusal is about the claim's form, not the pair. It clears the taps and locks nothing.
  - *Different letters traced* (the same two letters must be traced in the same order on both) gives "Follow the same letters on both.". This is also a form refusal, and it also locks nothing.
  - *Arrows running the same way* gives "Same way round.". The arrows stay on both models, dashed ochre, until the next move.
  - *Opposite arrows on a pair that matches anyway* (only possible with a repeated letter) gives "There is a way.".
  - *Opposite arrows on twins* solves the puzzle, and both blue arrows stay.

  The last two refusals are wrong claims. The letters they were made on are saved as locked, and Can't stays off whenever both models show those letters again: after turns, Undo, Start again or Restart, and after a letter is taken off and put back.
- **One change** (puzzle 6). Letter chips under the models: tap the letter to change, and it lifts with a pine ring. Then tap the letter it becomes. The change happens on both models at once, the chips go, and taps then turn your model. Undo takes the change back.
- **Set-ups** (puzzles 7, 9 and 10). The letters still to put on wait under the model as small stickers. In puzzles 7 and 10, the As of both models are buttons until each model has had one A changed to D. In puzzle 9, your model's four corners are blank, and taps put on A, B, C and D in that order. Turns and Can't open only when the set-up is done. **Start again** (off until a letter is on) puts the set-up back as it was; it is a move, so Undo takes it back. Puzzle 7 is solved by turning to a match. Puzzles 9 and 10 are solved only by Can't: a set-up alone, or a pair that matches, is not a solve. The app never says whether a set-up can match: the child finds out by turning, and nothing is shown until they press Hint.
- **Which pairs?** (puzzle 8). The chips work as in puzzle 6. When your model matches after a change, that pair of letters (B = C, for example) goes on the shelf, marked in pine until the next move, and both models start again from the twins. A pair already on the shelf gives "Found already.", and its shelf chip pops. **That's all** is off while the shelf is empty. It judges the whole shelf once. With a pair still missing it says "There is another." and stays off until the shelf grows. With all six, the puzzle is solved. Nothing counts the pairs.
- **Sorting** (puzzles 11 and 12). Every model is shown small, and one is on the big board. Tap a small model to put it on the big board, then tap the big board's corners to turn it. Small models that show the same letter in every place stack together automatically and glide into the stack. That's all opens after a turn. With two stacks that could still be turned to match, it says "Some can still match." and stays off while the stacks are the same. Neither the board nor the refusal shows a count of classes or stacks. A right That's all asks for a certificate: "Show with Can't that these two can't match." A model from each of the two stacks with the same letters comes up as a pair, the first on the sun card and a copy of the second that turns. The stacks stay below, with those two marked, and no longer change. Turning the copy never changes its stack. Can't then works as above; its right claim solves the sort. Stacks with different letters can never match and need no trace, so puzzle 12's A, A, B, C stack is never asked for. Undo from the proof goes back to the sort.
- **Copy.** Puzzles 1–5 show no objective. The others show one sentence, because the board alone can't say what is asked: "Make two letters the same, then turn to match.", "Change one A on each to D so they match." (or "so they can't match."), "Put on A, B, C and D so the two can't match.", "Find every pair of letters that, made the same, lets the models match." and "Turn models so the ones that match stack together.". How to play opens with what a model is and what matching means, and then describes the taps. It states rules, never the theorem.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. None of the first hints is Can't or That's all. After that the hints go step by step:
  - turning puzzles: the corner to tap, as "Tap the top corner.";
  - set-ups: where the next letter goes, as "On the left model, change the A in the middle to D." or "Put B at the top.", towards a set-up that reaches the goal while one is left, and otherwise to the first place the letter can go;
  - a change: "Change D to C.";
  - which pairs: the next missing pair, its taps, then "Press That's all.";
  - sorts: which small model to turn to look like which, by its letters, as "Turn the model with B in the middle to look like the model with D at the top: tap its middle.", then "Press That's all.", then the proof's turns and claim.

  For a pair that can't match, the chain first brings the same letter to the middle with at least one tap, so an attempt comes first. Then it gives the reason before naming Can't: "With A in the middle of both, B, C and D go round opposite ways, and a turn never changes that. Press Can't, then tap B and then C on each." No hint is a dead end, so the app never volunteers whether a set-up can match (a dead end's text would show without Hint). A set-up that misses its goal is shown on the models when Hint is asked for. In puzzles 9 and 10, a pair that can match is turned until it visibly matches, then "These two match. Press Start again.". In puzzle 7, twins get the turn that puts the same letter in both middles, then the reason: "With D in the middle of both, A, B and C go round opposite ways, and a turn never changes that. So these two can't match. Press Start again.". The second hint level marks the disc, chip, small model or button, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from every reachable board of the pair puzzles, from a fresh start, after random play, after each kind of refusal, after every set-up and from a sort's proof.

## Certificates and claims

- **The child supplies the certificate.** "Can't match" is a pair of traced arrows, one on each model, round the same middle letter. The child chooses the letter to bring to the middle, the two letters to follow and their order. The app judges the claim once, when the last tap is in, and the blue arrows stay, running opposite ways. That drawing is the whole argument of the mathematics above, shown on the child's own models.
- **Can't comes after an attempt.** Can't is off until the model has been turned, and the hint chain makes that turn before it names Can't. On a fresh board Can't solves nothing.
- **A sort's That's all takes a certificate.** A right That's all is not yet a solve. The child then shows with Can't, using the same arrows, that each two stacks with the same letters can't match (one pair of stacks in each sort puzzle). Stacks with different letters plainly can't match, so they need no trace. A wrong sort keeps its one short refusal, "Some can still match.".
- **The certificate can fail, and it is rarely hit by chance.** No tap is tinted, ringed or focused while tracing (except as a level-2 hint). On a pair that matches, every claim with the right form is refused, and the letters then lock. On a true twin pair, a correctly formed claim is accepted because it is true: with four different letters, opposite arrows are equivalent to can't match. The validator counts every reachable pose and all 36 trace pairs. It reports 18 of 432 (4.2%) accepted on puzzles 3, 9 and 10 and on the proofs of puzzles 11 and 12, and none on puzzles 2, 4 and 5. A random tapper must first land the right middle letter and then trace the same two letters in order on both models, which is 1 in 6 even with the right middle.
- **Refusals are not a free oracle.** A wrong claim ("Same way round." or "There is a way.") tells the child that the pair can be matched. The letters of both models are saved as locked, and Can't stays off whenever the models show exactly those letters, by sticker: through turns, Undo, Start again and Restart, and after a letter is taken off and put back. Only different letters free it. The two form refusals reveal nothing about the pair, so they lock nothing. A refused That's all stays off until the shelf grows (which pairs) or the stacks change (sorting).
- **Undo takes back moves, never a refusal.** It keeps the locks, the pairs on the shelf and a refused That's all. Undo always changes the screen. An Undo straight after a refusal takes away the message and the refusal's arrows but keeps the refusal itself. An Undo straight after a pair goes on the shelf takes away its pine mark (B and C match with no turn, so the mark is what changes). In a sort, the model whose turn is taken back goes onto the big board.
- **Find every is judged as a whole.** In which pairs there is no per-pair verdict before the match: a pair goes on the shelf only when the models match. Then That's all judges the shelf with "There is another." and no count. A sort has no Check per stack, and its That's all answers "Some can still match." without saying which stacks or how many.
- **No quizzes, no free dragging, no swaps.** Nothing asks "will these match?". The models turn only by the four corner taps, and no move swaps two letters, which is the one thing turning can't do.

## The playground

The playground has two models, starting as the printed twins (ADCB and ADBC), and both can be turned. **Letters** switches the taps to changing letters: each tap moves that corner on to the next letter, A to B to C to D and back to A. Press Letters again to turn. There is no goal and no Hint, and the playground never counts as solved. It is the worksheet's partner game (K–1 Problem 1 and Problem 5). One player sets a pair, and the other tries to match it or says why it can't be matched.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- **What is saved.** For a pair, the save holds your model's pose and letters, the other model's letters, whether a turn has been made, the set-up and merge, the claim, the last message and the locked letters. For which pairs it holds the shelf, the refused shelf, the last message (with the pair just found) and done. For a sort it holds each model's pose, whether a turn has been made, the refused stacks, the last message, the proof (which pair, the copy's pose, whether it has turned, the right claim) and done. The playground saves both models. Everything drawn is derived from the save.
- **Only legal moves are accepted:**
  - a turn about one of four corners, never during a set-up or after a solve; during a proof, only the copy turns;
  - a letter put only on a corner that is to change (an A, or a blank), on its own side, in order, and Start again only once a letter is on;
  - one change, only in puzzle 6 or which pairs, between two different letters on the board;
  - Can't only where it is offered, after a turn, on letters that are not locked, with two different outer corners traced on each model;
  - That's all only with something shelved, or after a turn in a sort, never twice on the same shelf or stacks, and not during a proof.
- **Saved boards are checked:**
  - Every pose must be a rotation, so a reflected pose is refused.
  - The letters must follow from the start by the set-up and at most one merge.
  - A claim is judged again against the letters, in the pose it was made in, which must be the board's (a turn clears it). An accepted claim needs a turn. A wrong claim must be of the kind it says, with its message, on locked letters.
  - Each lock must be a finished set-up (or the puzzle's own letters) on which the two models can match, so a wrong claim was possible there. Locks are listed once each, in order, and only where Can't is offered.
  - A message must fit the board: "Not the same letter in the middle." only when the middles differ, "Follow the same letters on both." only when they agree, neither on locked letters or with a claim, and nothing on models that already match.
  - The shelf must hold real pairs, each once. A refusal must fall short of six pairs, and done needs all six. The pair just found must be the last on the shelf, with both models back at the start.
  - The models must never rest matched after a change, because a match goes on the shelf at once.
  - Refused stacks must be a partition by class with too many stacks. A proof needs exactly one stack per class and names a real pair of same-letter stacks. A sort is done only with every certificate in and judged true, or, if no two stacks have the same letters, with exactly one stack per class.
- **Who computes the answers.** `scripts/build-tetra.mjs` computes the answers with the mechanic's own rotations and checks them against the table above. `scripts/validate-tetra.mjs` computes them again by parity and by a separate search over its own turn matrices.
