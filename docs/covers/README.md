# Area traps

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 48 of the Bellingham math circle (inside and outside covers). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 48 worksheets are unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-48.md) decided the port (Decision, October 10, 2026): split squares over a fixed polygon to reach a target gap within a budget, nested splits allowed; paint squares to match a whole-and-partial pattern and an area target or declare it impossible; and slide a grid over a fixed shape. The app colours every square and draws both bounds, so counting is never the task. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet problem or says it is new.

## The mathematics

A shape lies on a grid of unit squares. A square is **whole** (green) when the shape fills it, **partial** (yellow) when the shape covers part of it, and **outside** (blank) when the shape misses it or only touches an edge or a corner, the worksheet's convention.

- **Two bounds.** The whole squares make the **inside**, a lower bound for the shape's area; the whole and partial squares together make the **cover**, an upper bound. The **gap**, cover minus inside, is the area of the partial squares. When some square is partial, both bounds are strict: inside < area < cover.
- **Splitting only tightens.** Cut a square into four quarters. A whole square has four whole quarters and an outside square four outside ones, so nothing changes. A partial square's quarters are each whole, partial or outside: whole quarters join the inside, outside quarters leave the cover, partial ones stay. So a split never lowers the inside or raises the cover (Grades 4–5 Problem 6), and it removes at most its own square's share of the gap. One split can remove nothing at all, when every quarter is still partial, and still open quarters whose splits do (puzzles 4 and 10, the card's App fit). With a budget of splits, the best next split is not always the one that gains most now.
- **Best at one level.** One level deep, each square's split saves its own amount and the savings add, so the best plan of k splits takes the k largest savings. Which squares win depends on the goal: for the cover, the squares with the most blank quarters; for the gap, those with the most quarters that turn green or blank (puzzles 6 and 9, the worksheet's contrast of K–1 Problem 2 with Grades 4–5 Problem 5).
- **The marks decide a range, not an area.** Fix which big squares are whole (w of them) and which partial (p). A whole square adds exactly 1, a partial one more than 0 and less than 1, so w < area < w + p, and every area strictly between is possible with fine enough painting (Grades 2–3 Problems 5 and 6, K–1 Problems 5 and 6). A target at or beyond a bound is impossible; the bound it hits is the certificate.
- **Moving is not refining.** Sliding a grid of the same squares over a fixed shape changes both bounds, and a slide can make them worse: the L of bonus Problem 1 has inside 3 and cover 3 lined up, 1 and 5 half a square sideways, 0 and 8 half a square both ways. The area stays put between them.

These are the **inner and outer Jordan content** of the shape: a shape has an area exactly when the gap can be made as small as wanted by finer grids (A. Sapozhnikov, *Mathematics 3*, §1.2, pages 2–4, as the Week 48 adult guide cites).

Squares are classified exactly. The mechanic clips the shape to each square with fractions in BigInt arithmetic (every corner and square side here has a power-of-two denominator) and compares the clipped area with 0 and with the square's area. The validator recomputes every colour by integrating the shape's horizontal slices across the square, a different exact method.

What children are meant to find: by acting, that a split helps only where the edge crosses a square, that some first splits do nothing until their quarters are split, that the best squares to split depend on the goal, that a fully painted partial square stops being partial, and that sliding a grid can make things worse. The explanations (savings add at one level; a partial square adds more than 0 and less than 1; inner and outer content) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Squares are green, yellow or blank; the shape is drawn over them in a light ink wash with an ink outline, always visible. Below the board a bar runs from 0 to the whole board's area with one tick per unit: green from 0 to the inside, yellow from the inside to the cover, with both numbers in Georgia numerals under its ends. A goal is drawn on the bar in pine: a box as wide as the target gap starting at the inside, or a notch at a target. Nothing on the screen counts for the child; the colours and the bar are the app's.
- **Split** (puzzles 1, 4, 6, 9, 10). Tap a square to split it into four; each split fills one slot. A split square shows a dot at its middle while none of its quarters is split; tap the dot to join it again (Enter and Space work on squares and dots). Squares split only down to the puzzle's level, and only within the slots. A reach puzzle is solved the moment the gap fits the box. A Best puzzle is solved by pressing **Best** when no plan within the slots beats the board's cover (or gap); otherwise "A smaller cover is possible." and Best waits until the board beats the refused value. After a right Best the splits are ringed and every yellow square of the starting grid shows what its split saves, which is the whole argument at one level.
- **Paint** (puzzles 3, 7, 11, 12). Each big square is four small squares of area ¼, framed and tinted with its mark: green must end all painted, yellow partly painted, blank none painted. Tap a small square to paint or clear it, or slide a finger across several (the first square decides paint or clear). A frame is dashed while its big square does not match its mark. A needle on the bar shows the painted area. The puzzle is solved when every big square matches and the area meets the target. **Can't** works only once the painting fits the marks: it turns the bar's two numbers into buttons, and the child taps the bound that stops the target.
- **Slide** (puzzles 2, 5, 8). The shape stays still in a window; the grid under it slides. Drag the grid (it snaps to quarter squares), press the arrow buttons, or use the arrow keys on the board. Colours and the bar follow the drag live. Solved when the bounds meet the goal.
- **Copy.** One objective line, needed because the goal is a number the board cannot say alone: "Make the gap 2 or less.", "Make the cover as small as you can.", "Match the colours with area exactly 6.", "Make the inside 5." The slots, the bar and the buttons carry the rest. How to play has the colour key, the controls and the rules.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, which never names the answer or asks for Can't. Then: for splits, join a split the nearest working plan does not use, else split its next square ("Split the top-left quarter of the square in row 1, column 2."), and "Press Best." on a best plan; for painting, fix the squares that break their marks first, then move the partial squares toward the nearest painting that meets the target; for an impossible target, once the painting fits, the reason and then the bound ("Each yellow square keeps a clear small square, so the area stays under 6. Press Can't and tap 6."); for slides, one step toward the nearest place that meets the goal. The second level marks the square, dot, button or number, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start and after a wasted move or a wrong claim.

## Can't and Best: the certificates

- **Can't (paint).** The certificate is the bound the target sits on, and it can be pointed at: after Can't the child taps the inside or the cover number on the bar. Can't stays closed until the painting fits the marks, so the claim is about the child's own attempt, and a fresh-board tap does nothing. A right bound solves, and the argument is drawn on the child's painting: for the cover, a minus on every clear small square of a yellow square (each is missing from the cover); for the inside, a plus on every painted one (each adds to the inside). The wrong bound gets "Not that one." and Can't pauses until the painting changes. Can't on a target that can be met gets "It can be done." and stays closed for the rest of the puzzle. The impossible targets sit exactly on a bound and the possible ones are reachable with quarter squares, so the small squares' resolution never decides differently from finer painting (the validator checks both).
- **Best (split).** The claim "no plan of two splits gives a smaller cover" is about every plan at once, and its proof is a list of every square's saving, not one object to tap; asking the child to supply each saving would make a counting screen, which the card rules out. So the app draws the argument after a right Best: each yellow square's saving on the board, with the chosen splits ringed. Best puzzles are one level deep, where the savings add and the largest ones make the best plan.
- **Undo never escapes a claim.** Undo takes back moves, not claims: a refused Best keeps its value (so Undo after a refusal leaves the same splits, with Best still waiting), a refused Can't stays refused, and a wrong bound's pause stays until the painting changes.

## The playground

The five split shapes (triangle, diamond, plus, hexagon, tree), chosen by picture buttons, with no slots: split and join any square, two levels deep except on the hexagon, whose second level leaves pieces too thin to see. **Clear** joins every square. The bar shows both bounds; there is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. It is Grades 4–5 Problem 6's question in action: no split ever lowers the green or raises the cover.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the list of splits (squares named by level, column and row, in order) and, for Best, the refused value and the done flag; for paint, the painted small squares, the refusal, the wrong bound and the done flag; for slides, the grid's offset in quarter squares; for the playground, the shape and its splits. Everything shown, including colours, bounds, savings and signs, is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: splits of unsplit squares above the deepest level whose parent is split, within the slots; joins of split squares with no split quarter; Best only while open; paint strokes that change something; Can't only on a fitting painting, with no refusal or pause; slides to another offset; nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: splits well formed, ordered, closed under parents and within the slots; a refused Best value above the best; Best kept only on a best plan; Can't kept only for an impossible target with a fitting painting and the right bound; a refusal only when the target can be met; a wrong bound only when it is the other one; offsets in range.
- Pieces too thin to see are ruled out: the validator samples every partial square at every level a puzzle or the playground shows (and every square at every slide offset) and requires every patch of shape and of background to be at least 3.5 px thick on a 300 px phone board. The worksheet's trapezoid fails this at 2.8 px; the shapes here pass at 3.8 px or more.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-covers.mjs` with the mechanic's own searches and checked again by `scripts/validate-covers.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheets are unpiloted. Three things to watch: whether children read the bar (green inside, yellow gap, the pine box or notch for the goal) without help, and whether the word "gap" means anything to them; whether a first split that changes nothing (the plus, the tree's trunk) reads as a broken tap rather than an invitation to split the quarters, and whether the join dots are found or Undo is used instead; and whether, in painting, children discover that Can't waits for a painting that fits, and that after Can't they tap a number on the bar (there is no on-screen instruction; How to play says it).
- **A two-way certificate.** Can't's certificate is one of two numbers, so after "Not that one." the other is right; the pause until the painting changes is the only cost of a guess. A richer certificate (tap each yellow square's clear or painted small square) would cost more taps; watch whether guessing happens.
- **Sixteenths.** Two-level boards put sixteenths on the bar ("4 13⁄16"), which young children may not read; the bar's lengths carry the comparison. Watch whether they look at the numbers at all.
- **Undo after Best.** A refused Best is a move, so the first Undo after it takes back the claim and leaves the splits; the refusal stays, so the board looks unchanged.
- **Small tap targets.** On a 375 px phone the join dots are about 30 px across and the smallest splittable quarters about 37 px (4 × 4 boards); below the usual 44 px.
- **Not ported.** The bonus page's range cards (Problem 2) and its fixed-area, longer-boundary shapes (Problem 3), Grades 4–5 Problem 7 (the triangle's exact area by two copies), and splitting with real tiles on paper.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
