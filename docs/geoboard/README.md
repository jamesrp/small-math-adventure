# Geoboard

October 10, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 57 of the Bellingham math circle (area from dots). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 57 worksheet is an unpiloted prototype. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-57.md) chose this port (App fit B, size M) and its **Decision** of October 10 shapes it: a geoboard where the app refuses crossing or touching sides, which paper can't, and lights outline and inside dots; two shapes with matching dot counts; every area-6 record with a checked "That's all"; seams; holes; and Pick-count impossibilities as the "can't" certificates. Predicting area from the dot counts is never the main puzzle: each goal names two of area, inside dots and outline dots, the child makes a band on the board, and every goal is checked on its actual board by enumeration.

## The mathematics

A band stretched round pegs of a square lattice makes a **lattice polygon**: one closed outline with straight sides and corners on dots, whose sides never cross or touch except two sides in a row at their shared corner. Its **outline dots** B are all the lattice points on the band, corners and the dots between corners alike (a side from one dot to another holds gcd(steps across, steps up) − 1 dots between its ends); its **inside dots** I are the lattice points strictly inside. One square of dots has area 1.

- **Pick's theorem.** For every such polygon, area = I + B/2 − 1. Assumptions: corners on the lattice, straight sides, one simple closed outline, no holes. Shape and corner count alone do not fix the area; the two counts do.
- **The seam lemma.** Cut a polygon along a segment between two outline dots, lying inside it, holding k lattice points with its ends. The k − 2 middle points were inside dots of the whole; they become outline dots of both pieces, and the two ends stay outline dots of everything. So I = I₁ + I₂ + k − 2 and B = B₁ + B₂ − 2k + 2, and I + B/2 − 1 adds exactly as area does. With rectangles (I + B/2 − 1 = ab for an a-by-b rectangle), half-rectangles, a bridge for general triangles and ears for polygons, this proves the theorem (the worksheet guide's proof; NRICH's Proof of Pick's Theorem follows the same additivity route).
- **Holes.** For a region bounded by an outline and h holes, each a separate band strictly inside it and apart from the others, area = I + B/2 − 1 + h, with every hole's band counted in B and the dots inside a hole counted nowhere. Subtract each hole's filled polygon to see the +1.
- **Two counts fix the third.** So a goal naming two of them is possible only if the third is a count a band can have: area a positive whole number or half, I a whole number, B at least 3 (every corner is an outline dot). When the third value Pick's rule forces is not such a count, no band works, and that number is the certificate: area 6 with 6 inside dots would need 2 outline dots; area 5 with 3 outline dots would need 4½ inside dots. Conversely a half in the area is fine: 6 inside and 3 outline dots give area 6½, a triangle.
- **The records for one area.** With area A, I + B/2 = A + 1, so each extra inside dot costs two outline dots. For area 6 the pairs are (I, B) = (0, 14), (1, 12), (2, 10), (3, 8), (4, 6), (5, 4); the next, (6, 2), is impossible. All six fit on the worksheet's 7 × 7-dot board.

What children are meant to discover: shapes with the same counts have the same area (puzzle 2), slanted sides trade outline dots for inside dots (1, 3, 5), seam dots change from inside to outline (4, 7), the counts for one area move in steps of one inside dot for two outline dots (6), Pick's rule as a tool to decide what is possible (5, 8, 9, 10), and holes adding 1 each (11, 12). Experiments come first, on the board; the conjecture (a rule from the counts) is the worksheet's Problem 4 and is never asked for here; Pick's rule is printed in How to play in the puzzles that ask children to use it, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 (the worksheet prints it at its Problem 5); the cut and holes puzzles leave it out. Explanations are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes; the worksheet's written proofs (Problems 7 and 8) stay on paper.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Dots only, no grid lines, on the paper card. The goal row above the board shows two of area (a sun square), inside dots (a pine box) and outline dots (an ochre ring); the row below it is the band now on the board, with each value that matches the goal in green. Once the band closes, its fill is sun, every outline dot gets an ochre ring and every inside dot a pine box, the worksheet's circle-and-box convention.
- **Stretching the band.** Tap a dot to start the band, then tap dots to stretch it from peg to peg; its end is a filled dot. Tap its first corner (ringed once there are three corners) to close it, and tap its last corner to take that corner back. A tap between dots goes to the nearest dot when it lands within half a square of it (a tap in the middle of a square does nothing). A side that would cross or touch the band is refused: it flashes as a dashed red line with a red cross where it would meet, and nothing changes. With the band closed, tap a corner to pick it up (an ochre ring) and tap another dot to move it there, or drag the corner; a refused move flashes the same way. Undo lets go of a picked-up corner. **Clear** takes the band off; Undo takes back the last move. Arrow keys move between the dots that do something, and Enter or Space taps.
- **Can't** (puzzles 5, 8, 9 and 10). Can't works only once a band is closed on the board: the claim is made beside the child's own attempt, and a press on a fresh board does nothing. Press Can't and the goal's empty box becomes a stepper (− and +, by halves from 0, the number in plain ink); set the number Pick's rule would need there and press Can't again. A right claim solves: the band stays, and below it Pick's rule appears with the numbers in (for puzzle 8, inside 6 + outline 2 ÷ 2 − 1 = area 6) and the reason ("Every band has at least 3 outline dots." or "Inside dots come in whole numbers."). A wrong claim gets one short refusal and nothing else, so Can't is no calculator: "It can be done." when the number is Pick's but the goal is possible, otherwise the child's own number, as in "3 outline dots don't fit Pick's rule." or "Area 7 doesn't fit Pick's rule." Pick's rule worked out and the reason appear only after a right claim. The same number can't be claimed again until the band changes, and Undo keeps the refusal. The claim leaves the band as it was, so one Undo after a refusal also takes back the band move before the claim.
- **The pair** (puzzle 2). Below the board, a triangle slot and a four-sided slot. A closed band with the goal's counts and three or four real corners fills its slot with a small picture; it stays when the band is cleared or Undo is used. Solved when both are filled.
- **Find every** (puzzle 6). Each closed band of area 6 with a new pair of counts is kept below the board as a small picture with its two counts, in order of inside dots. There are no empty slots and no count of how many. **That's all** says "There is another." and waits for a new pair (Undo keeps both the pairs and the refusal, and as after Can't, one Undo also takes back the band move before it); with all six it solves, and the next pair, 6 inside and 2 outline, is drawn struck through with "Every band has at least 3 outline dots."
- **Cuts** (puzzles 4 and 7). The shape is fixed. Tap an outline dot, then another, to cut straight between them; tap the first again to let go. A cut that touches the outline between its ends, or runs outside or along a side, flashes red and is refused. The two pieces fill sun and sky, the seam is dashed, and the seam's middle dots, which were inside dots of the whole, become outline rings filled sun in both pieces: the seam dots change colour. The record shows each piece against the goal for each piece. A new cut replaces the old one.
- **Holes** (puzzles 11 and 12). The goal shows all three counts and a hole count of "?". With the outline closed, tap an inside dot to start a hole: a second band, stretched and closed the same way, which may not touch the outline or another hole. Its dots are outline dots, and the dots inside it are not inside dots. While a hole is being stretched, the record and the rings and boxes keep the counts of the bands already closed. The hole count shows when solved.
- **Copy.** Bands, pairs, holes and Can't puzzles show no objective: the goal row and slots show the task, and How to play states it. Puzzle 6 shows "Find every pair of dot counts with area 6." and the cut puzzles "Cut the shape so each piece matches the goal." Titles never name an answer or whether a goal is possible.
- **Hints.** Before any move the first hint is the authored nudge, which never names the answer or Can't. Then hints aim at a witness shape placed anywhere on the board, nearest the bands already there: continue the band ("Stretch the band 2 right and 1 up."), take back a corner that is off course, close it, move a corner of a closed band ("Move a corner 1 left."), start a hole, or clear and start again. Each corner move brings the bands one corner nearer a witness, so following the hints always ends. For an impossible goal the hints build the authored attempt ("Try a band of area 6 with as many inside dots as you can. Start the band at the bottom left dot.") until the band on the board is relevant: closed, with the goal's area 6 in puzzle 8 and the goal's 3 outline dots in puzzle 10 (the attempt's `match`). Then comes Pick's count alone ("With area 6 and 6 inside dots, Pick's rule needs 2 outline dots: 6 + 2 ÷ 2 − 1 = 6."), which marks no button; applying it opens Can't with that number in the goal's empty box and leaves the board and the save unchanged. Only then, with Can't open, the reason names Can't ("Every band has at least 3 outline dots. Press Can't.", or "… Set 2, then press Can't." if the child opened Can't with another number). "Press Can't." is never the first hint, and a closed band that is not yet relevant gets a band move, not the certificate. In puzzle 6 the last hint is "Press That's all.", and in the cut puzzles the hint names the cut. The second level marks the dots, the corner and its target, or the button; the third offers Apply hint. Hints are memoised per board state (and, for an impossible goal, whether Can't is open) and take a few milliseconds.

**Why the app, not the child, draws the That's all argument.** For Can't the child supplies the certificate, Pick's number for the missing count, by setting it in the goal box. For That's all in puzzle 6 the certificate is the whole chain: the six pairs found plus the impossible seventh. The six pairs are already on the shelf, put there by the child's own bands, and the seventh is a pair no band has, so there is nothing on the board to point at; the app draws it struck through once the child's That's all is right.

**Why hole puzzles show all three counts.** Hiding one count would let a band without holes match: goal (area 12, 0 inside) is met by a band with 26 outline dots and no hole on a big enough board. With all three shown, Pick's rule with no hole comes out one (or two) short, and the hidden quantity is the hole count, so the child still makes something the goal does not name.

## The playground

Boards of 5 × 5, 7 × 7 (the default) and 9 × 9 dots, chosen with three buttons; a new board clears the bands. Bands, corner moves and holes work as in the puzzles, and the record shows area, inside dots, outline dots and holes. There is no goal and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. This is the worksheet's Problem 4, "Draw a new shape that could defeat your rule", and the blank boards of Problems 5, 7 and 10.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the closed bands (each a list of dots, the outline first and then any holes) and the band being stretched; plus, by kind, the claim made and the claim refused (Can't), the shapes made in each slot (pair), the shapes kept, the answer to That's all and the done flag (find every), the cut's two ends (cut), or the board size (playground). Everything shown, the counts, rings, boxes, seam dots and notes, is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a band start, a stretch to a dot whose side does not cross or touch any band, closing at the first corner with at least three corners and a side that clears the band, taking back the last corner, a corner moved to a dot that keeps every band legal, a hole started on an inside dot only where holes are allowed, Clear, Can't only where offered, after a band is closed and not twice with one number, the Pick's-count hint only for an impossible goal with a relevant band (it changes no saved state), That's all only in puzzle 6 and not twice without a new pair, cuts between two outline dots of the shape that touch it only at their ends and run inside it, board sizes only in the playground, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: bands on the board with 3 to 40 corners whose sides never cross or touch, holes strictly inside the outline and outside each other; a kept claim only of Pick's impossible number beside a closed band, a refusal only of another number; pair slots holding a band of the goal's counts with three (or four) real corners, and a closed matching band kept; kept shapes of the goal's area with different pairs of counts, in order, and the closed band kept if it has the area; "There is another." only while a pair is missing; done only with every pair; cuts legal, ends in order.
- The goals, witnesses and censuses are checked by `scripts/build-geoboard.mjs` with the mechanic's counts, and again by `scripts/validate-geoboard.mjs` with geometry of its own.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch: whether children read the goal row's icons (sun square, pine box, ochre ring) as area, inside dots and outline dots without help, or need How to play read aloud every time; whether picking up a corner (tap, then tap a dot) or dragging it is found, or children clear and restart instead; and whether Can't after an attempt, with Pick's number set in the goal box, feels like a discovery or like arithmetic to get past, especially in puzzle 10 where the number is 4½.
- **K–1 route.** The review card's Decision keeps a missing K–1 route as a should, as for Weeks 54, 55 and 60. There is none here: every puzzle needs counting dots on and inside a band. A K–1 version could sort dots (on the band or inside it) on a tiny board without any area.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
- **Checking areas.** The worksheet asks children to check an area with a cut or a second copy, independent of the dot counts. The app shows the area outright; checking it by hand stays a grown-up conversation (puzzle 2's notes) and paper work.
- **Paper only.** The rule-finding of Problem 4, the seam explanation of Problem 7 and the proof of Problem 8 stay on paper; the cut puzzles show the seam lemma but don't ask for its explanation.
- **Scrolling on a phone.** So that corners can be dragged, a swipe that starts on the board does not scroll the page (as in the other boards with strokes); the page scrolls from the margins. A 7 × 7 board fills most of a phone's width.
- **Taps.** A band of four corners takes five taps and a hole another five. If moving whole shapes or copying a band would help in puzzle 6, that is not built.
