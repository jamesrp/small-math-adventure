# Meeting roads: Gaps

October 10, 2026. A group of ten puzzles inside [Meeting roads](../meeting/README.md), built from Week 69 of the Bellingham math circle (thin and fat road triangles). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 69 worksheet is an unpiloted prototype awaiting organizer review. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-69.md) decided the port: draw three routes the app holds to shortest, tap a dot to flood its gap, reach a gap of k, claim Best, or answer Can't, with the lit tripod and the homes' covering as certificates, ladders and a ring of 12 as contrasting maps, and no predict-the-gap screens. It also names the pitfalls this group avoids: boards whose homes force every route, a flood that runs along coloured roads only, and counts such as "x of N".

## The mathematics

Three homes A, B and C sit on dots of a connected road map, and every road is one step; d(X, Y) is the fewest steps from X to Y. A **road triangle** is a choice of one shortest route between each two homes: AB, BC and CA. Routes may share roads. The **gap** of a dot X on a route is the fewest steps, along any roads, from X to the union of the other two routes; a dot on two routes has gap 0. A triangle is **δ-thin** when no gap passes δ. This is the Rips condition for geodesic triangles measured at the dots (Druţu and Kapovich, *Geometric Group Theory*, Definitions 11.1–11.2, printed page 363); the book also measures points part way along a road, which can add at most ½.

- **Every map: the homes bound the gaps.** If X is on AB, the other two routes BC and CA hold all three homes, so the gap of X is at most its distance to the nearest home. No gap passes the farthest any dot is from its nearest home. On every Best puzzle here, that bound is reached (the builder checks it), so the homes' covering is a certificate: every dot within k steps of a home, and a gap of k drawn. The bound says where a gap of k could be, not where it can: on the 6 × 6 grid of puzzle 10, four dots are 5 steps from every home and only two of them can ever have a gap of 5.
- **The homes alone do not fix the gaps.** When a pair has several shortest routes, different choices give different gaps. With homes in three corners of the 4 × 4 grid, CA through B makes every gap 0 and CA round the other way makes a gap of 4 (puzzles 3 and 4); puzzles 7 and 8 do the same with homes that are not corners.
- **Trees: every gap is 0.** In a tree there is one route without turning back between two dots, so the triangle is forced. Cutting any road splits the tree in two: if all three homes are on one side no route crosses it, and if two are on one side and one on the other exactly two routes do. So the routes form a **tripod**, three legs from a centre, every road of a route on exactly two routes, and every gap 0. Trees are 0-hyperbolic (puzzle 2; all 220 choices of homes on its tree).
- **Ladders: no gap passes 1, however long.** On a ladder (two rails joined by rungs), a shortest route between homes in columns i and j visits every column from i to j and no other. For three columns a ≤ b ≤ c, every column from a to c lies in [a, c] and in [a, b] or [b, c], so two routes visit it, and a column is one rung. A dot on one route is at most one step from another (puzzle 5; every three homes on ladders 3, 5 and 7 squares long).
- **The ring of 12: at most 3.** Opposite homes have two routes; the one away from the third home leaves a gap of 3 at the far side. No three homes on the ring do better: every route is an arc of at most 6 steps between two homes, and its dots are within 3 of an end (puzzle 6). A loop of n roads gives triangles about n/4 thick.
- **Grids: as fat as you like.** With homes in three corners of an n × n square, AB and BC are forced along two sides. A dot of CA that is y up and x across is y steps from AB and n − x from BC, so the biggest gap is n, at the corner opposite B, reached only by CA along the far two sides (puzzle 4; n = 1 to 7 checked). No number bounds every grid triangle: the grid, like the plane, is not hyperbolic. Finite maps are always δ-thin for some δ; "not hyperbolic" means the gaps grow without bound as the grids grow, while trees stay at 0 and ladders at 1.
- **Every gap 0 is a tripod.** On these maps, every gap is 0 exactly when every road of the routes is on two of them (the validator checks every triangle), and then the three routes meet at a dot on a shortest route between each two homes: Meeting roads' meeting dot (puzzle 7, with Meeting roads puzzle 2's homes).

Experiments come first: draw three routes, flood dots, compare. The conjectures (trees never give a gap; ladders never give 2; a corner square gives its side; the same homes can be thin or fat) come from drawing and flooding. The explanations appear as certificates when a claim is right: the tripod's glowing roads and the centre the child taps, the ladder's ringed columns, and each dot's steps to its nearest home; the reasons are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes. The worksheet's written arguments, Problem 4 (whatever number is named, a grid triangle beats it) and Problem 5 (every tree, every three homes), stay on paper.

**Grade bands.** Grade-free in the app. The card rates the worksheet strong for grades 4–5 and gives K–3 no route, by design. The app takes over the bookkeeping that made it a 4–5 sheet (it refuses a step that is not shortest and measures every gap), so the easy puzzles may suit children who count steps confidently, with a grown-up reading the goal; the Best puzzles ask for planning three routes at once. All of this is untested.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Homes are dots with a thick ink ring and their letter. Above the map, three route buttons AB, BC and CA, each with its route's colour underneath (rose, blue, green), and a **Can't** button (gap puzzles) or **Best** button (Best puzzles); every-gap-0 puzzles have no claim button. Each route is drawn along its roads in its colour (blue dashed, green dotted, so they differ without colour); routes sharing a road are drawn side by side.
- **Drawing.** Taps draw the first unfinished route, AB at the start; its button has a pine ring. Each route starts at its first home: AB at A, BC at B, CA at C. The chosen route's end has a ring in its colour, and the dots it can step to while staying shortest are tinted; only those steps are allowed, so a route can never be too long. Tap a tinted dot, or the road between, or slide a finger along the dots. When a route reaches its second home the next unfinished route is chosen; in order, each starts where the last ended, so one slide can go A → B → C → A round the whole triangle. Tap a dot already on the chosen route to take it back to there; tap a route's button to choose it, and again to let go. Arrow keys draw along the road that points most nearly that way; Tab with Enter or Space works too. Undo takes back the last move and hands the pen back to the route it changed (or, if that route is finished again, to the first unfinished one), so a step taken back can be taken again with one tap.
- **Flooding.** When all three routes are drawn, no route is chosen, and a tap on any dot of a route floods it: sun light spreads from it one step at a time, along every road, coloured or not, until it reaches another route. The dots it reaches are ringed in pine, the roads it spread along glow, and the gap is the number on the flooded dot. A slide never floods: a slide that finishes the triangle and runs on stops drawing. Changing any route clears the flood.
- **Goals.** *Every gap 0* (puzzles 3, 7) is solved the moment the triangle is finished with every gap 0; the tripod's roads glow and its centre is ringed. *A gap of k* (1, 2, 4, 5) is solved by flooding a dot whose gap is k or more. **Can't** is right when no road triangle of these homes has such a gap, and the child backs it where the reason can be pointed at. Can't is a claim about the child's own triangle, so it is off until all three routes are drawn, and a refused Can't stays off until a route changes (a flood or Undo is no change). On the tree, Can't stays pressed and "Tap where all three routes meet." appears; the routes are forced and drawn, so the tripod is on the board. A tap on any dot but its centre gets "The three routes don't all meet there.", and Can't lets go and waits for a route to change, so the centre can't be found by tapping dot after dot; a tap on the centre solves it, and the forced triangle glows with that centre ringed. On the ladder the app draws the certificate itself: each column ringed in the colours of the routes that must cross it, with "Two routes cross every column." That reason is a fact about all eight columns at once, with no single dot or road to point at, and tapping column after column would only repeat what the rings show, so the ladder's Can't is one press. On the grids "It can be done." appears, and Can't waits until a route changes. (Before October 24, 2026 Can't was on from a fresh board: one press solved the ladder with no route drawn, and the tree's centre could be found by tapping dot after dot. An independent play-through found both.) *Best* (6, 8, 9, 10): flood a dot and press **Best**. It is right when that gap is the biggest any triangle of these homes has; then every dot shows its steps to the nearest home, the dots where that biggest gap can be made are ringed in ochre (not every dot that far from a home), and "Every dot is within 3 steps of A, B or C." (with the right number) says why no gap can be bigger. Otherwise "A bigger gap is possible." appears, and Best waits for a new flood. Undo keeps a refused Can't while the routes are the same and a refused Best while the flood is, so one press goes back past the refusal to the move before it. No screen asks for a gap to be predicted, and nothing counts triangles.
- **Copy.** One short goal: "Make a gap of 1 or more.", "Make a gap of 2 or more.", "Make every gap 0." or "Make the biggest gap you can." On the tree, "Tap where all three routes meet." while Can't is pressed. How to play gives the rules: shortest routes, the gap, the flood, Can't (and the tap on the tree) or Best.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then hints aim at the nearest triangle that reaches the goal (the one sharing the most of the routes already drawn). Where Can't is right, they first draw a triangle (the nearest one), and after a refused tap on the tree take CA back a step to draw it again; then they give the reason with the press: "On a tree each pair of homes has only one route, and the three routes share every road. Press Can't and tap where all three meet." or "Two routes cross every column, and a column is one rung, so no gap can be 2. Press Can't." Otherwise "Take CA back to C." or "Take CA back 2 steps." for a route that has left it, then "Draw AB one step right." (on the ring "Draw AB to 11 o'clock."), "Flood the dot at 0 across, 4 up." and "Press Best.". The second level marks the dot, road or button on the board, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle, from a fresh start, after a wrong triangle, after a refused Can't or Best, after a tap that missed the tree's centre, and after random play.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the three routes (each the list of dots from its first home), the flooded dot, the answer to the last Can't, tap or Best, and the done flag. Gaps, the flood's spread, rings and captions are derived from it. Which route is chosen, and whether Can't is pressed on the tree, is view state and is not saved; with none chosen, taps draw the first unfinished route, and flood once all three are finished.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a step from a route's end to a dot one step nearer its other home, a take-back to a dot already on that route, a flood on a dot of a route once all three are drawn, Can't only on gap puzzles, only once all three routes are drawn (on the tree only with a tap on a dot, elsewhere only without one) and not again after a refusal or a missed tap until a route changes, Best only after a flood and not twice without a new flood, and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: every route starts at its home and keeps every step shortest; a flood only on a route of a finished triangle; "It can be done." only when it can; "A bigger gap is possible." only with a flood smaller than the biggest; "The three routes don't all meet there." only on the tree, unsolved; done only with a right claim. Saves from before Can't waited for the triangle may hold a Can't answer on an unfinished one; they still load (a right Can't on the tree then draws the forced triangle).
- Every answer, count, biggest gap, covering bound and certificate is computed by `scripts/build-gapflood.mjs` with the mechanic's search and checked against the table above, and checked again by `scripts/validate-gapflood.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch: whether children find flooding once the third route closes (the route buttons let go and a tap floods), or keep choosing routes and drawing; whether Can't and Best get pressed as guesses, since "It can be done." and "A bigger gap is possible." say only that the claim is wrong, and whether the tree's centre is found by looking at the drawn routes (a missed tap now costs a step of CA taken back and drawn again); and whether children count a gap along coloured roads only and are surprised when the flood takes an uncoloured shortcut (the card's pitfall), or read the flood as a measurement they can predict.
- **Many taps and small dots.** The 6 × 6 grid takes 24 steps of routes, and its dots and the ladder's are small at phone width. Slides help; whether they are found, and whether taps land on the right dot, is untested.
- **No Homes tool.** Problem 5 (every three homes on a tree) and Problem 4 (beat any named number on the grid) stay on paper; there is no playground for moving the homes. The validator checks every choice of homes on the tree, the ladders and the ring.
- **Gaps at dots only.** The research definition also measures points part way along a road; the app measures at dots.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
