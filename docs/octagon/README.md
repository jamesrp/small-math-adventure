# Octagon streets

October 17, 2026. Ten puzzles and a playground built from Week 65 of the Bellingham math circle (hyperbolic octagon streets). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 65 packet is unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-65.md) says keep, and its Decision is what this family builds: a robot on a recentring octagon floor, with a square floor beside it for contrast; the robot's position kept as its exact sequence of moves, so returns are decided exactly and the drawing only shows them; solves that come home facing the same way in exactly 8 or 14 moves or fence a block on a turn budget; and "can't" goals that carry the mathematics: home in 4 or 6 moves, or in any odd number, and two rooms with fewer than 12 left turns. The child always makes the moves; there is no "run this program, say where it ends" screen. Each puzzle's `provenance` names its worksheet source.

The floor is the card's and the packet's {8,4} tiling: right-angled octagons, four meeting at every corner, so at every dot the robot can turn left, go straight on or turn right. (The port brief said three blocks at each corner; that floor would have no straight move and none of the card's numbers. The card and packet decide.)

## The mathematics

A **floor** is a tiling seen as streets: dots are the corners, roads are the sides, rooms are the tiles. The octagon floor is {8,4} in the hyperbolic plane; the square floor is {4,4}. The robot stands on a dot facing along one of its roads. A **move** turns at the dot (left, straight on or right) and walks one road to the next dot; the robot never turns round. **Home facing the same way** means back on the starting dot with the starting heading. A **fence** is a walk that comes home facing the same way and never uses a dot twice; the rooms it goes round are inside.

- **The robot's state is a group element.** The states (a dot and a heading) correspond one to one to the elements of the rotation group ⟨T, H | T⁴ = H² = (TH)^p = 1⟩, with p = 8 on octagons and p = 4 on squares: T turns a quarter at the dot, H walks the road and turns round. The moves are L = THTT, S = HTT, R = tHTT (t = T⁻¹). The mechanic keeps the robot as the product of its moves, reduced by a complete rewriting system (HH, Tt and tT vanish, tt → TT, TTT → t, (HT)^k → (tH)^k, (Ht)^k → (TH)^k and (Ht)^(k−1)HTT → (TH)^k t, with k = p / 2), whose normal forms are unique. A walk comes home facing the same way exactly when its reduced word is empty. Floating point is used only to draw.
- **Shortest return 8; none in 4 or 6 on octagons.** The smallest loop of roads on the octagon floor goes round a room: 8 roads (girth 8). So a walk of k ≤ 3 roads that never turns round is the only route of k roads between its ends: two different ones would close a loop of at most 6. A walk home of 2k moves would have to come back from its halfway dot along a k-road route, which can only be the way it came, starting with a turn round, which no move makes. Squares have girth 4, and there 4 and 6 work: 2 and 6 walks (Week 65 Problem 1 counts 2, 6 and 54 routes of 4, 6 and 8 moves). On octagons the walks home by length are 8: 2 (eight lefts, eight rights), 10: 0, 12: 0, 14: 14, 16: 50, 18: 144, 20: 620; and a walk home followed by eight lefts round a room is a walk home 8 moves longer, so from the four starting cases 14, 16, 18 and 20, adding 8 each time, every even length from 14 on works. That 10 and 12 fail is a count, not this halfway argument (routes of 4 or more roads can differ, round half a room).
- **No odd return on either floor.** Every room has an even number of sides, so the dots can be coloured light and dark with every road joining a light dot to a dark one. Every move changes the colour, so after an odd number of moves the robot is on the other colour from home.
- **Turning round a fence (Gauss–Bonnet).** Round a fence enclosing n rooms, left turns minus right turns is 4 + 4n on octagons and always 4 on squares. Each turn is a quarter turn and roads are straight (geodesics), so the total turning is a full turn plus the area inside; a right-angled octagon has area (8 − 2)π − 8 · π/2 = 2π, a full turn, and a square has none. The children's form is the card's 16 − 4: two octagons have 16 corners, and a fence round both goes straight at the two ends of their shared wall, losing 4, so 12 left turns; two squares have 8 corners and lose 4. In these puzzles every fence goes round anticlockwise, because the room on the robot's left as it walks the last road home is always a flower. Consequences: two rooms need 12 left turns with no right turns (puzzles 3 and 8), four rooms round one dot need 20 (puzzle 6), and three rooms with one right turn take 17 lefts (puzzle 9).
- **The bound is about every fence at once (Euler).** For any fence round n rooms, let k_v be the number of fenced rooms at the fence's corner v (1 where it turns left, 2 where it goes straight, 3 where it turns right). Then Σ_v (2 − k_v) = L − R = 4 + 4n on octagons (4 on squares). Proof by counting: with b dots on the fence and i dots inside, the 8n room corners give Σ k_v + 4i = 8n, and Euler's formula for the fenced disk (V − E + F = 1 with V = b + i, E = (8n + b)/2, F = n) gives i = 1 + 3n − b/2; so Σ k_v = 2b − 4n − 4. A fence holding both flowers has n ≥ 2, so L ≥ 12 + R ≥ 12, and no fence of any shape keeps to 11 lefts; this is puzzle 8's can't. The tight fence round the two rooms (16 corners, 4 lost at the shared wall) is the case n = 2 that the app draws; the hint adds "4 more for each room" for bigger blocks.
- **Rooms that meet at a corner.** A fence never uses a dot twice, so if both rooms that meet only at a dot are inside, a third room at that dot must be inside too. If the fourth is left out, the fence bends in round it with a right turn at that dot; with no right turns, all four are inside (puzzle 6).

Experiments come first: walk the robot on both floors with the same moves and see where it ends up. The conjectures (eight moves round a room; nothing shorter; nothing odd; twelve lefts round two octagons against four round two squares) come from tries and refusals. The explanations (the only short way back, the two colours, the corners lost at a shared wall, Gauss–Bonnet) are in each puzzle's grown-up notes; the certificate a child taps (or, in puzzle 8, the fence the app draws) after Can't is the start of each one.

**Why words, not coordinates.** On the hyperbolic floor rooms near the rim of the drawing are tiny, and any test by floating-point position would eventually confuse two dots. Returns, dots used twice, which rooms a fence holds and every certificate are decided on reduced words; the drawing follows the words.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The floors.** Each floor is drawn round the robot: the octagon floor in a disk (the Poincaré disk), the square floor in a framed square. Roads are grey lines, dots small grey dots, the flower rooms rose with a flower in each. Home is a yellow ring with a faint dashed arrow for the way the robot faced at the start; the robot is an ink arrow; its trail is a leaf-green line. After every move the floor slides (without turning) so the robot is back in the middle, and rooms near the rim are drawn smaller although they are all the same size. When home is off the drawing, a sun-coloured arrowhead on the edge, outside every target, points out toward it. Two floors sit side by side on a wide screen (each at most 340 px); in a narrow panel, **Squares** and **Octagons** tabs show one at a time.
- **Moving.** Tap ↰, ↑ or ↱ under a floor, or tap one of the three roads ahead of the robot on the floor itself; on a focused floor the arrow keys ←, ↑ and → do the same. Drag a floor (more than a few pixels) to look round it; a drag never moves the robot. Focus stays on the floor or the move buttons.
- **Home goals** (puzzles 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 10). Below each floor is a row of one box per move, which fills with each move's arrow; the move buttons stop when the row is full. A floor is done when its walk ends on the yellow ring pointing like the faint arrow (the trail turns dark green).
- **Fence goals** (puzzles 3, 6, 8 and 9). Dots the fence has used turn dark, and a move onto a used dot is not offered. Reaching home ends the walk; it is a fence if the robot faces the same way, and it holds the flowers if they are inside (the inside rooms turn pale green). "No right turns" disables ↱; a left-turn budget shows a row of ↰ chips that fade as left turns are used, and ↰ stops when they are gone. Fences are at most 30 moves.
- **Can't.** Can't is offered once a try is finished (every move made, or a fence walk that has come home, is stuck or has used its left turns) and no refusal stands. On a home goal, pressing Can't (shown pressed) draws only targets: white dots, all alike, every dot big enough to tap (on octagons within 0.94 of the middle, where neighbouring dots are still about 15 px apart on a 340 px floor; on squares within the frame), but not home; other dots are hidden until Can't is left and come within reach with a drag. A tap takes the target drawn nearest it, if it is within reach: 0.7 in hyperbolic distance on octagons (a road is 1.53), so the reach shrinks with the drawing toward the rim, and 0.16 on squares (a road is 0.36). Taps on home, at the rim or far from every target choose nothing. While a dot of the walk is too near the edge to tap, a line under the floor says "Drag the floor to reach dots near the edge." (the trail already shows this, so it names no answer). On an even goal one tap is the claim; on an odd goal each tap marks a dot ochre (or unmarks it), and the claim is judged when as many dots are marked as the answer has. On the keyboard the targets follow the picture, outward from the robot in the middle and clockwise within a ring, labelled "Dot on the walk" or "Dot" (the walk is drawn, so this says no more than the picture); Enter or Space taps, and Escape or Can't again leaves without claiming. On a fence goal Can't is a claim by itself.
- **Copy.** Every puzzle shows one short objective, because the board alone can't say how many moves or which rule: "Home in exactly 8 moves, facing the same way.", "Fence in the flowers with no right turns." and "Fence in the flowers." How to play opens with what a move and home are, then the gestures and Can't; the rules list adds the floor's shape and the fence and budget rules.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then, on a floor with an answer: the next move of the first answer that continues the walk ("Turn left."), or, when no answer continues it, "This walk can’t come home in time. Take back the last move." or "This fence can’t work. Take back the last move.". On a floor with none: moves of a natural try until it is finished, then the reason, which ends by saying what to press and tap ("… Press Can’t, then tap the dot after three moves."); after a refusal, "Take back the last move, then finish another try.", and the next moves finish a different walk (never only the refused one again). The second level marks the road, button or target, and when no dot still to tap is within reach it slides the floor to the next one (the dot after half the moves, or the first light dot of the walk not yet marked); the third offers Apply hint. Taking a move back is a hint move of that floor alone, never a dead end, because the app shows a dead end's text unasked, and on a two-floor puzzle that would say which floor has an answer. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after wrong moves, after wrong taps and after a refusal kept through Restart.

## Certificates and claims

Every can't is a claim judged at once, and Wave 5's and Wave 7's rules apply as follows.

- **The child points at the certificate after an attempt.**
  - *Home in 4 or 6 moves* (puzzles 2 and 4): after a finished walk of 4 (or 6) moves, the child taps **the dot the walk reached after half its moves**. If it is right, the app draws the first half of the walk in ochre, rings that dot and draws a dashed red turn-round arrow there: the only two-road (three-road) way back from it is the way the robot came, which starts with a turn round. The floor slides to centre that dot (again after an Undo and a new right Can't).
  - *Any odd length* (puzzles 5 and 10): after a finished walk, the child taps **every dot the walk reached after an even number of moves, other than home**: the dots of home's colour. If they are right, the app draws every dot in view light or dark, with those dots ringed, home light and the robot on a dark dot. The child's taps are the start of the argument: along the walk the colours alternate, and the taps pick out every other dot.
  - *Fewer than 12 left turns round two octagons* (puzzle 8): after a finished try, the child presses **Can't alone**. The app draws the tightest fence round the two rooms (dashed ochre), dots its 12 corners where it must turn left, and thickens the shared wall, at whose two ends it goes straight; the floor slides to centre that wall. Nothing is tapped because the claim is about every fence at once (Σ(2 − k_v) = 4 + 4n, in The mathematics), which no one wall or dot shows; an earlier version had the child tap the shared wall, which certified only the tightest fence. The reason comes in the hint before Can't is marked ("… A fence round more rooms needs 4 more for each room.").
- **The tap is judged first, on every floor.** Any wrong tap gets the same refusal, "Not that dot." (or "Not those dots." on an odd goal, with the marks cleared), whether or not the floor has an answer. Only the right tap says more: "There is a way." on a floor that has an answer, the certificate on one that hasn't. So a refusal never tells which floor of a two-floor puzzle is the impossible one unless the child has found the reason. On a fence goal Can't is pressed alone: in puzzle 8 on octagons it is right after any finished try; on any floor with fences it says "There is a way.".
- **Certificates can fail.** The targets are drawn alike; nothing tints, rings or focuses the right one until a hint is asked for at the second level (which also slides the floor to the next dot to tap when none is within reach). The validator counts how often a random choice is right in the first view after the authored try: for puzzles 2 and 4, 1 of 25 to 28 targets, or none in view after a straight walk, when the dot is near the rim or outside the square frame; for puzzle 5, 1 choice of 2 dots in 276 on squares and of 3 dots in 3,276 on octagons; for puzzle 10, 1 in 3,276. On an odd goal the moment of judging shows how many dots the answer has; it is never more than half the walk's length, which a child can count anyway.
- **Can't comes after an attempt.** It is disabled on a fresh floor and until a try is finished, so a certificate is always drawn round the child's own walk, and on home goals the dots to tap are a real choice among many. "Press Can’t." is never a first hint, and the reason comes before Can't is marked.
- **Refusals are not a free oracle.** A refusal is saved with the walk it is about. It stays, and keeps Can't shut, until a different walk is finished: undoing the claim itself also takes back the walk's last move, so the board visibly changes and the refusal stays; making the same last move again keeps it; undoing a later move brings it back; Restart keeps it on the fresh floor. Its words show only while the floor's walk is that walk: hidden after Undo or Restart, they come back when the same walk is finished again.
- **Goals don't give the answer away.** No title, objective or first hint names a walk or says which floor is impossible; on two-floor puzzles both floors carry the same goal and the same controls. The left-turn budgets are not the fewest (11 where octagons need 12 and squares 4; 19 where the fewest is 17). The move boxes of a home goal show the goal's length and the moves made, not a count of answers found; there is no found-count anywhere.

## The playground

Both floors side by side, with no goal: walk the robot anywhere (up to 60 moves on each floor) and compare where the same moves take it. **Clear** empties a floor. There is no Hint, Can't or record row, and it never counts as solved. The worksheet's Problems 1 to 3 are free walking of this kind.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is each floor's walk, its refusal (`{say: "way" | "miss", walk}`, the walk it is about, or none) and the certificate after a right Can't (a dot's key for an even goal, the sorted keys of the dots for an odd goal, the empty word for a fence); the playground saves one walk for each floor. The view (pan, tab, Can't pressed, dots marked) is not saved. Everything drawn is derived from the save.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a move the button would offer (within the goal's length, 30 moves for a fence, 60 in the playground; never onto a used dot of a fence or past its end; within the turn rules), taking back a floor's last move, a claim only after a finished try with no refusal standing, with one dot, a list of different dots or nothing as the goal asks, Clear only on a walk in the playground, nothing on a floor that is done and nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: walks of legal moves only; a refusal only about a finished walk that misses the goal, and only while the floor's walk is unfinished or that same walk; "There is a way." only on a floor that has an answer and "Not that …" only where a dot is tapped; a certificate only on a floor with no answer, after a finished try with no refusal, only the right one (the dot after half the moves of that walk, exactly the walk's dots of home's colour, or nothing for a fence) and only in its saved form. A puzzle is solved when every floor is done: its goal met, or a right Can't.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-octagon.mjs` with the mechanic's words and checked against the table above, and again by `scripts/validate-octagon.mjs` with exact matrices and whole-number coordinates.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and Week 65 itself is unpiloted. Three things to watch first:
  - whether children keep track of the robot's heading as the octagon floor slides and the roads curve, and read "home facing the same way" from the yellow ring and the faint arrow, or need a grown-up to trace the first moves;
  - whether the taps after Can't are reasons or guesses: in puzzles 2 and 4, does the child find the dot after half the moves because of the reason, or try dots one at a time (each wrong tap costs a new, different try)? Many certificate dots are beyond the targets at first: on octagons 567 of the 729 six-move walks (puzzle 4) and 2,133 of the 2,187 seven-move walks (puzzle 5) leave a dot to tap too near the edge, and nearly every thirteen-move walk does (puzzle 10). The drag line under the floor and the second hint level help; whether children drag without a grown-up is to watch. In puzzles 5 and 10, do children find the dots of home's colour by counting even moves along the walk, and does the colouring then explain why no walk works?
  - whether the fence rules (never a dot twice, flowers inside, home facing the same way) are clear without help, and whether children count left turns and see 12 against 4 (puzzles 3 and 8) or only follow hints; in puzzle 8, Can't needs no tap, so whether the drawn fence and corners land as a reason is for the grown-up to check.
- **Gestures.** The floor takes every touch (`touch-action: none`) so that drags pan it; on a phone, scrolling the page has to start outside the floor. There is no move animation: the floor jumps to its new place.
- **Screen readers.** The floor's label lists the moves made, and the targets after Can't come in order outward from the robot, labelled "Dot on the walk" or "Dot"; which step of the walk a dot was reached at is not spoken (it would name the answer), so without sight the targets are still hard to tell apart.
- **Checking.** Puzzle 8's octagon can't is checked by searching every walk of up to 30 moves; the completeness of the octagon fence lists in puzzles 3, 6 and 9 rests on Gauss–Bonnet (the validator lists every block of rooms it allows). The walks-home counts the notes quote (none at 10 or 12; 50, 144 and 620 at 16, 18 and 20) are checked by counting in the validator, not by a proof children could follow.
- **Not built.** Week 65 Problem 4 (two streets through P that miss R; the card's proposed row mentions tapping them, but its Decision leaves them out), Problem 5 (the card says skip it), the card's fix 4 (48 rooms two crossings from home against 8 on squares), fences with right turns on squares (the L of three squares, 5 left and 1 right), and three flowers in a row (in the grown-up notes as an extension).
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail. The card rates grades 2–5 adequate with an adult reading and K–1 weak (the packet has no K–1 route); puzzles 1 and 2 and the playground are the likeliest entry points for younger children.
