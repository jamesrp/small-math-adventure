# Missing square (a group in Tile gardens)

October 23, 2026. Twelve puzzles built from Week 93 of the Bellingham math circle (squaring a rectangle). They are a group in **Tile gardens**, after Squares (Week 32's grid cuts), and are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road, so road stars do not apply. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 93 packet is unpiloted. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-93.md) proposes fit B, size S: cut a grid rectangle and fit the pieces into its average-side square with one square left over, vary the short side so the size of that square shows its rule, harder goals that place the square within a piece budget with "fewest" claims checked by search, and a short reverse-move set. Following the card, there is no reshape button that plays the convergence, no ordering of the worksheet's cards and no decimal squaring; the worksheet's later problems (exact fractions, the number lines, the narrowing ranges) stay on paper.

## The mathematics

A start shape on the square grid is cut along grid lines. A cut is straight: it follows one whole grid line across one piece and leaves two pieces. Pieces may be turned a quarter at a time (not flipped) and must lie on the grid without overlapping.

- **The average-side square.** For an a × b rectangle with a < b and a + b even, let m = (a + b)/2 and d = (b − a)/2. Then **m² − ab = d²**: the m × m square holds the rectangle with exactly one d × d square to spare. The picture proves it: cut m off the long side; the m × a piece lies across the square and leaves a band m − a = d tall, and the other piece, a × d, turned, fills that band except one d × d square. (Week 93 Problem 6, `w93.average-square`.) The area alone forces the size of the square left over: whatever the cutting, m² − ab = d² squares stay uncovered, so if they form one square, its side is d.
- **One cut: always that cut, always a corner.** The validator searches every one-cut fit for every a < b ≤ 12 with a + b even (30 rectangles), with the left-over square anywhere: every fit uses exactly the m × a and d × a pieces, and the square left over is always in a corner. With no cut the rectangle never fits, since b > m.
- **Moving the square left over costs cuts.** With the square at the middle of the top edge (6 × 2 into 4 × 4), one cut is not enough and two are. With it in the middle of 7 × 7 (from 10 × 4), two cuts are not enough and three are: four 5 × 2 pieces go round it like a pinwheel, as do two 7 × 2 and two 3 × 2.
- **The certificate for Can't.** A rectangle cut by straight cuts falls into rectangles. A rectangular piece holding two squares holds the box between them, so two squares whose box crosses the dashed square need different pieces. A square beside each of the dashed square's four sides gives four such squares, and two cuts make only three pieces. Squares that pairwise need different pieces are a lower bound on pieces that anyone can check pair by pair. In 7 × 7 with the middle 3 × 3 kept clear, four is the most such squares (search), so this certificate can't rule out three cuts, and three cuts do work. On the top-middle board the most is three, so its Can't claim never holds either.
- **The reverse move.** The m × m square with a d × d corner gone is the (m − d) × (m + d) rectangle rearranged, and one cut along a line from the missing corner gives it back. For every such shape up to 12 × 12 (search), one cut makes **only** that rectangle, except when m = 2d with d even: the 4 × 4 square with a 2 × 2 corner gone also makes 4 × 3, and the 8 × 8 with 4 × 4 gone makes 8 × 6. 4 × 3 is Week 93's reshaping move applied to 6 × 2: average the sides, (6 + 2)/2 = 4, and divide the area by it, 12/4 = 3 (Problems 3–4, `w93.reshaping-move`, `w93.one-reshape`; the card's "which 12-tile rectangle reshapes to 3 by 4?"). Two cuts make more: the 3 × 3 with a corner gone also makes 8 × 1, and the 5 × 5 with 3 × 3 gone makes 8 × 2 and 4 × 4. Flips make no further rectangles in any of these (checked), so offering only turns hides nothing.
- **Why the reshaping move never reaches a square.** Since m² = ab + d² > ab when a ≠ b, the new side m is longer than the side √(ab) of the square with the same area, and the other new side ab/m is shorter; the move keeps the area and narrows the gap but never closes it (Newton's method for a square root; Problem 7, `w93.narrow-forever`). Free cutting is different: 8 × 2 cuts into a 4 × 4 because 16 is a square number. This contrast is in the grown-up notes of puzzles 7 and 12, not in a button.

Experiments come first: cutting, fitting, turning and making rectangles. The conjectures children can reach are that the square left over has side half the difference of the sides (puzzles 1–3, 5, 6 and 8 vary the short side, as the card asks), that the long piece lies right across the square, that a square away from the corner needs more pieces, and that the shape with a corner gone is the rectangle rearranged. The explanations (the band picture, the certificate, the reshaping move) are a tapped claim in Can't and grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes. Week 93 is written for grades 3–5 with no K–1 pages. By prerequisites, puzzles 1–4 need counting and turning only and suit K–1 with an adult reading; 5–8 suit grades 2–3, who can predict the square left over before fitting; 9–12 and the Can't argument suit grades 4–5. The group itself is grade-free.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** The start shape sits at the top with its sides in Georgia numerals; the grid is below it, with its sides when it is the square. Scissors above show the cuts left.
- **Cutting, at the top only.** Tap a piece at the top to choose it (a lone piece starts chosen). Dashed lines cross it wherever a straight cut can go; a tap within about a third of a square of a line cuts there, and a tap in the middle of a square lets the piece go. From the keyboard the lines take focus, and Enter or Space cuts. Pieces on the grid are not cut; a piece is taken back up to cut it again.
- **Moving.** Drag a piece by any of its squares: the square held lands under the finger, and a drop where it can't go only shakes it. Or choose a piece and tap the grid where its top-left corner should go (kept on the grid, and turned the other way if only that fits). A second tap on a chosen piece on the grid, or **Turn**, turns it a quarter, kept at one of its corners. Drag a piece back up, or tap its empty place at the top, to take it back. Arrow keys slide the chosen piece on the grid.
- **Tape** joins every piece back into the start shape at the top, a way to try another cutting without Restart. Undo takes it back like any move.
- **Square puzzles** (1–3, 5, 6, 8) are solved when every piece is on the grid and what is left uncovered is one square, wherever it is; it is ringed and its side written in it. **Dashed-square puzzles** (9–11) are solved when exactly the dashed square is left.
- **Can't** is offered on all three dashed-square puzzles, so its presence does not say which one can't be done, and it is off until a cut or a move. Pressed, it lets the child tap squares of the grid that each need a piece of their own. A square that one piece could share with a square already picked is refused, "One piece can cover both.", with both outlined; tapping a picked square drops it; the claim is accepted as soon as one more square than the cuts make pieces is picked. On puzzles 9 and 11 no claim can be completed. The picker belongs to its board: Undo, Restart or leaving closes it.
- **Rebuild puzzles** (4, 7, 12). Every rectangle made from all the pieces goes on a shelf the moment it is made, up to turning, with its sides. **That's all** says the shelf is complete; if it is not, "There's another." appears and That's all stays off until a new rectangle is made. Undo keeps the shelf and the refusal. The shelf has no empty slots and no count.
- **Copy.** One sentence: "Fit every piece in the square, leaving one square uncovered.", "Fit every piece in the square, leaving the dashed square uncovered." or "Make every rectangle you can from all the pieces." How to play holds the controls and the rules (the cuts allowed, turning, Can't's rule); the grown-up notes hold the mathematics, prerequisites and sources.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, which never names the answer. Then the hints keep the child's pieces where a finish still fits around them with the cuts left ("Cut the 4 by 2 into a 2 by 1 and a 3 by 2.", "Turn the 2 by 1 and put it in the top right corner.", "Put the 3 by 2 across the bottom."); when the cuts made can't work, "Tape the pieces back together."; otherwise they take back a piece that is in the way. On puzzle 10 the first hint is a cut (a try), then the reason and the claim; on rebuilds the hints aim at a rectangle not yet on the shelf, then "Every rectangle is on the shelf. Press That's all." The second level marks the squares, piece, line or button, and the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after every first cut, after pieces put anywhere, after a refused That's all, after Undo and from random play. A hint worked out from nothing takes about 20 ms at most (validator, best of three on fresh and random boards).

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the pieces, each as its home squares (indices into the start shape, in reading order) and its place on the grid ([column, row, quarter turns] or none), with the Can't claim (square puzzles) or the rectangles found, the refusal and That's all (rebuilds). Everything shown is derived from it; the chosen piece and the Can't picker are view state outside the save.
- Only legal moves are accepted: straight cuts of a piece at the top, within the cuts allowed; a piece put on the grid in a quarter turn that differs from where it is, clear of other pieces and the dashed square; a piece taken back from the grid; Tape after a cut or a move; Can't after a try with squares that pairwise need different pieces, one more than the cuts make pieces; That's all with something on the shelf and not already refused for it; nothing after a solve. There is no reshape move.
- Saved boards are checked: the pieces split the start shape exactly, in order, no more than the cuts make, and come from straight cuts; places are on the grid, apart, off the dashed square and in a canonical turn; a claim only where Can't is offered, after a try, sorted and a certificate; rectangles found only answers, each once, with a rectangle on the grid kept; the refusal and That's all consistent; no unknown or missing fields.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-reshape.mjs` with the mechanic's searches and checked again by `scripts/validate-reshape.mjs` by other methods.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the Week 93 packet is unpiloted. Three things for testers to watch: whether children find the cut by tapping near a dashed line (and are not surprised when a tap in the middle of a square lets the piece go); whether, after a few square puzzles, they predict the size of the square left over before fitting, or only fit by trial; and whether Can't on puzzle 10 is reached by an argument about pieces touching two sides of the dashed square, or pressed as a guess after a few failed tries (on 9 and 11 the picker never completes, which may need an adult's word).
- **Cutting on the grid.** Pieces are cut only at the top. Cutting a piece where it lies on the grid would be closer to paper, but a finger drag on the grid must stay a move; a long-press or a cut mode would be needed.
- **Flips.** Pieces turn but do not flip. Every answer is the same with flips (checked), but a child with paper may flip a corner piece and expect the app to allow it.
- **The reshaping move itself.** The worksheet's move (average the sides, divide the area) and its trapped ranges are not in the app, by the card's decision; puzzle 7's 6 × 2 to 4 × 3 and the grown-up notes are the only link.
- **One playground per family.** Tile gardens' Playground button opens the Squares playground; this group adds none.
- **Story and grade levels.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines. Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
