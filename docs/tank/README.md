# Spring-water Jugs full jugs

October 5, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 29 of the Bellingham math circle (two-length builders), added to the Spring-water Jugs as its **Full jugs** group. They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. No child has played them in the app, and the Week 29 worksheets have not been piloted either. The theme's review card, `plans/review/week-29.md` in the worksheets repository, confirms the App fit: a small group in the water jugs, because the contrast is the point. The base jugs pour from one jug into another, so a 3-jug and a 4-jug measure 1, 2 and 5; full jugs only add, and 1, 2 and 5 are exactly the levels they can't reach. The card's pitfalls shaped the puzzles: find-every puzzles end with That's all and show no count or slots, and the last gap is never typed. It is claimed on a level the child chooses, and the claim is accepted only when the levels after it are lit, so guessing ab − a − b is not enough.

The worksheets use rods laid end to end on a number track. The app uses full jugs poured into a tank, which keeps the family's art and controls and makes the contrast with the base jugs visible: the same jug sizes, a different rule.

## The mathematics

Two jugs of sizes a and b. Pour empties one full jug into the tank, and − lifts one jugful back out. The order does not matter.

- **The tank reaches exactly the levels ia + jb, with i and j whole numbers.** These levels form the numerical semigroup generated by a and b. Every level that can't be reached is a gap.
- **A run settles everything after it.** If a levels in a row fill, a the smaller jug, then one more a-jug on each of them fills the next a levels, and so on forever. So a run of a filled levels proves that every later level fills. This is the certificate the last-gap puzzles ask for.
- **If a and b share a factor g > 1, the gaps never stop.** Every level the tank reaches is a multiple of g, so every other level is a gap: 4 and 6 fill only even levels.
- **If a and b share no factor, the last gap is ab − a − b** (Sylvester, 1884), and exactly (a − 1)(b − 1)/2 levels are gaps. 3 and 4 miss 1, 2 and 5; 4 and 5 miss six levels, the last being 11; 5 and 7 stop at 23. Pairs with last gap g are the coprime pairs with (a − 1)(b − 1) = g + 1: last gap 11 comes from 3 and 7 or 4 and 5; last gap 13 from 3 and 8 alone (2 and 15 is out of range).
- **Two ways to fill the same level differ by trades.** When a and b share no factor, any way to fill a level turns into any other by swapping b jugs of a for a jugs of b: four 3s and three 4s both make 12, so 24 is eight 3s, four 3s and three 4s, or six 4s.
- **Why a level can't fill.** Take away zero, one, two, … of the larger jug; the level fails when no leftover is a multiple of the smaller one. 17 with 4 and 7 leaves 17, 10 or 3, and none is a multiple of 4.

What is checked in the app: that every lit level really sits on its line with whole jugfuls of the chosen jugs; that That's all is accepted only when no dark level in the row can fill (or no other way exists), and refused otherwise; that "Last gap is t" is accepted only when t can't fill and the next a levels (a the smaller jug) are lit, which proves no later gap; that "Gaps never stop" is accepted only when the jugs share a factor, and only after every level in the row that can fill is lit. What is not checked: that the child can say why. The grown-up notes carry the explanations, including why a run is enough and why 4 and 6 never stop.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The tank is the board.** A tall vessel with a mark at every level and a line at the chosen level. Each jugful is a layer, shaded by jug, so the order of pours shows; the level is written above the water. When the water sits exactly on the line, the level lights in the row above.
- **Pouring.** Each jug has Pour and −. Pour empties a full jug into the tank; − lifts one jugful back out. Empty pours the tank away. The tank holds the top level in the row plus one more jug, so going over by one jug is possible and can be taken back.
- **Fill** (1): fill the tank to the line.
- **Which fill** (2, 4): light every level the jugs can fill, then press That's all. That's all is refused with "Another level can be filled" while a dark level could fill. After a solve, the levels that can't fill are struck through.
- **Every way** (3, 5): each different way (how many of each jug) joins a list drawn as little stacks. That's all is refused with "There is another way" while one is missing.
- **Last gap** (7–10): tap a level and press "Last gap is …", or press "Gaps never stop". The row ends in … because the claim is about every level, not only the ones shown. Refusals say what is wrong: "Level 10 can be filled", "A level after 7 can't be filled either", "Show that every level after 11 can be filled" (the run is not lit yet), "Fill every level you can first" (before Gaps never stop), or "The gaps do stop".
- **Choose** (6, 11, 12): tap two jugs at the top. Putting a jug back pours its water out and darkens the levels it helped fill; levels filled without it stay lit. In 11 and 12 the claim button already names the last gap the jugs must have.
- **Playground:** choose any two of the 2- to 9-jugs and fill any level from 1 to 40. It starts with 3 and 5.
- **Hints** first choose jugs that work, then the level to fill (in the last-gap puzzles, the run just after the last gap), then the way nearest the water already in the tank, taking jugfuls back before pouring more ("Take one 4 back out", "Pour one more 3"), and finally the claim. At the second level the button is outlined; the third applies it.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- A save lists the jugs (for choose puzzles), the level on the line, the jugfuls in the tank and the lit levels with their jugfuls. Every record must use the jugs as they stand and sit exactly on its level.
- One record per level, or one per way in the every-way puzzles. A save may hold a claim only when it is true, and a refusal only when it is false for the stated reason.
- At most two jugs may be chosen, and only from those offered. The tank cannot hold more than its top.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Things to watch: whether children use − to step back after going over or press Empty and start again (puzzle 1 rewards stepping back); whether a run of filled levels reads as a reason the gaps have stopped, or the "Show that every level after 11 can be filled" refusal only sends them to light levels without seeing why; and whether the 24- and 30-level rows in puzzles 8 and 10 feel long.
- **The contrast with the base jugs.** The card calls the contrast worth a puzzle of its own: the same 3- and 4-jug measuring 1, 2 and 5 by pouring between jugs, then failing at those levels with full jugs only. The two groups sit side by side, but nothing links them.
- **The "can't" picture.** The card suggests showing why a level fails by laying zero, one, two, … of the larger jug and showing that no leftover is a multiple of the smaller. The hints say this in words; nothing draws it.
- **Fewest jugs and three jugs.** The bonus pages' three rod lengths and fewest rods are not built.
- **A playground in the Spring-water Jugs.** The family had no playground, so its Playground button now opens the full jugs. If the base jugs get a playground of their own, the two will need separate buttons.
- **Story.** Full jugs are not on the Lantern Road.
