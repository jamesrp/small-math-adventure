# Shrink strips

October 17, 2026. Ten puzzles and a playground built from Week 76 of the Bellingham math circle (substitution strips, the Thue–Morse word). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children; the organizer promoted the Week 76 packet on October 15 after reading it, which is not classroom piloting. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-76.md) says port, and its App fit and Decision shaped this family: a strip of lettered tiles; tapping a gap drops seams and Shrink makes the row below, marking any AA or BB; seam placement is a tool with instant feedback, not the puzzle. The solves are catching the impostor in a strip (Problem 6) with the child's own shrinks as the certificate and the exact factor language as the check, repairing a piece with one flipped tile by finding every fixing flip and a checked That's all, and every seam placement for short pieces such as ABA (Problem 4), again with That's all. Growing rows is the playground. There is no "Is this a whole row?" screen and no "6 of 8" counter. Problem 2, Problem 5's explanation and Problem 7's proof stay on paper. The packet's sources have not changed since the card (the October 15 commit touched only its QA notes, README and manifest).

## The mathematics

- **Growing.** A row grows by the substitution μ: A → AB, B → BA, applied to every tile at once. From A the rows are A, AB, ABBA, ABBABAAB, ABBABAABBAABABBA, …, the prefixes of the Thue–Morse word T. Each row begins with the row before, and its second half is its first half with A and B swapped. Tile n of T (counting from 0) is A exactly when n has an even number of ones in binary. A *piece* is a run of tiles of T (a factor).
- **Pairs.** A grown row is a string of the pairs AB and BA, starting at even places. So it has no AA or BB at an even place, and no AAA or BBB anywhere.
- **Shrinking (desubstitution).** Seams cut a piece into pairs in one of two ways: *paired from the first tile* (pairs start at the piece's even places) or *the first tile alone* (pairs start at its odd places). A tile cut off from its partner at an end gets the partner back as a dashed ghost, always the other letter, since true pairs are unequal. Each pair shrinks to its first letter (a ghost before the first tile is the first letter of its pair). A pair AA or BB is *marked*: no tile grew into it, and that way has no shrunk row.
- **Exactness.** A way of placing seams is unmarked with a shrunk row that is a piece exactly when the piece occurs in T at a place of that parity. (If the piece sits at place i, the seams of T's own pairs are the way i mod 2, and the shrunk row is the run of their parents, a piece of T. Conversely, if the shrunk row p sits at place j, then μ(p) sits at 2j and contains the piece, ghosts included, at 2j + s.) Every word of one or two tiles is a piece, and from three tiles on every shrunk row is shorter. So:
  - a piece can shrink, one unmarked way at a time, all the way to one tile;
  - a word that is not a piece is *stuck*: each way of placing its seams marks a pair, or shrinks to a stuck row. The tree of ways the child builds is a finite certificate, and by the first point no piece has one.
- **Recognizability.** Pieces of four tiles or more fit one way only; ABA and BAB fit both ways (AB and BA too, trivially). Week 76 Problem 5 asks this for five tiles; its explanation stays on paper.
- **Overlaps and periods.** T has no overlap (a block, the block again, and its first letter: Thue, 1912), so a strip that repeats any block u has a stuck run of at most 2|u| + 1 tiles. Shrinking a strip that repeats ABBA gives the strip that repeats AB, which has ABABA, stuck because it shrinks to AAA one way and BBB the other. Two copies of a block can be a piece (ABBABAAB ABBABAAB is, at place 40); three never are.
- **Why ghosts.** The worksheet's Problem 4 says "decode only complete pairs", which drops a lone end tile. That shrinking is not exact: ABABA's ways shrink to AA and BB, which are pieces, so the impostor of Problem 6 could not be certified in five tiles. The card's math-check fix 4 notes that reading the lone tile's parent, as the guide's Problem 5 explanation does ("extend the clipped end-pair with a tile"), makes the 8-tile certificates of the ABBA strip work. The app draws that extension as the dashed ghost.

What children are meant to discover by experiment: some short runs fit both ways and longer ones one way; a way with no marked pair can still be stuck one row down; the strip that repeats AB has a stuck run of five; repeating strips shrink to repeating strips with half the block; a flipped tile makes a short stuck run near it; and one flip can have several repairs. The explanations are in each puzzle's grown-up notes.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The rows.** A row is its tiles (A in sun, B in sky, Georgia letters) in a strip with an empty slot at each end for a ghost. Tapping anywhere on a row places the seams at the join nearest the tap; the joins at the ends reach to the row's edges, so a tap on an empty end slot is the end join. Joins of one parity give the same way, so a tap is really a choice between the two ways, and the other way is one tap away. Seams are ink bars; ghosts are dashed tiles of the other letter; each marked pair gets a rose-red bracket, under the row for paired-from-the-first-tile and over it for the first tile alone, and the brackets stay for every way tried, so both ways' marks show at once. With unmarked seams, **Shrink** (where the next row will go) draws the shrunk row below. Each row has a column for each way shrunk, paired-from-the-first-tile on the left, so a fork shows both shrunk rows side by side; the current way's stub is pine. A row is stuck when each way is marked or shrunk to a stuck row: it gets a red ✕. A row of one tile turns pine. Keyboard: each join is a button (Tab, Enter); after Shrink, focus moves to the new row.
- **Impostor (2, 5, 7, 8, 10).** The strip sits on top, at most ten tiles to a line, with … when it repeats forever. Tap a tile and then another (or slide along the strip) to choose them and the tiles between; the chosen tiles get a pine underline and become the top row below. A second tap more than k tiles away is refused ("At most 6 tiles.") and nothing is chosen. Choosing again starts a new top row. The puzzle is solved as soon as the chosen tiles are stuck: there is no claim button, because the child's tree of seams and shrinks *is* the claim, and it can only be completed on tiles that no grown row has. The chosen tiles turn red-underlined and the tree stays. The card's "chain ending at AA, BB or AAA" is a single path; a path alone doesn't rule out the other way at each level, so the app asks for both ways at each row, which is a full certificate (puzzle 2's ABAB shows why: one way gives BBB, stuck, and the other gives AA, which fits).
- **Every way (1, 4).** The pieces are small pictures on top; tap one to work on it (the pine ring shows which). **Keep** puts the seams on the piece's top row on the shelf, unjudged, as a small picture with its cut and ghosts; Keep is off before seams and for a way already kept; a tap on a kept way takes it back. **That's all** judges the whole shelf at once: "One of these gets stuck." if a kept way can't shrink to one tile, otherwise "There is another." if one is missing; with exactly the ways that fit it solves. A refusal is saved; That's all stays off until the shelf changes, and Undo takes the note away (so the Undo shows) but not the refusal. No count, no slots. A move, or an Undo, on another piece brings that piece to the work area.
- **Repair (3, 6, 9).** The piece sits on top; tap a tile to flip it (ochre ring), again to flip it back, and a tap on another tile moves the flip there, since one tile was flipped. The row below is the piece as it now is, with the flipped tile ringed, for seams and shrinks. Keep and That's all as above, on the kept flips.
- **Playground.** One tile on top (tap it to start from the other letter) and Grow, up to five rows; each pair sits under the tile it grew from, its two tiles touching. No goal and no hints.
- **Copy.** The board shows one objective at most: "Keep every way to place seams that lets a piece shrink to one tile." (1, 4), "Choose at most k tiles that get stuck." (impostor), "Keep every one-tile flip that lets the piece shrink to one tile." (repair), none in the playground. How to play opens with the mathematics (growing, pairs, ghosts, first letters, marks), then the taps.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, never a claim. Then: in an impostor, if the chosen tiles fit a grown row, "Choose tiles 1 to 5." (the leftmost shortest stuck run); otherwise the next step of the smallest certificate, depth first ("On ABABA, pair the first two tiles.", "Shrink ABABA.", "On AAA, leave the first tile alone."). In every-way puzzles they take back a kept way that gets stuck ("Take back the seams on ABAB that leave the first tile alone: that way gets stuck."), then place seams for a missing way and "ABAB can shrink to one tile this way. Press Keep.", then That's all. In repairs they take back a kept flip that still gets stuck, then flip a missing fix and keep it, then That's all. The second level marks the tiles, the join, the piece, Shrink or the kept picture; the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start and after wrong choices, wrong keeps, refused That's alls and Undo.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is: the chosen tiles and the child's tree (impostor); each piece's tree, the shelf of kept ways, the refusal and its note, and done (every way); the flipped tile, its tree, the shelf of kept flips, the refusal and its note, and done (repair); the top letter and the number of rows (playground). A tree node is the way drawn now, which ways have been tried, and the shrunk row of each way shrunk. Everything shown is derived from it.
- **Undo takes back moves, not claims.** A refused That's all stays refused while the shelf is the same.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a run of 2 to k tiles of the strip, not the one already chosen; seams on a row of two tiles or more that is in the tree; Shrink only on the current way, unmarked and not yet shrunk, within ten levels and 48 rows (more than any certificate here needs: the largest for any word of up to 14 tiles has 5 rows and 4 levels, and Shrink is not offered past the limits); flips of tiles of the piece; Keep only with seams (or a flip) not already kept; taking back only what is kept; That's all not twice on one shelf; Grow up to five rows.
- Saved boards are checked against all of this: trees are well formed (a way shrunk was tried and is unmarked, nothing deeper than the limits), the chosen run is in range and at most k tiles, kept ways and flips are in order and unrepeated, a note matches the shelf, a refusal never stands on the right shelf, and done only when the shelf is exactly the answer. An impostor is solved only when the saved tree certifies its chosen tiles.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-shrink.mjs` with the mechanic's own functions and checked again by `scripts/validate-shrink.mjs` against the Thue–Morse word.

## Certificates

- **Impostor.** The certificate is the child's tree, built by taps, and judged when it is complete: nothing marks which way or row is left to do, and a row is only marked stuck (✕) once the child has done its part. The choice of tiles is the real decision. The validator counts random play (a random run of up to k tiles, then random seams and shrinks): it solves about as often as a random run is stuck, 33% in puzzle 2 (any five or six alternating tiles), 10% in 5, 5% in 7 and 8, and under 1% in 10. A tree on tiles that fit a grown row can never be completed (checked for every word of up to 14 tiles against the full tree of every way).
- **Every way and repair.** That's all judges the shelf as a whole; a random shelf is right 1 time in 64 (every way: three pieces, two ways each) or in 256 (repair: eight tiles).

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Three things to watch: whether children read the tree (two columns under a row, one per way) and keep track of which ways are done, or need the ✕ to tell them; whether the impostor becomes sweeping runs along the strip and shrinking each, rather than using what a strip shrinks to (puzzles 5 and 10 reward the second); and whether taps on long rows (16 slots on a phone, about 20 px a join) land on the join meant, since a mis-tap flips the way.
- **Ghosts versus the worksheet.** The app's shrinking gives a lone end tile its ghost partner; the worksheet's Problem 4 decodes only complete pairs. Children who did the worksheet may need a word from a grown-up about the dashed tiles.
- **Problem 1's count.** Finding every run of three tiles in a row of 16 (six of them) is not here; the card rules out a "6 of 8" counter, and a find-every shelf for it was not built.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail. The packet is for Grades 3–5.
- **Paper only.** Problem 2 (why no AAA or BBB), Problem 5's explanation (no five-tile piece fits both ways) and Problem 7's proof (the endless word never repeats) stay on paper.
