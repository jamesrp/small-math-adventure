# Shrink strips

October 17, 2026. Ten puzzles and a playground built from Week 76 of the Bellingham math circle (substitution strips, the Thue–Morse word). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children; the organizer promoted the Week 76 packet on October 15 after reading it, which is not classroom piloting. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-76.md) says port, and its App fit and Decision shaped this family: a strip of lettered tiles; tapping a gap drops seams and Shrink makes the row below, marking any AA or BB; seam placement is a tool with instant feedback, not the puzzle. The solves are catching the impostor in a strip (Problem 6) with the child's own shrinks as the certificate and the exact factor language as the check, repairing a piece with one flipped tile by finding every fixing flip and a checked That's all, and every seam placement for short pieces such as ABA (Problem 4), again with That's all. Growing rows is the playground. There is no "Is this a whole row?" screen and no "6 of 8" counter. Problem 2, Problem 5's explanation and Problem 7's proof stay on paper. The packet's sources have not changed since the card (the October 15 commit touched only its QA notes, README and manifest).

## The mathematics

- **Growing.** A row grows by the substitution μ: A → AB, B → BA, applied to every tile at once. From A the rows are A, AB, ABBA, ABBABAAB, ABBABAABBAABABBA, …, the prefixes of the Thue–Morse word T. Each row begins with the row before, and its second half is its first half with A and B swapped. Tile n of T (counting from 0) is A exactly when n has an even number of ones in binary. A *piece* is a run of tiles of T (a factor).
- **Pairs.** A grown row is a string of the pairs AB and BA, starting at even places. So it has no AA or BB at an even place, and no AAA or BBB anywhere.
- **Shrinking (desubstitution).** Seams cut a piece into pairs in one of two ways: *paired from the first tile* (pairs start at the piece's even places) or *the first tile alone* (pairs start at its odd places). A tile cut off from its partner at an end gets the partner back as a dashed ghost, always the other letter, since true pairs are unequal. Each pair shrinks to its first letter (a ghost before the first tile is the first letter of its pair). A pair AA or BB is *marked*: no tile grew into it, and that way has no shrunk row.
- **Rows of two tiles.** A row of two different tiles, AB or BA, is a single pair, so its seams go only at its ends: it shrinks to its first letter. Leaving its first tile alone is not offered, because it gives two tiles again and rows would go round forever (AB → BB → AB, AA → BA → AA). So a tree ends soon after its first row of two tiles: AB and BA shrink to one tile, and AA and BB (marked when paired) shrink to BA and AB, then to one tile. The rule leaves out only ways that do not shorten a row; it changes no answer and no certificate below, since only rows of three tiles or more can be stuck.
- **Exactness.** A way of placing seams is unmarked with a shrunk row that is a piece exactly when the piece occurs in T at a place of that parity. (If the piece sits at place i, the seams of T's own pairs are the way i mod 2, and the shrunk row is the run of their parents, a piece of T. Conversely, if the shrunk row p sits at place j, then μ(p) sits at 2j and contains the piece, ghosts included, at 2j + s.) Every word of one or two tiles is a piece, and from three tiles on every shrunk row is shorter. So:
  - a piece can shrink, one unmarked way at a time, all the way to one tile;
  - a word that is not a piece is *stuck*: each way of placing its seams marks a pair, or shrinks to a stuck row. The tree of ways the child builds is a finite certificate, and by the first point no piece has one. The largest certificate for any word of up to 16 tiles has 5 rows on 4 levels; the largest tree a child can draw at all (every way allowed, every unmarked way shrunk, on any word of up to 16 tiles) has 10 rows on 7 levels and forks once, so it has two columns at most.
- **Recognizability.** Pieces of four tiles or more fit one way only; ABA and BAB fit both ways (AB and BA too, trivially). Week 76 Problem 5 asks this for five tiles; its explanation stays on paper.
- **Overlaps and periods.** T has no overlap (a block, the block again, and its first letter: Thue, 1912), so a strip that repeats any block u has a stuck run of at most 2|u| + 1 tiles. Shrinking a strip that repeats ABBA gives the strip that repeats AB, which has ABABA, stuck because it shrinks to AAA one way and BBB the other. Two copies of a block can be a piece (ABBABAAB ABBABAAB is, at place 40); three never are.
- **Why ghosts.** The worksheet's Problem 4 says "decode only complete pairs", which drops a lone end tile. That shrinking is not exact: ABABA's ways shrink to AA and BB, which are pieces, so the impostor of Problem 6 could not be certified in five tiles. The card's math-check fix 4 notes that reading the lone tile's parent, as the guide's Problem 5 explanation does ("extend the clipped end-pair with a tile"), makes the 8-tile certificates of the ABBA strip work. The app draws that extension as the dashed ghost.

What children are meant to discover by experiment: some short runs fit both ways and longer ones one way; a way with no marked pair can still be stuck one row down; the strip that repeats AB has a stuck run of five; a strip with no AAA or ABABA on top can hide a stuck run that shows one row down; repeating strips shrink to repeating strips with half the block; a flipped tile makes a short stuck run near it; and one flip can have several repairs. The explanations are in each puzzle's grown-up notes.

## How a puzzle plays

- **The rows.** A row is its tiles (A in sun, B in sky, Georgia letters) in a strip with an empty slot at each end for a ghost. An untouched row shows a faint dashed tick at each join. A tap on it places the seams at the join nearest the tap; the joins at the ends reach to the row's edges, so a tap on an empty end slot is the end join. On AB or BA only the end joins are there, so any tap pairs the two tiles. Joins of one parity give the same way, so a tap is really a choice between the two ways. Once a row has seams, each seam keeps only a quarter slot on either side and the other way's joins take the rest (a slot and a half each), so nearly any tap moves the seams over to the other way, the only change a tap can make. Seams are ink bars; ghosts are dashed tiles of the other letter; each marked pair gets a rose-red bracket, under the row for paired-from-the-first-tile and over it for the first tile alone, and the brackets stay for every way tried, dimmed for the way not drawn, so both ways' marks show at once. With unmarked seams, **Shrink** (where the next row will go) draws the shrunk row below. Each row has a column for each way shrunk, paired-from-the-first-tile on the left, so a fork shows both shrunk rows side by side; the current way's stub is pine. A row is stuck when each way is marked or shrunk to a stuck row: it gets a red ✕. A row of one tile turns pine. Keyboard: each join is a button (Tab, Enter); after Shrink, focus moves to the new row. On puzzle 10, the busiest board, the trees that go deepest (tiles 1–15: six levels, a fork) and have the most rows (tiles 1–12: nine rows, a fork) stay readable at 375 px, with tiles at least 13 px wide.
- **Sizes.** On a phone the strip and the rows reach to 2 px from the panel's edges (the panel's padding is measured when the board is drawn and again on every resize, and the reach never comes within 4 px of the window's edges, so a board drawn wide and then narrowed to 320 px still fits). On a 390 × 844 phone, strip and piece tiles are at least 40 × 44 px, pieces and kept pictures 44 px tall, a row of eight tiles has 35 px slots (the other way's joins 52 px once seams are on), and puzzle 10's fourteen-tile rows have 22 px slots (the other way's joins 33 px). On a tablet (700 px and wider) the board grows to 760 px, slots to 56 px and strip tiles to 52 × 50 px, so the fourteen-tile rows have 45 px slots.
- **Impostor (2, 5, 7, 8, 10).** The strip sits on top, at most eight tiles to a line (so the repeating strips' blocks of 2, 6 and 8 line up), with … when it repeats forever. Tap a tile and then another (or slide along the strip; a slide that starts up or down scrolls the page instead) to choose them and the tiles between; the chosen tiles get a pine underline and become the top row below. A second tap more than k tiles away is refused ("At most 6 tiles.") and nothing is chosen. Choosing again starts a new top row. The puzzle is solved as soon as the chosen tiles are stuck: there is no claim button, because the child's tree of seams and shrinks *is* the claim, and it can only be completed on tiles that no grown row has. The chosen tiles turn red-underlined and the tree stays. The card's "chain ending at AA, BB or AAA" is a single path; a path alone doesn't rule out the other way at each level, so the app asks for both ways at each row, which is a full certificate (puzzle 2's ABAB shows why: one way gives BBB, stuck, and the other gives AA, which fits).
- **Every way (1, 4).** The pieces are small pictures on top; tap one to work on it (the pine ring shows which). **Keep** puts the seams on the piece's top row on the shelf as a small picture with its cut and ghosts, but only once the child's own rows show those seams reaching one tile: Keep is off before that, and for a way already kept; a tap on a kept way takes it back. So nothing that gets stuck can be kept, and the shelf holds only ways the child has shown to work. **That's all**, on once something is kept, judges the whole shelf at once: "There is another." if a way is missing; with every way that fits it solves. ("One of these gets stuck." answers only a shelf saved before Keep waited for one tile.) A refusal is saved; That's all stays off until the shelf changes, and Undo takes the note away (so the Undo shows) but not the refusal. No count, no slots. A move, or an Undo, on another piece brings that piece to the work area, and a puzzle opened again or reloaded shows the piece of the last move.
- **Repair (3, 6, 9).** The piece sits on top; tap a tile to flip it (ochre ring), again to flip it back, and a tap on another tile moves the flip there, since one tile was flipped. The row below is the piece as it now is, with the flipped tile ringed, for seams and shrinks. Keep and That's all as above, on the kept flips: Keep is on once the rows show the flipped piece reaching one tile.
- **Playground.** One tile on top (tap it to start from the other letter) and Grow, up to five rows; each pair sits under the tile it grew from, its two tiles touching. No goal and no hints.
- **Copy.** The board shows one objective at most: "Keep every way to place seams that lets a piece shrink to one tile." (1, 4), "Choose at most k tiles that get stuck." (impostor), "Keep every flip that lets the piece shrink to one tile." (repair), none in the playground. How to play opens with the mathematics (growing, pairs, ghosts, first letters, marks), then the taps.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, never a claim. After that a hint never judges the child's own tiles, flip or kept seams until the child's tree has settled them: it leads the child's own shrinking one step at a time, and names another run, flip or way only once the tree shows the child's is real (reaches one tile) or stuck.
  - *Impostor.* With nothing chosen, "Choose some tiles and shrink them." With tiles chosen and no seams yet, "Place seams on tiles 1 to 5 and shrink."; then the next step on the child's own tiles: for a stuck run, the next step of the smallest certificate, depth first ("On ABABA, pair the first two tiles.", "Shrink ABABA.", "On AAA, leave the first tile alone."); for a run that fits a grown row, the steps down to one tile, and only then "Tiles 1 to 5 can shrink to one tile. Choose tiles 9 to 13." (the leftmost shortest stuck run).
  - *Every way.* A kept way that gets stuck (only on a shelf saved before Keep waited for one tile) is checked by shrinking it ("Shrink ABAB the way you kept it.", then its steps); once the tree shows it stuck, "Take back the seams on ABAB that leave the first tile alone: that way gets stuck." Seams the child has placed and not kept are shrunk next ("Shrink ABAB."), until the tree shows them stuck or reaching one tile (then "ABAB shrinks to one tile this way. Press Keep."). Only then is a missing way placed and shrunk to one tile ("Place seams on ABA and shrink.", or "Try the other way on ABA." when the piece already has seams), then "ABA shrinks to one tile this way. Press Keep."; with every way kept, "Every way that can shrink to one tile is kept." and That's all.
  - *Repair.* A kept flip that gets stuck (again only from an old shelf) is checked the same way ("Check the flips you kept: flip one and shrink the piece.", then "Take back the flip of tile 1: it gets stuck."). With no flip, "Flip a tile and shrink the piece." ("Try another flip." once something is kept). The child's own flip is shrunk first ("Place seams on the piece and shrink."): one that still gets stuck leads, once the tree shows it, to "Flip tile 5."; one that works to "It shrinks to one tile. Press Keep."; then "Every flip that works is kept."
  - The second level marks the tiles, the join, the piece, Shrink or the kept picture; the third offers Apply hint. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start and after wrong choices, wrong keeps, refused That's alls and Undo; from a fresh start they take 6 to 31 moves (puzzles 1, 4 and 6 take 31, since each way or fix is shrunk to one tile before it is kept).

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is: the chosen tiles and the child's tree (impostor); each piece's tree, the shelf of kept ways, the refusal and its note, and done (every way); the flipped tile, its tree, the shelf of kept flips, the refusal and its note, and done (repair); the top letter and the number of rows (playground). A tree node is the way drawn now, which ways have been tried, and the shrunk row of each way shrunk. Everything shown is derived from it.
- **Undo takes back moves, not claims.** A refused That's all stays refused while the shelf is the same.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a run of 2 to k tiles of the strip, not the one already chosen; seams on a row of two tiles or more that is in the tree, at a join the row allows (AB and BA only at their ends); Shrink only on the current way, unmarked and not yet shrunk, within eight levels and 16 rows (more than any legal tree reaches: the largest, for any word of up to 16 tiles, has 10 rows on 7 levels); flips of tiles of the piece; Keep only once the child's rows take the seams on the top row (or the flipped piece) to one tile, and not for one already kept; taking back only what is kept; That's all only with something kept, and not twice on one shelf; Grow up to five rows.
- Saved boards are checked against all of this: trees are well formed (a way tried is one the row allows, a way shrunk was tried and is unmarked, nothing past the limits), the chosen run is in range and at most k tiles, kept ways and flips are in order and unrepeated, a note matches the shelf, a refusal never stands on the right shelf or an empty one, and done only when the shelf is exactly the answer. An impostor is solved only when the saved tree certifies its chosen tiles.
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-shrink.mjs` with the mechanic's own functions and checked again by `scripts/validate-shrink.mjs` against the Thue–Morse word.

## Certificates

- **Impostor.** The certificate is the child's tree, built by taps, and judged when it is complete: nothing marks which way or row is left to do, and a row is only marked stuck (✕) once the child has done its part. A tree on tiles that fit a grown row can never be completed (checked for every word of up to 16 tiles against the full tree of every way). How much the choice of tiles matters depends on the puzzle. The validator plays three ways: random play (one random run of 2 to k tiles, then random legal seams and shrinks, 40 moves in all; 400 seeded tries), which solves about as often as a random run is stuck; taking k tiles anywhere; and sliding k tiles along the strip from the left, one tile at a time:

  | Puzzle | k | Random play solves | Stuck among runs of 2 to k tiles | Runs of k tiles stuck | Slide from the left sticks on |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 6 | 30.5% | 33.3% | 100% | try 1 |
  | 5 | 10 | 4.8% | 6.1% | 43% | try 3 (from the right too) |
  | 7 | 6 | 2.3% | 4.6% | 18% | try 8 |
  | 8 | 9 | 5.0% | 5.6% | 40% | try 2 |
  | 10 | 15 | 0.8% | 2.6% | 40% | try 3 |

  (Runs of k tiles are those the board shows.) So in puzzle 2, the Easy introduction, the choice of tiles is no decision: every run of the full budget is stuck, and the puzzle is the tree, shrinking a long run until it is shown stuck both ways. Puzzle 5 was the strip that repeats ABBA, where every run of nine or ten tiles is stuck at every phase, so it repeated puzzle 2 with a longer tree; on October 24 it became a grown row with two neighbouring tiles swapped, whose only stuck runs contain the ABBA strip's BBAABBAA (tiles 5–12), so only the middle three of the seven full runs are stuck, and nothing on top (no AAA, BBB, ABABA or BABAB) shows where. The build and the validator now check that some run of the full budget fits in every impostor after puzzle 2. In 8 and 10 taking the full budget finds a stuck run within three tries, and in 7, where one flipped tile sits in a strip that doesn't repeat, only two of eleven full runs are stuck. The budgets stay as they are (each is more than the shortest stuck run, so it never names the answer's length); the table is what to watch against.
- **Every way and repair.** Each kept way or flip carries its own certificate: Keep waits until the child's rows take it to one tile. So That's all judges only completeness ("There is another."). Until October 24 Keep took anything, and That's all judged a shelf of one as readily as a full one, so a child could sort the candidates without shrinking anything (place seams or flip, Keep, That's all, take back: shrink-03 in 19 taps with no Shrink).
