# Chip firing

October 4, 2026; Still boards added October 16, 2026. Twelve puzzles, a two-puzzle **Still boards** group and a playground built from Week 11 of the Bellingham math circle (chip firing with a sink). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. None of this has been played by children in the app yet; the worksheets it comes from have not been piloted either. The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-11.md) says keep; its Decision added the two Still boards puzzles (the add-one cycle and capacity) and asked the find-every puzzles to end in a claim the app checks.

## The mathematics

Each board is a few circles joined by lines, usually with a **sink**. A circle can **fire** when it holds at least one chip per line that touches it; firing sends one chip along each line. The sink keeps what it receives and never fires.

- **Every run stops when there is a sink** (on a connected board). Chips drift toward the sink and are lost there, so a run cannot go on forever. Puzzle 10 makes this a count: on the square, the weight W = 3A + 4B + 3C drops by exactly 2 at every firing, so a start's weight caps its number of firings.
- **The finish does not depend on the order** (the abelian property). Every complete run from a start ends at the same board and fires each circle the same number of times. Children meet this by finding every order (puzzles 1, 3, 4, 6, 11) and seeing one finish. Only the letters' arrangement changes; how many of each letter does not.
- **Invariants decide which starts reach a finish.** On the triangle, firing A or B changes A − B by 3, so A − B keeps its remainder after division by 3. Puzzles 5 and 9 ask for every start with a given total that finishes like the card; the answers are exactly the starts with the right remainder.
- **Finishes of big starts form a small set.** With enough chips, the finishes on the triangle are the three boards (1,1), (1,0), (0,1), and on the square the four boards with at most one empty circle (puzzles 2 and 8). These are the recurrent configurations; under addition followed by firing they form the sandpile group (order 3 and order 4 here, the number of spanning trees).
- **Without a sink, firing can go on forever.** On a closed triangle, 3 chips placed 2, 1, 0 in any arrangement cycle forever, while 3 chips on one circle stop after one firing (puzzle 12).
- **Avalanches.** On a still board, one extra chip can set off a long run. On the square the largest is four firings, from 1, 1, 1 with the new chip on B (puzzle 7).
- **Capacity.** A board is **still** when no circle can fire. A still circle holds at most one chip fewer than its lines, so a still board holds at most the sum of (lines − 1) over its circles, and only the board with every circle at that limit holds that many. On the square with a diagonal A and C have three lines and B two, so the most is 2 + 1 + 2 = 5, on A 2, B 1, C 2 alone (Still boards 1; the guide's further question, p. 12). The bound is a certificate for "the most": it covers every arrangement at once. Without the diagonal every circle has two lines and the most is 3 (K–1 Problem 4's "Can any of these ways use 4 chips?").
- **The add-one cycle.** Adding one chip at A and firing until still is a map on the still boards. On the triangle it sends A 0 B 0 → A 1 B 0 → A 0 B 1 → A 1 B 1 → A 1 B 0: three boards go round a loop and come back every three chips, and the empty board leads into the loop and never comes back (Still boards 2). A 0 B 0 and A 1 B 1 both go to A 1 B 0, the only two still boards that merge; this is where the empty start joins the loop, and since each board on the loop already has the one before it on the loop, a run that has merged can never get off it. The boards that come back are the recurrent configurations (the finishes of puzzle 2), adding at A permutes them as a generator of the sandpile group (cyclic of order 3), and the empty board is transient. The limit: this is one board and one adding circle; on the square, adding at A, four boards come back and four do not.

Experiments come first: a child fires, records the order, and looks at the finish. Conjectures follow (“it always ends the same”, “three boards keep coming round”), and explanations are for older or keener children with a grown-up: the weight argument for stopping, the remainder argument for the triangle starts, one chip short of the lines at every circle for capacity, and the merge for a start that never comes back. The puzzles never ask for a written proof; the two Still boards claims are checked with a certificate on the board.

## Where they appear

**Puzzles → Chip firing** is a satchel family on the family seam ([Adding a family](../ADDING-A-FAMILY.md)): the newest such family comes first and starts open. A **Playground** button sits above Easy, Medium and Hard, and **Still boards** follows Hard as a group of its own, with mechanic `chipstill` (puzzle ids `chips-s01`, `chips-s02`). The puzzles are grade-free (`band: "all"`) and use Easy/Medium/Hard like the other expansion families, so K–1 children can start at puzzle 1 and older children can go straight to Medium.

The two Still boards puzzles join as a group rather than replacing any of the twelve. The card notes that five of the twelve (1, 3, 4, 6, 11) enumerate firing orders, but removing a puzzle from the pack makes an old save that holds it fail to load (`dist/storage.js` rejects a saved puzzle the pack doesn't have), and reusing its id for a new puzzle would make its save invalid. So every existing id, revision and save is unchanged.

## How a puzzle plays

- **Placing.** Puzzles that start empty show a tray of chips. Tap a circle to put one there. Firing waits until the tray is empty, so a start is always complete before the run.
- **Firing.** A circle that can fire has a gold ring and a gentle glow. Tap it to fire; chips fly along the lines. The letters fired so far show below the board, with a count in firing and avalanche puzzles.
- **New run.** The primary board control returns to the start (or to an empty tray) and keeps everything found so far. Undo takes back one move and also keeps discoveries. Restart clears the collection.
- **What was found.** Puzzles that ask for every answer show only the orders, finishes or starts already found, with no empty slots and no count. Every complete run shelves its order or finish without a verdict (in puzzles 5 and 9 a start is shelved when its finish matches the card, which the board shows). **That’s all** judges the whole shelf once: it is off while the shelf is empty; an incomplete shelf gets "There is another." and That's all stays off until a new answer joins the shelf. The line shows on the board the claim made: the next move, or the Undo of the claim press, takes it away, and Undo back onto that board shows it again, so each Undo changes the screen. That's all stays off through Undo and New run. A run that ends without counting in puzzles 5, 7, 9 and 10 (a finish unlike the card, or too few firings) turns the goal card or the count red.
- **Avalanche (puzzle 7).** Chips can be added while nothing can fire. The chip that first makes a circle ready is the extra chip; after it, only firing is allowed.
- **Loop (puzzle 12).** The board has no sink. If a run comes back to a board it has already shown, a ↻ appears and the puzzle is solved.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Otherwise hints name a move toward an answer not yet found (“Fire C. An order you haven’t found yet goes this way.”), and the third level offers Apply hint. Hints can lead all the way to a solve, as in the other expansion families.

## Still boards

- **Room for chips (capacity).** The square with a diagonal starts empty. Tap a circle to add a chip; a circle glows once it has a chip for each line, and a tap on a glowing circle fires it (chips can be added anywhere that doesn't glow, up to 30 on the circles; a game can go on as long as a child likes). **Most** is off until the board has chips and nothing glows. It claims that no still board holds more chips. A wrong claim gets "More chips fit." beside Most, and Most stays off until the board changes; Undo takes the line away but keeps Most off. A right claim draws the argument on the board: at each circle a sun dot sits on one line end for each chip and one line end is left as a dashed ring, captioned with the conclusion, "One more chip on any circle would make it glow." The objective is shown ("Put as many chips on the circles as you can with nothing glowing.") because the board can't say "the most".
- **Comes back (the add-one cycle).** The triangle starts empty. Put chips on the circles for a start (tap a glowing circle to fire it), then tap **+ ● A** to add a chip at A; from then on circles only fire, and + ● A is off while one glows. After each chip, once nothing glows, the board joins the row under the board as a small picture, start first. When a still board repeats, the run ends with ↻; if it is the start, the start joins the shelf of starts that came back (each with ↻). New run clears the board and keeps the shelf. + ● A, New run and **That's all** share one line under the board. That's all is off until the shelf has a start and the run has ended. Pressing it turns the boards in the row into buttons (keyboard focus moves to the row itself, not to a board in it) and the objective line into "Tap two different boards that go to the same board."; the second board tapped makes the claim, judged once: two different boards that go to the same board (a chip at A, then still) are the certificate, and with every start that comes back on the shelf the puzzle is solved. The same board tapped twice (A 1 B 0 and its repeat at the end of the row) is not a claim: "Those are the same board." and the picking goes on. Otherwise one refusal: "Those two don’t go to the same board." (That's all is off for that row) or, for a right pair and an incomplete shelf, "There is another." (off until the shelf grows). The line shows on the board the claim made; the Undo of the claim press takes it away and keeps the lock, and Undo back onto that board (past New run) shows it again. A refused pair's lock belongs to its row: it stands while Undo stays in the row or goes back to it past New run, and goes when Undo leaves the row for the placing of a start. A solved row rings the two boards in pine and the board after each in ochre, captioned "The start runs into the loop and never comes back." The objective sits on the board in place of the usual line so that one line is shown at a time.
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge. Then: capacity fires what glows, then adds a chip to a circle short of its limit ("Put a chip on C."), then "Every circle has one chip fewer than its lines. Press Most." (the reason before the claim); the cycle builds a start not yet shelved, adds at A and fires, plays out any run whose start isn't saved, says "Press New run" otherwise, and once the shelf is full runs from no chips and gives the claim ("Press That’s all, then tap the two different boards that go to the same board."). Level 2 marks the circle, + ● A, New run, That's all or the two boards; level 3 offers Apply hint. Hints alone solve both puzzles from fresh, from random play and after each refusal.

## Certificates and claims

- **Capacity: the argument is drawn, not tapped.** The certificate is one fact per circle (it holds at most one chip fewer than its lines), and every circle is part of it. On the only board a right claim can be made on, every circle is at its limit, so any tap certificate would be "tap every circle", which can't fail; pairing chips with lines can't fail either, since a still circle always has a line left over. So the child's part is the attempt (a still board) and the claim (Most), which can fail ("More chips fit." on each of the 16 other still boards with chips, checked in the validator; Most is off on the empty board), and the app draws the argument after a right claim.
- **Comes back: the certificate is tapped.** That's all claims that no other still start comes back. On the triangle the one that doesn't is the empty board, and the reason is in its own row: it runs into the loop where two boards go to the same board. The child taps those two boards in a row they made, after an attempt (a finished run). Of the 18 ordered pairs of different boards in that row, 2 pass (11%); a row from a start that comes back has none (the validator counts both). Nothing marks the right boards before they are tapped, and one wrong pair costs a whole new run. This is why the card's "two that merge" is part of this puzzle and not a second one: the merge is the certificate for the start that never comes back, and on this board there is exactly one such start and one merging pair, so a separate merge puzzle would ask the same fact again.
- **Refusals lock; their lines stay on their own board.** Undo never takes back the lock of a refused Most, That's all or pair. The refusal's line shows only on the board the claim made: the Undo of the claim press takes it away, and Undo back onto that board shows it again, so each Undo changes the screen. After a refusal the shelf has since outgrown, the board the claim made draws just like the one before it, and Undo passes over that step (`undoPress` in `dist/engine.js`). No refusal prints a count.
- **No answer in the copy.** Titles ("Room for chips", "Comes back"), objectives, How to play and first hints name no count and no board that is an answer; the validator checks the titles, objectives, controls and first hints.

## The playground

The playground has no goal, no Hint and never counts as solved.

- **Boards:** triangle, square, square with a diagonal, kite (three circles joined to each other and to a sink in the middle), star, ring of four, the closed triangle, and a 25 × 25 grid.
- **Tools:** Add (tap a circle to add a chip), Fire (tap a ready circle), **Fire all** (fire until nothing can, or until a board repeats on the closed triangle, shown by ↻), and Clear. The sink shows its total.
- **Grid:** every square is a circle with four lines; the edge is the sink. Tap a square to add 1, 10, 100 or 1000 chips; the grid then fires in waves until it is still, with colors for 0–3 chips and gold for squares about to fire. 1000 chips on the center square take 161 waves and 18,226 firings and leave the familiar sandpile fractal. Arrow keys and Enter work too.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- Only legal moves are accepted: no firing a circle that is short of chips, no firing during placing, no adding in order puzzles, and no moves after a solve. In Still boards: no chip onto a glowing circle, no placing once a chip has been added at A, no chip at A while a circle glows or after the run ends, no claim before an attempt, and no second claim on the same board, row or shelf.
- Saved boards of the twelve puzzles are replayed from their placed chips and fired letters, so their piles are always derived, never stored (Still boards store theirs; see below). A save whose found list holds anything that is not an answer to that puzzle is rejected.
- New exhaustive collections store their completeness claim. Older completed saves retain their count-based completion, so this interaction change preserves their progress and puzzle revisions. A stale "There is another." on a full shelf, which older saves can hold, does not lock That's all.
- Undo keeps discoveries and locks through the `carry` hook (applied by `undoPress` in `engine.js`, which `main.js` calls), and keeps a refusal's line (`told`) only on the board the claim made. Restart clears them.
- Still boards saves hold the piles and the sink count as they are (so a long game never fills a step record), the claim, `told` while a refusal's line shows, and in the cycle the start's run as steps (+ adds a chip at A, a capital letter fires), the shelf and the two boards of a solve. Saved boards are replayed and checked: piles within the 30-chip cap and a state play can reach (if anything has fired, because the sink holds chips or a circle holds more chips than lines, then the circles next to the last circle that fired each hold a chip; the validator confirms every board play reaches with up to 8 chips in the sink is accepted), legal steps from a still start, a claim only as the app would judge it on that board (Most right only on the fullest still board), a shelf of starts that come back and that holds the start of a row that came back, a refusal of the shelf only while it is incomplete, and a solve only with the full shelf and two boards that merge in the row.

Two small hooks were added for this family and are available to any mechanic: `carry(p, from, to)` lets Undo keep part of the board, and `noHint(p)` hides the Hint button (`ui.js`). The pack is additive, like the proofs pack, so existing puzzles and their saves are unchanged. The family's one line in `dist/families.js` brings in its mechanic, pack, stylesheet, symbol and focus target; `tests/chips.test.mjs` and `node scripts/families-browser-smoke.mjs` (which solves all twelve puzzles through hints) check it.

To change a puzzle, edit `scripts/build-chips.mjs`, then run `node scripts/build-chips.mjs`, `npm test` and `npm run build`.

## Not yet done

- **Children’s play.** Nothing here has been tried by children. Things to watch: whether K–1 children read the letter record or just the board, whether **New run** is found without help, and whether the saved pictures make it clear that a finish has already been found. In Still boards: whether children tell the row (this run) from the shelf (starts that came back), whether "Tap two different boards that go to the same board" is understood or tapped at random (each wrong pair costs a run), and whether Most is pressed as a guess after the first glow.
- **Phone height.** On a 375 × 667 phone the card and the Undo row fit one screen on the busiest states, but the app's header comes first, so at the top of the page the Undo row of the cycle (and, after a refusal, of capacity) starts below the fold until the page is scrolled.
- **Five order puzzles.** The card notes that puzzles 1, 3, 4, 6 and 11 all enumerate firing orders. They stay, because removing a puzzle breaks saves; a later revision could rework one of them in place.
- **Not built from the card.** K–1 Problem 4's "find every still board" (8 on the square, 18 with the diagonal), adding at B (2–3 Problem 6's second rule), the add-one cycle on the square (four come back, four don't, two merges), and batch timing (K–1 Problem 5, a prediction, left to the playground).
- **Story.** Chip firing is not on the Lantern Road and has no keeper or companion lines.
- **Grade levels.** The family is grade-free; it does not yet appear in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
- **Proofs.** The weight and remainder arguments live in the grown-up notes. A later proof puzzle could ask a child to show that a start cannot reach a finish, using the remainder, in the style of the Proofs groups.
