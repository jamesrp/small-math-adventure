# Three-arm lines

October 17, 2026. Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 78 of the Bellingham math circle (three-armed lines, tropical lines). They are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the worksheet itself has not been piloted (its guide, p. 1: "Physical rehearsal and classroom piloting have not been performed"). The theme's [review card](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/blob/main/plans/review/week-78.md) says keep, and its decision of October 10 shaped this family: a new satchel family, with the placement checked only when the child submits, so it is never sliding until the stars light; place one junction so the line passes every target; mark the ray of junctions for aligned targets and declare done; claim "can't" for three targets, with each target's fan of allowed junctions as the certificate; place a second line to meet the first in a named point or ray; arms run past the window, and there is no rotate handle.

The packet was revised on October 15, after the card. Problem 1 now asks for one single-point meeting and one whole-stretch overlap on the top grids (puzzles 1 and 2 here), and page 1 gained a worked example, blue U = (1, 4) and orange V = (4, 2) sharing only (4, 4) (the playground's starting lines and the second How to play picture). Problems 1–7 are otherwise the same, and the decision still fits.

## The mathematics

A **line** with junction J = (a, b) is the set of points (x, y) where the least of x − a, y − b and 0 occurs at least twice (the min-plus convention, the worksheet's page 1). The three ties give its three closed **arms** from J: right (a + t, b), up (a, b + t) and down to the left along the rising diagonal (a − t, b − t), for every t ≥ 0. Every line is this one shape, moved without turning; the arms run on for ever. Two lines **share** a point when it is on both. Everything below holds for all real points; the puzzles use a window of the plane and whole or half squares for junctions.

- **Two lines always meet (puzzles 1, 2, 5, 6, 10; worksheet Problems 1–3 and 6).** Two lines with junctions A ≠ B share exactly one point, unless A and B share a column, a row or a rising diagonal. Then they share a closed ray and nothing else: the up arm of the higher junction, the right arm of the right-hand one, or the slanting arm of the lower one; the stretch between the two junctions is not shared. (Same junction: the same line.) So there are no parallel lines and no two lines share exactly two points. Where they meet: if one junction is up and to the left of the other, its right arm crosses the other's up arm at the far corner of their rectangle, (B's x, A's y). If B is up and to the right of A, B's slanting arm comes down to A's line: to A's right arm when the rectangle is wider than tall, at (B's x − h, A's y), and to A's up arm when it is taller, at (A's x, B's y − w), with w and h the rectangle's width and height. Every meeting point of two half-square junctions is a half-square point, because the arms run along rows, columns and rising diagonals.
- **Fans (puzzles 3, 4, 7–9, 11, 12; Problems 4 and 5).** The junctions J whose line passes a point T fill T's **fan**: the closed rays from T to the left, down, and up and to the right. (T is on J's right arm exactly when J is left of T on its row, and so on.) The fan is the line shape turned half a turn, so two fans meet exactly as two lines do, turned round: two different points lie on **exactly one line**, unless they share a row, a column or a rising diagonal; then the junctions form a closed ray: left from the left-hand point (a row), down from the lower point (a column), or up and to the right from the upper point (a diagonal). That ray runs past the board and holds every point between grid dots.
- **Three points (puzzles 11 and 12).** Three points lie on one line exactly when their three fans share a point. If two of them, P and Q, are not on one row, column or rising diagonal, their fans meet in one junction X, and the three points lie on a line exactly when the third is on the line from X. The **certificate** that no line passes all three is then two circles whose fans leave one junction, and that junction's line missing the third circle. In puzzle 12, P = (2, 2) and Q = (7, 5) leave only (5, 5), whose arms run along row 5, column 5 and the diagonal through (2, 2), and R = (6, 6) is on none of them. Q and R leave only (6, 5), whose line misses P: a second certificate. P and R share a rising diagonal, so they allow a whole ray and certify nothing. In puzzle 11, R = (5, 8) is on the line from (5, 5), so a line exists and Can't is wrong.

**What children are meant to discover.** Experiments come first: move B or the junction and press Check, slide marks and press That's all. The conjectures: two lines meet in one point, or share a whole stretch when the junctions line up; the stretch starts at the higher, right-hand or lower junction, never between them; two circles fix one line unless they line up, and then a whole ray of junctions works, past the edge and between dots; three circles usually can't share a line. The explanations (each circle must be on one of the three arms, so the junction is left of it, below it or up and to the right; two such fans meet in one point) are grown-up conversations in each puzzle's notes. The worksheet's written questions (Problem 3's "exactly two points?", Problem 6's rule and Problem 7's rule) stay on paper, as the card decided.

**Grade bands.** The packet is grades 4–5 only; the app's puzzles are grade-free. As a rough map: puzzles 1–4 need no reading, with a grown-up reading the goal: move a shape without turning it and look along its arms (a K–1 or 2–3 entry point). Puzzles 5–8 add the slanting arm and finding every junction on a row or column. Puzzles 9–12 match the packet: half squares, a slanting ray of junctions, and three circles with Can't. Easy, Medium and Hard have four puzzles each. The playground fits every band.

These are **tropical lines** in the min-plus convention (David Speyer and Bernd Sturmfels, *Tropical Mathematics*, 2004, §1, pp. 1–2, the repeated minimum, and §3, pp. 6–8, tropical lines, their meeting and the line through two points; Ayush Kumar Tewari, *Point-Line Geometry in the Tropical Plane*, 2020, §§3–4, pp. 3 and 5–8, incidence and duality, in the max-plus convention, which turns the arms round).

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Graph paper with the frame in ink, whole-square lines in grey-green and half-square lines faint where junctions go on half squares. Every line is drawn as its three arms with an arrowhead in the margin past the frame, so the arms are seen running on; there is no rotate handle, and no line can be turned. A is sky blue, B dashed orange (so a shared stretch shows both), and the child's junction in puzzles 3, 4, 7–9, 11 and 12 is ink. Circles are open, with an italic letter up and to the left (no line through a point runs that way). A ring goal is a sun disc with a dashed edge; a stretch goal is a broad sun band along one of A's arms, from A's junction to past the frame.
- **Moves.** Tap where grid lines cross to move the junction there, or drag it (one move, one Undo; the line follows the finger while dragging and nothing is judged). On a touch screen a dragged junction rides 1.2 squares above the finger, and at least 36 px. A tap a little off a crossing goes to the nearest one; a tap on a circle's letter goes to the circle, never to the crossing under the letter. Arrow keys move the junction a step. In the playground, a tap on A or B chooses it, and Enter switches.
- **Check (meet and through puzzles).** Nothing is judged while a junction moves. Check judges the placement: right, and the puzzle is solved, with what the lines share drawn in pine (or the circles ringed). Wrong, and the board draws what the lines really share, in ochre, with one short line: "They meet somewhere else.", "They share a whole stretch.", "They share only one point.", "They share a different stretch." or "They share everything."; in the through puzzles the missed circles are ringed with "The line misses Q." Check then stays off until the junction moves, and Undo keeps it off (it takes back the words). Moving away and back allows another Check, so a child can sweep the board Check by Check. The validator counts how many Checks a sweep needs before the first answer (the start excluded), in four orders: page order (top row first, left to right), bottom row first, nearest the drawn goal first (the ring, or A for a stretch; ties in page order) and the mean over random orders, (n + 1)/(k + 1) for k answers among n places:

  | Puzzle | Places | Answers | Page order | Bottom up | Nearest goal | Random (mean) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 80 | 6 | 26 | 7 | 5 | 11.6 |
  | 2 | 80 | 4 | 37 | 36 | 3 | 16.2 |
  | 5 | 80 | 4 | 9 | 50 | 7 | 16.2 |
  | 6 | 80 | 7 | 8 | 27 | 3 | 10.1 |
  | 10 | 168 | 2 | 143 | 2 | 3 | 56.3 |

  Every nearest-goal sweep needs at least two Checks (the validator asserts it). Puzzle 10's bottom-up sweep finds (½, 0) on its second Check, because that corner answer is in the bottom row.
- **Marking (puzzles 7–9).** Keep marks the point where the junction is; Drop takes off every mark through that point. A slide from a point marks every point along the way to where the finger stops (whole steps along a row, column or diagonal, the nearest of eight directions), and a slide on off the board makes the mark a ray running for ever. With a keyboard, Shift and an arrow key mark a ray from the junction (Home, Page Up, End and Page Down for the diagonals). Marks are broad leaf-green bands; a mark being drawn is lighter.
- **Copy.** One objective above the board: "Make the two lines share only the ringed point." (1, 6, 10), "Make the two lines share exactly the shaded stretch." (2, 5), "Make the line pass through every circle." (3, 4, 11, 12) and "Mark every junction whose line passes both circles." (7–9). How to play opens with the mathematics ("A line is three arms from one junction: right, up, and down to the left along the diagonal, each running on for ever. Every line is the same shape, moved without turning."), then the gestures, then two pictures from page 1 (P on the line and Q off it; U and V sharing one point, in the meet puzzles and the playground) and the rules: the arms go on past the edge, points between grid dots count, and for Can't "If no line can, press Can't and tap two circles that show why.", which doesn't say which two (the rules list adds how the claim is judged: the line from the child's junction must pass both circles, be the only line through both, and miss another circle).
- **Hints.** Before any move, the first hint is the authored nudge, which never names the answer ("Follow A's arm through the ring. Which arm of B could reach the ring another way?"). After a move, the card names the step for the board as it stands: which way B goes from the ring ("Put B below the ring."), the idea for a stretch ("Two junctions in one row share the right arm of the right-hand one."), where the junction goes from two circles ("Put the junction below P and left of Q."), the ray to mark ("Every junction further left works too, between dots and past the edge. Slide from P left, off the board."), or "Press Check." and "Press That's all." The second level rings the place on the board with a larger dotted ochre ring (a stretch to mark is dotted too), unlike the dashed rings of Can't and of a refusal, and the screen reader hears it in coordinates; the third offers Apply hint. In puzzle 12 the hints lead to an attempt at a certificate's junction first, and then give the reason with Can't: "Only this junction's line passes P and Q, and it misses R. Press Can't, then tap P and Q."; Can't is never the first hint. After a refused claim the hints go straight to the right pair. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after a wrong move, after random play, after a refused Can't and after a refused That's all. Each hint is remembered per board; the slowest, cold, takes under 10 ms of CPU.
- **Phone size.** The board is at most 380 px and reaches the play panel's edges on a phone, and it shrinks on a short screen so that the board, the line of words under it and the button bar fit under the header without scrolling (the board's top is measured when it is drawn; it is never smaller than 220 px). Measured at 375 × 667 with a nickname that wraps the header onto a second row ("Isabella", or the smoke's "Tropical QA"): the board is 235 px, whole squares are 24.5 px apart, the 6-square boards of puzzles 9 and 10 have 30.9 px squares and 15.5 px half squares, and the button bar ends at 630 px. With a short nickname ("Look") the board is 291 px (30.3 px squares, 19.2 px half squares); at 390 × 844 it is 354 px (36.9 px squares, 23.3 px half squares); on a 1024 × 768 or 1280 × 720 desktop it is 380 px with 39.6 px squares, and the bar ends at 677 or 682 px. The phone smoke checks that the board, the words and the bar are on screen in puzzle 12 with Can't pressed and with a refusal showing, and in puzzle 8 with a refusal showing; a slide on the board never scrolls the page.
- **Focus.** A click or tap on the board gives the board focus, so the arrow keys work straight after (the smoke checks this after a click and after a tap). After a move, focus lands on the board or the move buttons (or, after a solve, the completion heading); a refused Check, Can't or That's all goes grey, and focus falls back to the board. The family's focus selector names only the live board (`[data-tr-board]`), never a How to play picture. Focus never lands on Can't or That's all after a move.

## Certificates and claims

- **Can't (puzzles 11 and 12).** Can't is greyed until the junction has moved off its start, so a claim is drawn round the child's own attempt. Press Can't (it stays pressed, and "Tap two circles." appears) and tap two circles; every circle gets the same dashed tap ring, none is marked, and a tap away from the circles moves the junction instead and lets go of Can't (with a keyboard, Tab reaches the rings and Enter or Space taps). The claim is judged once, when the second circle is in. It is right when both circles are on the child's line, they are not on one row, column or rising diagonal (so their fans meet only at this junction), and the line misses the third circle. Then the puzzle is solved and the app draws the argument: the two circles' fans dashed in pine, the third circle's fan faint, the junction ringed in pine and the missed circle ringed in ochre. A wrong claim gets one short refusal and clears the taps: "The line misses R." (a picked circle is off the line), "Other junctions pass P and R too." (an aligned pair) or "There is a way." (the line passes all three; puzzle 11 only). A refusal locks that pair at that junction, not the junction: Can't stays available for the other pairs, and the same pair again only repeats its words. The refused pairs are kept until the junction moves, also across Undo (the `carry` hook), so a pair can't be asked about twice at one place, and no detour is needed to try a different pair. The validator counts how often random play finishes the certificate in puzzle 12: with the junction at any of the 81 crossings and any of the three pairs, 2 of 243 (0.8%); even at a certificate's junction, one pair in three.
- **Why the child taps circles and the app draws the fans.** The certificate is the pair of circles together with the child's junction; the fans are the argument for why no other junction can pass that pair, and they are drawn after a right claim and explained in the grown-up notes.
- **That's all (puzzles 7–9).** The shelf of marks is judged as a whole, once: right when the marks together are exactly the junctions that work, every point of the ray (between dots and past the edge) and nothing else. Otherwise one short line: "This one doesn't work." (the first mark holding a junction that fails: that mark turns ochre, the line from one such junction on it is drawn dashed in ochre, and a circle that line misses is ringed), or "There are more." There is no per-mark verdict, no slot per answer and no count. That's all stays off until the marks change, and Undo keeps it off.

## The playground

Two lines from the worksheet's page 1 example: A at (1, 4) and B at (4, 2), sharing only (4, 4). Both are movable, by a tap (on A or B to choose, then on a crossing) or a drag, and what they share is always drawn in pine, redrawn while dragging: a ringed point, a thick ray, or every arm when the junctions coincide. There is no goal, no Check and no Hint, and it never counts as solved. Its notes suggest putting B on A's row and in A's column, trying to make the two lines miss each other (they can't: Problem 6's question), and Problem 6's pair, which meets off the board.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the junction's place and the record of claims: in the meet puzzles whether Check was refused there and whether its words are showing; in the through puzzles whether the junction has moved (an attempt), a refused Check, the list of pairs refused at this place, the pair whose words are showing, and the right pair; in the find-every puzzles the marks, the shelf That's all refused and whether its words are showing; in the playground, the two junctions. Everything shown is derived from it.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a junction to a different crossing inside the board; Check once per place, and not in the find-every puzzles; Can't only in puzzles 11 and 12, after an attempt, with two different circles (a pair already refused here only repeats its words); Keep where no mark covers the junction, Drop where one does, a mark along one of eight directions inside the board (a ray may run on), at most 24 marks and no doubles; That's all only with marks that differ from the refused shelf; nothing after a solve. In the playground, only the two junctions move.
- Saved boards are checked: whole numbers in half squares on the board's crossings; no attempt unless the junction has moved; a refused Check only where it is wrong; refused pairs only after an attempt, each a pair that does not certify here, in order and without doubles, with words only for one of them; a right claim only of a certifying pair at its junction, with done; done only where the placement is right or the shelf is exactly the ray; refusal words only with their refusal; no unknown fields.
- **Exact arithmetic.** Points are stored in half squares, so every junction, circle, meeting point and fan crossing has whole-number coordinates, and every test is a comparison of whole numbers (`dist/families/tropical/lines.js`).
- The answers are computed by `scripts/build-tropical.mjs` with the mechanic's geometry and checked again by `scripts/validate-tropical.mjs` from the definition.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the worksheet has not been piloted. Three things to watch: whether children keep the two gestures apart in puzzles 7–9, where a slide marks a stretch, while everywhere else a drag moves the junction (a stray slide makes a mark, which Drop or Undo takes off); whether Check in the meet puzzles becomes a sweep, Check after Check across the board (each Check judges only that place and stays off until B moves, but moving away and back allows another; the sweep counts are under Check above), rather than reasoning from which arm reaches the ring or lies along the stretch; and whether Can't in puzzle 12 is found by noticing that two circles leave one junction, or by trying pairs at many junctions (random play rarely finishes it, 0.8%, but a patient child could).
