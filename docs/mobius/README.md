Twelve puzzles and a playground built from Week 38 of the Bellingham math circle (seams and cuts): the base packets' ring pictures on bands A and B (K–1 and grades 2–3 Problems 2, 3 and 6, grades 4–5 Problems 5–7), the review card's B cut a third of the way across (its fix 3), and the bonus page's lanes, circles of strips, and holes and notches (bonus Problems 1–3). The family symbol is ∾. The puzzles are in the puzzle satchel only, not on the Lantern Road. Nothing here has been played by children, and the Week 38 worksheet is unpiloted.

The family runs on the shared [portal board](../portal-board.md), extended for it with a seam that reverses (see [Changes to the shared board](#changes-to-the-shared-board)). Puzzles 6, 10, 11 and 12 are new; each puzzle's `provenance` names its source.

## The mathematics

A **band** is made of strips: one strip, or a circle of three or four. Each strip's left end has • at the top and □ at the bottom, as on the worksheet. A **seam** joins a strip's right end to the next strip's left end (the last strip's to the first's): **M** joins • to • and □ to □ straight across (height y goes to y), **R** joins them after a half-turn (y goes to n − y, the paper turned over). One strip with M is band A, an annulus; with R it is band B, a Möbius band. The edges of the paper are the top and bottom of each strip and both sides of every cut. A **closed edge** is an edge followed through the seams until it is back where it started: a cycle of the seam maps on edge lines.

1. **Parity.** An uncut band has two closed edges when an even number of its seams are R, and one when the number is odd. Following the top edge of strip 1, each R seam moves the trip to the other side (top to bottom or back), so after one lap it is on the top again exactly when the count is even; if it is on the bottom, it carries on and closes after a second lap, so there is only one closed edge. An arrow carried once round along the middle comes home flipped exactly when the count is odd (on paper it comes home on the other face, which is the one-sidedness). In a circle of strips an edge on top of strip 1 is on the bottom of strip k exactly when an odd number of the seams before strip k are R.
2. **Ring pictures.** Four dots on the edges can group like a ring picture (dots in one ring on one closed edge, different rings on different closed edges, other closed edges empty) exactly when the picture has no more rings than the band has closed edges. With enough closed edges, give each ring its own edge; in every dots puzzle each edge line has at least eight places, so any ring fits. With too few, two rings would share a closed edge (pigeonhole). With b closed edges the pictures that work are the partitions of 4 into at most b parts: on A (b = 2) 4, 3 + 1 and 2 + 2; on B (b = 1) only 4. The certificate for Can't is one tap on each closed edge, none twice: fewer taps than rings.
3. **Cuts.** A cut along a line runs round through the seams until it closes, so on B a cut at height h comes back at height n − h. The cuts divide the strip into lanes, and the seams map lanes to lanes (on R, lane [a, b] to [n − b, n − a], turned over). The pieces are the cycles of that map. A lane an R seam takes to itself is a Möbius band, one closed edge; two lanes it swaps are one annulus, two closed edges; under M every lane is its own annulus. So the middle cut leaves A in two annuli (four closed edges) and B in one annulus whose two closed edges are the old edge and the cut; a cut a third of the way across B leaves a Möbius band (its edge is the inner sides of the cut) beside an annulus (the old edge and the outer sides): three closed edges, and pieces with one and two.
4. **Holes and notches.** A small hole inside a piece adds one closed edge to that piece and leaves it in one piece; a notch bitten out of an edge only bends that edge and adds none. (Each hole lowers the Euler characteristic by one and adds a boundary circle.) So a band with four closed edges in one piece is M with two holes or R with three.
5. **What decides a seam.** Two marks on edge lines of a circle of strips are on one closed edge when the seams between them turn over the right number of times, and the two kinds of mark are on different closed edges only when the band has two closed edges, an even number of R seams. Marks fixed on particular edges therefore pin the seams one by one, and the parity pins the last.

**Experiments, conjectures, explanations.** The experiments are placing dots and watching which edges light, lighting edges through seams, changing seams, cutting, and punching holes. The conjectures come from them: B has only one edge; a cut makes new edges on both its sides; the middle cut of B stays in one piece; the number of R seams decides the number of edges; a hole always adds an edge and a notch never does. The explanations are the Can't certificate on screen (each closed edge tapped once, fewer than the rings) and the grown-up notes: the trip that changes sides at each R, the lanes as cycles, and the hole count.

**Grade bands.** The puzzles are grade-free. Puzzles 1–5 need following an edge with a finger and grouping four dots by colour (K–1 Problems 2, 3 and 6, with a grown-up reading the goal). Puzzles 6–8 add seams and cuts to change, and counting closed edges to four (grades 2–3 and the bonus). Puzzles 9–12 go past the base packets: B in thirds, four strips with a fixed seam, three lines on one strip, and cuts with holes (grades 4–5 and the bonus).

## How a puzzle plays

- **The board.** Each strip is a rectangle with • and □ at its ends; at the right end they are the same way up for M and swapped for R, and the seam's letter sits between them. The paper's edges are thick ink. A line the child may cut is dashed, with a ✂ handle at the left; a cut is a gap between two ink lines. A place for a hole is a dashed ring and a punched hole a white disc with an ink rim; a notch is a dashed half disc on an edge. In a circle of strips the strips are laid in two rows and numbered, each with the seam after it at its right end.
- **Dots (puzzles 1–5, 9).** Each edge line is divided into places, one per lattice step; tap a place to put a dot there, and tap a dot to take it off (four at most). A dot lights its whole closed edge, through the seam, and dots on one closed edge share a colour, in the order the closed edges get their first dot. The goal is a ring picture: rings of dots above the band. Four dots that group like the rings solve the puzzle, and the rings then take the closed edges' colours.
- **Can't.** Can't stays off until four dots are down that don't match the rings, so a claim is always about the child's own attempt. Pressing it hides the colours (every dot turns plain, so the taps can't be read off the colours) and turns every place into a control; the child taps places, which get flags (a second tap takes a flag off), and presses **That's all**, which judges the taps once:
  1. **The certificate.** The taps must be on different closed edges and cover them all. Otherwise "Tap each closed edge once." (the same words on every puzzle, which say nothing about whether the picture can be made), the taps clear and Can't stays open.
  2. **The puzzle.** A whole certificate solves the puzzle when the picture can't be made: every closed edge lights in its colour, the rings take those colours, and the rings left over are crossed out. When the picture can be made, the answer is "There is a way.": the flags stay drawn, and Can't stays off until a dot moves. Undo straight after takes back the flags and keeps the words; the next Undo takes back a dot and lifts it. (Puzzles 1, 3 and 4 can reach this.)
- **Why the child supplies the certificate.** The fact that can be pointed at is the closed edges themselves: one place on each. The rest, that four dots in fewer closed edges than rings can't make the picture, is counting, so the app draws it after a right claim (the rings crossed out). Puzzle 2's certificate is a single tap, because B has one closed edge; the choice there is to treat the top and the bottom as one closed edge, and a child who taps both, as two edges, is refused. The colours of the dots before Can't show the same thing, which is the point of puzzle 2.
- **Lamps (puzzles 6–8, 10–12, the playground).** Tap an edge to light its whole closed edge in a colour, and again to turn it off. A lamp keeps its colour while others go out. Lamps are not moves: Undo doesn't touch them, and they are not saved. After a seam change a lamp lights the new closed edge through its line; a lamp on an edge that goes (a cut taped back, a hole filled) goes out.
- **Seams (puzzles 6 and 10).** Tap a seam's letter to change it between M and R. A fixed seam has a padlock and no control (seam 3 of puzzle 10). The goal picture is a ring of two stars and a ring of two diamonds; the marks sit on particular edges of particular strips. The puzzle is solved when the stars share a closed edge, the diamonds share another, and the two closed edges differ. The arrow button draws an arrow carried once round along the middle of the strips, with the arrow on strip 1 as it comes home (flipped exactly when the number of R seams is odd); it is drawn on every solved seam puzzle.
- **Pieces (puzzles 7, 8, 11, 12).** Tap the seam's letter to change it; tap a dashed line or its ✂ to cut it, and the ✂ again to tape the cut back; tap a dashed ring to punch it out, and again to fill it. On B a cut at one height is cut at the other height too, since it runs on round; while the band is cut the seam has a padlock, because turning it over would change the cut (tape the cut back to change it). Each piece is tinted its own colour. Puzzle 8 shows pips for the holes allowed (three; two is the fewest that works). The goal is a row of cards, one per piece, each with a small ring per closed edge. The pieces must match the cards exactly.
- **Copy.** One sentence per kind: "Put four dots on the edges to match the rings.", "Change seams so the marks group like the rings." and "Make pieces like these." The joining rule, closed edges, dots, Can't, cuts and holes are in How to play, which opens with the definition: "A band is a strip with its ends joined: M joins • to • and □ to □ straight across, R joins them after a half-turn. A closed edge is an edge you can follow round until you are back where you started." Refusals are "Tap each closed edge once." and "There is a way." No count of closed edges or pieces is shown. The screen-reader status reads the seams, the dots grouped by closed edge (what the colours show), the number of lit lamps and the number of pieces (what the tints show).
- **Hints.** Before any move the first hint is the authored nudge (never "Press Can't"). After that, dots hints name the next dot to place or take off ("Put a dot on the top edge.", "Take the dot off the edge under the cut."), heading for the grouping that needs the fewest changes, with hinted dots placed apart from the others. On a Can't puzzle the hints first fill four dots, then say "Two closed edges hold every dot, and the picture has three rings. Press Can't, tap each closed edge once, then press That's all." (with that puzzle's numbers), and level two marks each place to tap. Seams and pieces hints follow a shortest route to a solve: "Change the seam after strip 1 to R.", "Cut the lower line.", "Tape the lower line back.", "Punch the left hole." Level two marks the control and level three offers Apply hint. Every move can be taken back by another move, so there is no dead end. Hints alone finish every puzzle from a fresh start, after wrong moves, after a refused Can't and after Undo. Hints and searches are memoised; the slowest hint in the validator takes a few milliseconds of CPU time at most.
- **What the app avoids.** No "how many pieces?" or "how many edges?" questions: the child places, changes and cuts, and the board lights and tints. It never opens a cut band out (its length, its twists, B in thirds as two linked loops): the cut's surprise stays on paper.

## The playground

Three bands, chosen with small pictures: one strip with lines at a third and two thirds, a hole in each lane and a notch; a circle of three strips; and a circle of four. Change seams, cut and tape (one strip), punch and fill holes, light edges, and carry the arrow round (not on a cut band). There is no goal and no Hint, and the playground never counts as solved. These are the base packets' Problems 1 and 4 (tracing, cutting), grades 4–5 Problem 6 (the arrow) and the bonus Problems 1–3.

## Random taps

Parity is one bit, so a goal that only needs the right number of R seams would be won by half of all random seam choices. Seams avoids that, and the validator counts it (`scripts/validate-mobius.mjs` prints the numbers):

- **Seam puzzles.** Marks fixed on particular edges pin every seam, so exactly one seam choice of the eight solves each (12.5%). A random walk of as many taps as the fewest changes, each free seam equally likely, solves 22% of the time (2 of 9 on puzzle 6, 6 of 27 on puzzle 10). No single change solves either puzzle, and seam puzzles never ask for one closed edge alone (any one change from an even start would do it).
- **Pieces puzzles.** Of all reachable boards, 1 in 6 solves puzzle 7, 7 in 30 puzzle 8, 1 in 12 puzzle 11 and 2 in 48 puzzle 12; random walks of the fewest moves solve 17%, 19%, 8% and 4%.
- **Can't certificates.** Choosing how many places to tap (one to six, equally likely) and then that many random places passes the certificate 16.7% of the time on puzzle 2, 8.6% on puzzle 5 and 3.9% on puzzle 9 (8.9%, 1.9% and 8.6% for the "There is a way." certificate on puzzles 1, 3 and 4). Tapping places in page order passes only on puzzle 2, whose one closed edge any single tap certifies.
- **Dots.** Four random dots match the picture 43% of the time on puzzle 1, 11% on puzzle 3 and 40% on puzzle 4. These are the matching puzzles, where the lights do the work by design; the reasoning is in the Can't puzzles.

## Rules that keep the record honest

- The save is the dots in order, the refusal (`told`), the flags shown with "There is a way." (`flags`) and the claim's taps (`claimed`) for dots puzzles; the seams for seam puzzles; the seams, cuts and holes for pieces puzzles; the band, seams, cuts and holes for the playground. Everything shown, including lights from dots, tints, crossed rings and arrows, is derived from it. Lamps, the arrow button, an open Can't and its flags are view state, outside the save.
- Only legal moves are accepted: a dot on a place of this band, four at most; Can't with one to six different places, only with four dots that don't match and not after "There is a way." while the dots are the same; a seam change only on a free seam of an uncut band; a cut on one of the puzzle's lines, the whole cut at once (on B both heights); a hole on one of its places, within the allowance; nothing after a solve.
- Saved boards are checked: dots on places of the band, four at most, with no repeats; "Tap each closed edge once." only with four dots that don't match; "There is a way." only on a picture that can be made, with flags that are a whole certificate or none; a claim only on a picture that can't be made, with a whole certificate; flags only with "There is a way."; fixed seams as given; cuts on the puzzle's lines and, on one strip with an R seam, at both heights; holes on the puzzle's places within the allowance. A forged claim or a forged solve is refused.
- Undo takes back dots, seam changes, cuts and holes, never a refusal: "There is a way." stays while the dots are the same.
- `scripts/build-mobius.mjs` computes the answers with the mechanic's model (closed edges and pieces as cycles of the seam maps; breadth-first searches over seams, cuts and holes) and checks them against the table above. `scripts/validate-mobius.mjs` checks them again by another method: each strip refined into quarter-step square cells, glued across seams by explicit maps, with closed edges and pieces found by union-find on cell sides and corners, and orientability and Euler characteristic for each piece.

## Changes to the shared board

`dist/portal-board.js` gained one option, a seam that reverses, documented in [portal-board.md](../portal-board.md#a-seam-that-reverses) and tested in `tests/mobius.test.mjs`:

- `flip: {id: true}` on a surface spec glues square id's right side to the left side of `right[id]` turned over, its top end to the other's bottom end. Only right sides reverse. `validSurfaceSpec` checks it (an object of booleans on squares that exist; a true flag needs a right neighbour).
- `surfaceOf` adds `flip` (by side) and `flipped` to the surface. Corner classes, room edges (a turned side is always a seam, with `reversed: true`), seam marks (a quarter along the run and three quarters along its partner, both with `reversed: true`), `canonical` (the height turns over across the seam), `step` and `roomSpots` follow it.
- `lift`, `shoot` and `develop` (the unrolled view, lifts and straight shots) throw a `RangeError` on a surface with a reversed seam rather than draw plane copies that would be turned over.

Without `flip` every function returns what it did before: the test checks that surfaces without it have no `reversed` field and `flipped` false, with marks at the same place on both runs, and the portals, glued, shields and strands tests and browser suites pass unchanged. Seams uses the board for the room, its coordinates and taps (`surfaceOf`, `roomBoard` with no lattice points, `roomXY`, `wirePortal`); it hides the board's seam marks and draws the worksheet's • and □ instead, and computes closed edges and pieces itself.

## Not yet done

- **Children's play.** Nothing here has been tried by children, and the Week 38 worksheet is unpiloted. Three things to watch first:
  - whether children read the • and □ marks and the seam letter as how the ends join, and whether a light that goes through the R seam and comes back along the other side reads as "one edge" or as a mistake;
  - whether, on a Can't puzzle, children tap each closed edge once because of what the colours showed, or press Can't and tap once out of habit: puzzle 2's certificate is a single tap (random taps pass 1 time in 6, and a first tap anywhere passes), so its real test is puzzles 5 and 9;
  - whether doing the cut puzzles in the app before cutting paper takes away the paper cut's surprise, and whether children follow a cut on B running on at the other height and the tints of the pieces, or need a grown-up to.
- **The hole across the seam.** The bonus's band D (a hole on the seam) is left out: every hole place sits inside one strip.
- **One-edge seam goals.** Seam puzzles ask for two closed edges only. A one-edge goal is the parity bit alone, so one closed edge is met on B in the dots puzzles and through the playground's seams and arrow. The card's "carried arrow" is a picture the child can turn on, not a certificate the child taps.
- **Cuts on a circle of strips.** Only the one-strip band has lines and holes; the three- and four-strip circles have seams only.
- **The opened band.** The app never draws a cut band opened out, with its length, its twists or B in thirds as two linked loops; that stays on paper.
- **The board's reversing seam.** Only right sides reverse (enough for a Möbius band or a Klein bottle, not a projective plane), and the unrolled view, lifts and straight shots refuse a reversed surface.
- **Story.** Not on the Lantern Road; no keeper lines.
- **Grade levels.** Grade-free; not yet in a K–1, 2–3 or 4–5 trail.
- **Rehearsal.** Checked by tests, the validator and browser suites at phone and desktop widths only; not tried on a real phone or tablet, and not set beside the paper strips the worksheet uses.
