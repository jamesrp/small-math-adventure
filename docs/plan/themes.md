# The themes and the app

A first pass, October 4, 2026, for [the horizontal push](README.md). Three readers took the opening pages of each facilitator guide (its theorem-first overview) in [math-circle-worksheets](https://github.com/jamesrp/math-circle-worksheets/tree/main/lowell-math-circle-year-2), looked at student pages where needed, and compared each theme with the app's families. The ratings are a starting point; the review cards in Track C replace them. Each week's current decision, in the app or ruled out with a reason, is in [decisions.md](decisions.md).

The app ideas are not ports of the worksheets. Each asks what would be interesting to do with the theme's mathematical object on a screen. Children have enjoyed clicking around to solve puzzles far more than predicting an outcome (see Track B in the plan).

**Fit.** A: an existing mechanic covers it, or there is an obvious tap-or-drag solve with checkable answers. B: it needs a new mechanic, but the solve is clear and checkable. C: the substance lives in paper, folding, measuring, continuous geometry or open discussion. **Size** is a rough guess at the effort to port: S, M or L.

Totals: 24 A, 65 B and 12 C over the 101 rows. The first pass gave Weeks 1–63 18 A, 37 B and 8 C, and the encore a B; the cards give Weeks 64–82 4 A, 12 B and 3 C, and Weeks 83–100 2 A, 15 B and 1 C. Weeks 1, 2 and 15 have been taught; the rest are unpiloted.

## What the readers noticed

- **Shared engines.** A triangle grid (1, the encore, 16); a flip graph (1, 14, the flip-map proof); bead rings (33, 34); listing and sorting equally likely cases (24, 42, 43, 44, 45, 60, 63); a graph editor (13, 39, 52, 53, 62); the cups (43, 63).
- **Overlaps.** Weeks 7 and 8 are Nim with menus; 4 is Clockwork Gates; 18 is the design side of Signal Lanterns; 21 and 31 belong with Mirror Couriers; 29, 31 and 32 all land on gcd; 62 largely repeats Neighbor Lanterns; 52 and 53 both reach spanning trees.
- **Thin.** The Caesar wheel (4), 21, 36 at nine tiles, 45, 50 and 51 look thin for a family. 22, 34 and 42 suit a short set rather than twelve puzzles.
- **Two-sided certificates.** 13, 16, 17 and 19 each pair a construction with a checkable reason it can't be beaten, in the style of the existing star and paint proofs.
- **Counting found answers.** In "find every one" puzzles (3, 11, 12, 14, 19) knowing you have them all is the mathematics. A visible "3 of 5" removes it. Better: the child declares "done" and the app checks the claim.
- **Guessing.** Where the app does the arithmetic (49, 55, 60), the solve must be a reverse or design goal, with the anti-guessing limits in [insight-without-ai.md](../insight-without-ai.md).
- **Where the app beats paper.** Instant counterexamples (17, 23), repeated shuffles that light up loops (3), averaging that settles (20), rhombi that turn into cubes (1), infinite strips and planes (35, 41), live margins (25) and a wobbling frame instead of a physical kit (52).
